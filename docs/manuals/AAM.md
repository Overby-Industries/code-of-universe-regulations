# AAM — Animal Advocate Manual

*What the Code requires of you if an animal depends on you, or if you speak
for one, in plain language.*

- **Version:** 1.0.0-Draft
- **Covers:** CUR-A.7
- **Status:** Draft

---

## Before you read this

**This manual has no authority of its own.** Every statement in it points at a
provision of the Code, and where the two differ the Code governs and this
manual is wrong.

This manual is not written from the animal's side, because an animal cannot
read it. It is written for two roles the Code recognises: a being who holds an
animal in their care, and a being who serves — or might serve — as an animal's
advocate in a Commonwealth proceeding. If you are neither, §3 still tells you
what protects any animal you interact with, regardless of your role.

CUR-A currently consists of one drafted Part, CUR-A.7, covering cognitive
harm, standing, and representation. Other Articles of the RFAL Animal Kingdom
Bill of Rights are not yet given their own Parts; where this manual is silent,
that silence reflects the corpus's own current state, not an absence of
protection under RFAL.

## 1. If an animal is in your care

"Human Care" means any situation where an animal's access to food, water,
shelter, space, social contact, or veterinary attention depends on what you do
or don't do. *(CUR-A.7 §7.1(b)(4))*

**Food, water, shelter, veterinary care, and sanitation for an animal in your
care are Vital Continuity Services.** You may never withhold, reduce, delay,
or condition them — not as a sanction, not as a training method, not as
leverage in a dispute, and not during any investigation or proceeding, against
you or anyone else. *(CUR-A.7 §7.9(a)–(b))* Using deprivation of food or water
to train, control, or research an animal is prohibited absolutely; no
scientific, commercial, or husbandry purpose makes it permissible.
*(CUR-A.7 §7.9(e))* If you genuinely can no longer provide for an animal, your
obligation is to transfer it to someone who can — never to withhold.
*(CUR-A.7 §7.9(f))*

**You owe the animal in your care an environment that avoids cognitive harm** —
injury to its mental or experiential state, including chronic fear, boredom,
loneliness, learned helplessness, and the frustration of behaviour typical to
its species. This is assessed from the animal's position, by what its species
needs, not by what a human observer would find distressing. *(CUR-A.7
§7.1(b)(2), §7.1(c))* Concretely, that means avoiding:

- Isolating a social species;
- Confining an animal in a way that prevents species-typical movement,
  foraging, or exploration;
- Conditions producing chronic fear, boredom, or learned helplessness;
- Unpredictable aversive conditions the animal has no control over; and
- An absence of enrichment appropriate to the species.

*(CUR-A.7 §7.3(a))*

**A physically healthy animal can still be cognitively harmed**, and its
health is not evidence that no harm occurred. *(CUR-A.7 §7.3(d))* If the
species is highly social and cognitively complex — great apes, cetaceans,
elephants, corvids, parrots, and comparable species — solitary confinement is
prohibited; where separation is genuinely necessary for welfare, it has to be
the minimum duration necessary, monitored, and reviewed at least every seven
days. *(CUR-A.7 §7.3(b))* For social species generally, you're expected to
provide social housing unless a documented welfare assessment says otherwise,
plus an environment complex enough for species-typical behaviour and
appropriate enrichment. *(CUR-A.7 §7.3(c))*

**Young animals develop under conditions meeting their developmental needs** —
maternal care, peer socialisation, play, gradual weaning. Separating them from
their mothers early for human convenience is prohibited. *(CUR-A.7 §7.1(e))*

**None of this is suspended by an emergency.** No characterisation of a
condition as an outbreak, infestation, or crisis creates authority to do what
this section forbids, and none of it narrows the obligations above — including
during a genuine disease outbreak, which is handled as a fault condition:
contained by the least harmful available means, published, reviewed.
*(CUR-A.7 §7.11(b), §7.11(e))* Culling is not treated as an emergency measure.
Where population management is genuinely necessary, habitat expansion has to
be preferred over it, and whoever proposes a lethal measure carries the burden
of showing no non-lethal alternative exists. *(CUR-A.7 §7.11(c))*

## 2. If you're considering serving as an advocate

Where an animal's interests are affected by a proceeding, it has standing
regardless of whether it can appear, communicate, or understand what's
happening — and the proceeding doesn't go forward on the merits until an
advocate is appointed to represent it. *(CUR-A.7 §7.6(a), §7.6(c))*

**To be eligible, you need demonstrated expertise** in animal welfare,
ethology, and the specific needs of the species in question.
*(CUR-A.7 §7.7(b))*

**You are disqualified — not merely disfavoured — if:**

1. You're employed by, contracted to, funded by, or otherwise dependent on any
   party whose conduct is in question in the proceeding;
2. You hold a commercial, research, or operational interest in the animal or
   the outcome; or
3. You'd be representing both the animal and a party whose interests are
   adverse to it.

*(CUR-A.7 §7.7(c))* These are disqualifications, not factors weighed against
your other qualifications. An appointment made in breach of them is void from
the start, and any determination reached with a disqualified advocate can be
voided on that ground alone. *(CUR-A.7 §7.7(d), §7.6(e))*

**Your duty runs to the animal's interests as the animal would have them** —
assessed by species-typical needs and by that individual animal's observable
preferences, condition, and history. It is not a duty to whoever appointed
you, to the party holding the animal, or to any wider interest, and it doesn't
bend if the party holding the animal disagrees with your assessment.
*(CUR-A.7 §7.7(e)–(f))*

**You're entitled to what you need to do the job**: access to the animal, to
the conditions it's held in, and to records concerning it, sufficient to form
your own independent assessment. Denying you that access is a fault in its own
right and grounds for the proceeding to infer against whoever denied it.
*(CUR-A.7 §7.7(g))*

**What you cannot do, even with good intentions:**

- Exercise any authority over the animal, own it, or dispose of it — your
  appointment confers none of that. *(CUR-A.7 §7.8(a))*
- Consent on the animal's behalf to anything the Code requires the animal's
  *own* consent for — including telepathic access, non-therapeutic
  modification, or any procedure whose justification depends on consent
  rather than welfare. *(CUR-A.7 §7.8(c))* Where a matter turns on consent the
  animal can't give, it's decided on welfare and interests instead — your
  agreement is never treated as a substitute. *(CUR-A.7 §7.8(d))*
- Waive a protection this Part gives the animal, agree to lowering its tier,
  or accept a measure §7.9 forbids. *(CUR-A.7 §7.8(e))*

Acting in breach of any of this can get you removed on any being's
application, and voids what was decided while you held the role.
*(CUR-A.7 §7.8(f))*

**Your appointment, identity, expertise, and any interest you declared are
recorded and published**, so the record is checkable. *(CUR-A.7 §7.7(h))*

## 3. What protects the animal regardless of your role

**Where an animal's tier is unclear, it's treated as Tier 2 until an
independent assessment says otherwise** — and self-assessment by a party with
a commercial, research, or operational interest doesn't count as independent.
*(CUR-A.7 §7.2(c)–(d))* Uncertainty resolves toward protection, not away from
it. *(CUR-A.7 §7.2(e))*

**Non-consensual access to an animal's cognition is prohibited to the same
extent as for any other conscious being.** An animal cannot give the consent
the Code requires — explicit, informed, ongoing, revocable — so no telepathic
access to an animal is consented access, and an advocate cannot consent on its
behalf either. *(CUR-A.7 §7.5(a)–(c))* Conditioning designed to induce learned
helplessness or otherwise non-consensually modify an animal's cognition is
forbidden outright. *(CUR-A.7 §7.5(d))*

**An animal isn't required to have resisted, suffered visibly, or otherwise
"earned" protection for a harm to be found.** A course of conduct is judged as
a whole; nobody defeats a claim by breaking it into individually tolerable
pieces, and intent isn't required — though it, along with malice or
recklessness, makes things worse rather than better. *(CUR-A.7 §7.4(c)–(d))*

**An animal or species can't be misrepresented to gain an advantage.**
Describing a species as unfeeling automata, or as human-like in ways it isn't,
in order to facilitate exploitation or reduce moral consideration, is a fault
against the forum. *(CUR-A.7 §7.10(a)–(c))*

## 4. If something needs to be reported or reviewed

Every tier determination, advocate appointment, finding of cognitive harm, and
measure taken is logged as a matter of record, including who was affected,
the tier assessed, the advocate appointed and any interest they declared, and
the outcome. *(CUR-A.7 §7.12(a)–(b))* A determination that isn't recorded
didn't lawfully happen. *(CUR-A.7 §7.12(d))*

**Conditions where animals are held in human care can be audited** — triggered
by an advocate, by a report from any being, or by routine and random
scheduling that doesn't wait for a complaint. *(CUR-A.7 §7.12(c))*
