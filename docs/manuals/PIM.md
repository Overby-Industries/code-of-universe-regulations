# PIM — Parent Information Manual

*What the Code says about raising a child, and what it says a child already
holds regardless of who is raising them.*

- **Version:** 1.0.0-Draft
- **Covers:** CUR-H.1, CUR-H.2, CUR-H.3, CUR-H.4, CUR-H.5, CUR-H.6 §6.5,
  CUR-D.6, and the parts of the Foundations that reach every being without
  qualification
- **Status:** Draft

---

## Before you read this

**This manual has no authority of its own.** Every statement in it points at a
provision of the Code, and where the two differ the Code governs and this
manual is wrong. Read [HIM](HIM.md) first if you have not — everything in it
applies to a child in exactly the same way it applies to you, and this manual
does not repeat it.

**This manual is honest about what does not exist yet.** There is no dedicated
CUR title on custody, guardianship, or education. Section 8 says so plainly.
What follows is built only from provisions that already exist, and it does not
paper over the gap by implying a settled answer where the corpus has not given
one.

---

## 1. The one fact that governs everything below

**A child is a sentient being, and the Code does not have a smaller version of
what that means.**

There is no separate classification for a child anywhere in how the Code is
built. The same protection that keeps an adult from being suspended,
blacklisted, or stripped of standing applies to a child with no special
handling required to make it apply — because nothing checks age before
applying it. *(CUR-H.5 §5.1(c): "Age, diagnosis, disability, cognitive
condition... are not conditions on the rights stated here and are not grounds
for their reduction.")*

So before anything else: **everything in HIM belongs to your child already, in
full, right now.** Being young does not discount it, and nothing below reduces
it. What follows adds absolute protections specific to childhood, and states
what raising a child does and does not give you authority to do.

---

## 2. What raising a child does not require you to hold

You do not hold your child's body, mind, or consent. You hold responsibility
for them — which is a different thing, and the Code is precise about where the
line falls.

**You cannot consent on your child's behalf to something done to their body.**
Not a parent, not a guardian, not a family member, not a court. None of them
holds that authority, because a child's body is not within any other party's
authority to dispose of. *(CUR-H.6 §6.5(c), CUR-H.5 §5.2(g))*

**A diagnosis, a finding of distress, or a professional's concern about your
child gives nobody — including you — authority over them.** It does not
authorise treatment, medication, detention, confinement, restraint,
surveillance, guardianship, or any modification of their mind, without their
own consent. *(CUR-H.4 §4.4(a))* This is stated for every being without
exception; nothing in the Code carves out a parent's authority to override it,
and a manual that implied otherwise would be inventing a right the Code does
not grant.

**Your child determines their own identity.** No diagnosis, correction, or
external validation is required, and nobody — including you — may treat their
gender, sexuality, or identity as something to be fixed. *(CUR-H.5 §5.4)*

## 3. What raising a child does let you do

**Raise them within a tradition.** Teaching your child your beliefs, your
faith, your values, and your practices is protected. The Code does not judge
theology and does not prevent a parent from raising a child within it. *(CUR-D.6
§6.9(a))*

**Make ordinary decisions of care.** Health, education, daily life, and the
countless decisions that come with responsibility for someone who cannot yet
make every decision themselves — that is what guardianship is, and it is
distinct from the authority the Code withholds in section 2. Where education
specifically is concerned, CUR-H.3 states the floor: your choice of what to
teach and emphasise is yours, but not the power to withhold the evidence-based
account of a matter or replace it with doctrine presented as settled fact.
*(CUR-H.3 §3.2(c), §3.4)*

The line between the two is not always obvious in the moment, so the next
sections state where the Code has drawn it explicitly.

---

## 4. Where teaching becomes coercion

The Code protects raising a child in a faith. It does not protect using that
relationship as a lever.

**Fear, guilt, and exploitation of dependency are named directly, with a
child's vulnerability called out specifically.** Manipulation through fear,
guilt, shame, or exploitation of childhood is listed as coercive worship
alongside targeting of the grieving, the ill, and the isolated. *(CUR-D.6
§6.3(a)(3), §6.3(a)(10))*

**A threat that reaches beyond disagreement into consequence is coercion,
whatever the subject.** Making love, shelter, standing in the family, or
continued relationship conditional on belief is not teaching — it is the
mechanism CUR-H.2 and CUR-D.6 exist to name and refuse.

**None of this stops you disagreeing, correcting, or setting expectations.**
Ordinary parenting — rules, consequences, expectations, discipline that does not
cross into what section 5 forecloses — is not what these provisions reach.
What they reach is fear and dependency used as the *mechanism* of belief, not
disagreement about behaviour.

## 5. Absolute protections — no exception exists, for any reason

These do not depend on your judgment as a parent, your community's practice, or
your child's apparent willingness. There is no process by which any of them
becomes available, to anyone.

**No child can consent to sexual contact with an adult.** Not as a legal
presumption to be argued — there is no capacity for the consent standard to
attach to. *(CUR-H.6 §6.5(a))*

**A child cannot be married, to anyone, under any authority.** No parental
consent, no religious rite, no community custom, no court order, and no
marriage performed elsewhere makes it valid here. Pregnancy is evidence of an
offence, not a reason to formalise one. *(CUR-H.6 §6.5(d))* **A marriage
certificate does not change what the arrangement is** — the Code treats it as
what it is in substance, whatever paperwork accompanies it. *(CUR-H.6 §6.5(e))*

**The age at which this applies is one Commonwealth-wide standard.** It does
not vary by community, tradition, or accommodation — a rule permitting one
group's children to marry younger than another's is void on the same grounds
that forbid treating different species differently. *(CUR-H.6 §6.5(h))*

**Anyone who arranges, facilitates, or profits from either of these is
accountable — independently of whoever the arrangement was made with, and
independently of whether anything happened as a result.** *(CUR-H.6 §6.5(f))*

## 6. Your child's right to leave — later, and now

**When your child becomes an adult, walking away from what you raised them in
costs them nothing.** No guilt, no shame, no loss of family connection is a
lawful consequence of an adult child revising or rejecting what they were
taught as a child. The beliefs formed under your authority do not bind the
adult they become. *(CUR-H.2 §2.3(c), and the wider right to leave any
community "without penalty, coercion, or loss of any right")*

**Expulsion from the family as a response to questioning or leaving is named
as a weapon, specifically.** Removal from family imposed as punishment for
revising or leaving a tradition is listed alongside excommunication and social
punishment as coercion the Code refuses, whoever imposes it. *(CUR-H.2
§2.3(b)(3))*

**This does not reach while they are a minor in your care in the way it will
once they are an adult** — see section 8. What it means now is that the
relationship you are building does not get to end in a demand: believe this or
lose your family. That demand is what section 4 already forecloses.

## 7. If something happens to your child

Everything in [HIM §4](HIM.md#4-if-something-happens-to-you) applies without
change. In particular:

**Every report is investigated — including one you make on your child's
behalf, and one made by someone else about your child.** Not the credible
ones. Every one. *(CUR-H.6 §6.8(a))*

**Nothing that follows attaches to your child.** No record, no consequence, no
loss of standing arising from having been harmed. *(CUR-H.7 §7.6(e), applied to
a child under §6.5(g))*

**Your child's identity is not published**, whatever institution is found
responsible. *(CUR-X.5 §5.5)*

**A child removed from a marriage or an exploitative arrangement retains every
entitlement in full, and the Commonwealth assumes their continuity without
condition, without means assessment, and without reference to the family that
arranged it.** This is stated because removal has historically been treated as
a punishment in itself, leaving a child with nothing. The Code does not permit
that reading. *(CUR-H.6 §6.5(g))*

## 8. What this manual cannot yet tell you

The Code does not yet have a dedicated title on custody, guardianship
standards, or education. This manual has drawn on what exists — CUR-H.5's
bodily autonomy, CUR-H.6 §6.5's absolute child protections, CUR-D.6's limits on
coercive worship, CUR-H.2's freedom of conscience — but none of those Parts was
written primarily to answer the questions that come up most in actually raising
a child:

- What standard governs a custody dispute between parents
- What a guardian's authority looks like where it is not addressed above
- What a school or an education system owes a child, and what it may
  require — **narrowed by CUR-H.3**, which states what may not be withheld
  or replaced with doctrine, but does not state what a school owes a child
  beyond content
- How authority transfers, is shared, or is withdrawn
- What happens where a parent's own rights and a child's protection appear to
  conflict, beyond what section 4 already resolves

**This manual does not answer these because the Code does not yet answer
them.** Writing an answer here that the Code has not actually given would
create a second, unreliable source of law — precisely what [HIM's opening
promise](HIM.md#before-you-read-this) refuses to do. When a title covering
these is drafted, this manual will be revised to cite it, and not before.
