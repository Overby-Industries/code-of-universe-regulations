# EHM — Ecosystem and Habitat Manual

*What the Code requires of you if you operate in, extract from, or are
responsible for an environment, in plain language.*

- **Version:** 1.0.0-Draft
- **Covers:** CUR-E.1, CUR-E.2, CUR-E.7
- **Status:** Draft

---

## Before you read this

**This manual has no authority of its own.** Every statement in it points at a
provision of the Code, and where the two differ the Code governs and this
manual is wrong.

This manual is written primarily for an operator: anyone conducting
extraction, construction, propulsion, or decommissioning; anyone responsible
for a habitat; anyone whose activity touches Earth or an extraterrestrial
environment. §4 is written for anyone considering serving as an environmental
advocate. If you're neither, §5 tells you what environmental protection may
never be used to do to you — which matters regardless of your role, because
that limit is what keeps this whole domain from becoming a route to authority
over beings.

CUR-E currently consists of three drafted Parts: CUR-E.1 (definitions,
standing, and representation), CUR-E.2 (habitats, Earth, and extraterrestrial
environments), and CUR-E.7 (orbital debris). Other Articles are not yet given
their own Parts; where this manual is silent, that reflects the corpus's
current state.

## 1. Before you extract or operate

**You need a debris budget declared before you start, not after.** An
authorisation that hasn't declared one has no debris allowance — an
undeclared budget is never read as unlimited. A declared budget of zero
permits operations that generate no debris; no declared budget permits no
operations at all. *(CUR-E.7 §7.1(a)–(d))*

**Material you extract, process, or displace has to be recovered, reused, or
kept under controlled custody.** What isn't is waste, and waste isn't a
permitted output — it's a measured deficiency against your declared budget,
reported as such. Deciding recovery wasn't economically attractive doesn't
change that. *(CUR-E.7 §7.2(a)–(c))* You're excused only where recovery is
physically impossible or would itself create a greater environmental burden —
and even then, you have to document and publish why. *(CUR-E.7 §7.2(d))*

**Before commencing, you also need:**

1. An ecological standing classification for the environment affected;
2. Documentation of the alternatives you considered and why you chose this
   operation; and
3. Provision for restoring or stabilising the site once you're done.

*(CUR-E.2 §2.5(b))*

**Ecological standing is classified ES-0 through ES-3** — how fast the system
could be replaced, from a growing season up to never. Where it's unknown,
disputed, or unassessed, it's treated as ES-2 until an independent assessment
says otherwise, and the burden of establishing a lower classification is on
you, never on the system. Self-assessment by a party with a commercial,
developmental, or operational interest — meaning you — doesn't count as
independent. *(CUR-E.1 §1.4(a)–(c), (f))* **Extraction from an ES-3
environment is prohibited outright**, whatever the material is worth.
*(CUR-E.2 §2.5(c))*

**If you're operating somewhere life hasn't been detected, that isn't
evidence nobody's there.** Before any operation that would materially alter
an extraterrestrial environment, you need an assessment of whether life is
present, whether conditions supporting it are present, and whether the
environment holds scientific, cultural, or ecological value your operation
would destroy. Where you can't exclude the presence of life, the environment
is classified ES-2 or higher and the burden is on you. *(CUR-E.2 §2.4(a)–(c))*
Environments of exceptional value, and ones that are unique or unreplicated,
are ES-3 regardless of how remote, uninhabited, or economically attractive
they are. *(CUR-E.2 §2.4(e))*

**Where a credible risk of irreversible harm can't be excluded, the operation
does not proceed.** This is a stopping rule, not something weighed against the
operation's benefits — and it binds the Commonwealth's own operations exactly
as it binds yours. *(CUR-E.2 §2.6(c)–(d))*

**Extraction is an enterprise, and CUR-X.4 applies to you in full** — its
transparency requirements and its accountability measures, on top of
everything here. *(CUR-E.2 §2.5(e))* An extraction authorisation gives you
the right to extract and process — not to own the resource itself, which
remains common heritage. *(CUR-E.2 §2.5(a))*

## 2. If you're responsible for a habitat

**Your habitat's atmosphere, water cycle, thermal regulation, waste
processing, food production, and radiation shielding are Vital Continuity
Services.** In a closed volume there's no outside to absorb an error, so
margin isn't an engineering nicety — it's a constitutional obligation.
*(CUR-E.2 §2.2(a)–(b))*

You have to maintain:

1. Reserve capacity in every life-supporting system, enough to sustain
   everyone aboard through the longest credible restoration interval;
2. Redundancy so a single failure doesn't interrupt a Vital Continuity
   Service;
3. Continuous, published monitoring of life-supporting parameters; and
4. A documented, actually-tested restoration procedure for each system.

*(CUR-E.2 §2.2(c))*

**Your reserve calculation covers everyone present** — including visitors,
beings in transit, and Silicon-Based Life whose power and computational
continuity depend on your habitat. Nobody is excluded because of their
status, membership, role, or how long they're expected to stay.
*(CUR-E.2 §2.2(d))* This is enforced, not just stated: a docking is refused
where the declared reserve doesn't clear the floor a new arrival would
require. A habitat that has only counted its own complement has not declared
a floor covering an arrival.

**No being aboard may ever be denied access to a life-supporting system, for
any reason, in any compliance state, during any proceeding.** The closed
nature of a habitat makes exclusion from its atmosphere indistinguishable
from a sentence, and this admits no exception. *(CUR-E.2 §2.2(f))*

**Your habitat design has to meet the species-typical needs of every lifeform
you intend to have present** — including animals and plant systems. Meeting
only human requirements isn't compliance. *(CUR-E.2 §2.2(g))*

**A habitat failure is a fault, handled through the fault-response protocol —
not an emergency, and it creates no additional authority.** The offending
process is rejected, the domain reverts to its last known safe state,
Protected Mode is entered, and a complete report is published within 24
hours. Every right and every Vital Continuity Service is preserved throughout.
*(CUR-E.2 §2.9(c)–(d))* If you've met your reserve and redundancy
obligations, your crew never actually faces a choice between rights and
survival — and if that choice appears to arise, the fault happened earlier,
at the point the margin wasn't provided. *(CUR-E.2 §2.9(e))*

## 3. Reporting on the orbital environment

**Measure debris in both tracked object count and aggregate mass, reported
separately** — a single large uncontrolled mass and a cloud of small
fragments are different risks, and neither measure should be able to hide the
other. *(CUR-E.7 §7.4(a))* Report at the intervals your authorisation sets,
and in every case on completing an extraction campaign, on any unplanned
disassembly or collision, and on decommissioning. *(CUR-E.7 §7.4(b))* Failing
to report is a fault; misreporting is worse; misreporting that conceals a
risk to a Vital Continuity Service is worse still. *(CUR-E.7 §7.4(d))*

**Recovered material only counts toward your Commons Reserve contribution
once it's actually under your custody** — material transferred while
uncontrolled doesn't count, and structuring a transfer to make it look like it
does is void. *(CUR-E.7 §7.5)*

**Everything here is published, and none of it may be classified above public
on commercial grounds.** Commercial sensitivity isn't a constitutional
justification for secrecy. *(CUR-E.7 §7.6)* Any being may petition for an
audit of your declared budget or reported measurements.
*(CUR-E.7 §7.6(c))*

**If you exceed your declared budget, your authorisation is in violation** —
not you personally, unless you're the one holding it as a license rather than
as a being. Available responses include restitution, recovery obligations,
contract restriction, suspension, and — after due process and exhausted
appeals, for repeated violation — withdrawal. *(CUR-E.7 §7.7(a)–(b))* **No
sanction may withhold a Vital Continuity Service** from anyone affected,
including people employed by, resident within, or dependent on your
operation — and where withdrawing your authorisation would leave them
without one, the Commonwealth assumes continuity before the withdrawal takes
effect. *(CUR-E.7 §7.7(c)–(d))*

## 4. If you're considering serving as an environmental advocate

Where an environmental interest has standing — including the interest of
future beings who will depend on a system, which counts now — an advocate has
to be appointed before a proceeding materially affecting an ES-2 or ES-3
system continues on the merits. *(CUR-E.1 §1.5(c), (f))*

**To be eligible, you need demonstrated expertise in the ecology of the
system in question.** Where the system is subject to a stewardship
relationship, you're appointed in consultation with the people who hold it —
this isn't a courtesy, and an appointment made over their heads is void, not
merely irregular. *(CUR-E.1 §1.6(b))*

**You are disqualified — not merely disfavoured — if:**

1. You're employed by, contracted to, funded by, or otherwise dependent on
   any party whose conduct or proposed activity is in question;
2. You hold a commercial, developmental, or operational interest in the
   outcome; or
3. You'd be representing both an environmental interest and a party adverse
   to it.

*(CUR-E.1 §1.6(c))* These disqualify rather than weigh against you. An
appointment made in breach is void, and a determination reached with it can
be voided on that ground alone. *(CUR-E.1 §1.6(d))*

**Your duty runs to the condition, integrity, and continuity of the system,
on the best available evidence.** Not to the appointing body, not to any
party proposing activity, not to any wider economic interest.
*(CUR-E.1 §1.6(e))*

**You're entitled to access** — to the system, to monitoring data, to
documentation of any proposed activity — sufficient to form your own
independent assessment. Being denied it is a fault and grounds to infer
against whoever denied it. *(CUR-E.1 §1.6(f))*

## 5. What environmental protection may never be used to do

This is the limit that makes everything above safe to state as strongly as it
is stated. It reaches beings, not activities — measures under this domain
attach to operations, authorisations, and enterprises, never to beings.
*(CUR-E.1 §1.7(a), §2.8(c))*

No environmental interest, however assessed, may ever be advanced to:

- Suspend, reduce, or condition any right you hold;
- Withhold, delay, degrade, or condition a Vital Continuity Service for
  anyone;
- Justify reducing anyone's constitutional standing or capacity to
  participate in governance;
- Justify removing, displacing, or excluding a people from land they hold a
  stewardship relationship with; or
- Justify a measure directed at a population or a category of beings, rather
  than at an activity or an authorisation.

*(CUR-E.1 §1.7(b))*

**This list is stated this specifically because each item has been used
before.** Conservation has historically been a vehicle for displacing exactly
the peoples it claims to protect land for, and a habitat's finite capacity has
made "someone here is surplus" a locally plausible argument. Neither is
available under this Code. Capacity is addressed by expanding capacity and by
not exceeding what a habitat can actually support — never by deciding a being
already present doesn't belong. *(CUR-E.1 §1.7(d), (g); CUR-E.2 §2.8(d)–(e))*

**Where an environmental interest and a being's Vital Continuity genuinely
conflict, continuity governs first** and the environmental question is
resolved afterward — the Commonwealth assumes continuity in the interim.
*(CUR-E.1 §1.7(e))*

**No emergency changes any of this.** No characterisation of a condition as a
crisis, tipping point, or point of no return creates authority that didn't
already exist, or narrows anything above — even though environmental
conditions supply the most credible-sounding argument for exactly that.
*(CUR-E.1 §1.9(b)–(c))* An argument for suspending rights is not improved by
being made about something true.
