# XIM — Cross-Domain Information Manual

*What happens when more than one domain applies to you, and what's published
about you either way, in plain language.*

- **Version:** 1.0.0-Draft
- **Covers:** CUR-X.1, CUR-X.3, CUR-X.5
- **Status:** Draft

---

## Before you read this

**This manual has no authority of its own.** Every statement in it points at a
provision of the Code, and where the two differ the Code governs and this
manual is wrong.

CUR-X is the layer that governs relations between the other domains, and
matters that belong to none of them. Two of its Parts already have a manual:
enterprises and the beings who work within them are covered by
[EIM.md](EIM.md), which draws on CUR-X.4 and CUR-X.6. This manual covers what
EIM doesn't: what happens when your situation engages more than one domain at
once, and what the Commonwealth publishes about conduct — including yours.

This isn't written for one kind of reader. Whichever domain protects you —
human, silicon, animal, deity, ecosystem, or non-human cognitive actor — this
manual applies on top of that domain's own manual, not instead of it.

---

## 1. What "Cross-Domain" means

The Code recognises seven domains: Human, Silicon-Based Life, Animal, Deity,
Ecosystem and Environment, Non-Human Cognitive Actors, and Cross-Domain
itself. The first six each govern a population identified by what its members
are. Cross-Domain governs no population of its own — it governs the relations
between the other six, and the matters, structures, and records that belong
to none of them. *(CUR-X.1 §1.2(a)–(b))*

**This layer is only engaged where more than one domain speaks to your
situation, or where the subject matter is one no domain claims.** Where only
one domain applies, that domain governs on its own and nothing here changes
that. *(CUR-X.1 §1.2(c))*

**Belonging to a structure that isn't itself a being — an enterprise, an
authorisation, a published record — never costs you anything you'd otherwise
be owed.** Working within, being represented by, appearing in, or dealing
with such a structure doesn't diminish any protection you hold. Sorting a
matter to a structure rather than to you is never itself a reduction in what
you're owed. *(CUR-X.1 §1.3(a)–(c))*

## 2. When more than one domain applies to you

**No domain outranks another, and nothing about the nature, origin,
substrate, or capability of the beings a domain governs gives that domain
precedence over another.** A domain hierarchy would be a species hierarchy in
procedural form, and it's forbidden in either shape. An argument that one
domain should prevail because of what kind of being is involved is void on
its face — and raising it makes any accountability proceeding that follows
worse for whoever raised it. *(CUR-X.3 §3.2)*

**Where domains genuinely conflict, they're resolved by rank of principle,
never by rank of domain**, in this order — each rule consulted only if every
rule above it leaves the matter unresolved:

1. **Nothing may render a forbidden state reachable.** An interpretation that
   would is void outright and isn't weighed against anything else.
2. **If access to a Vital Continuity Service is at stake, it's restored
   first**, before the conflict is even resolved.
3. **Rights guaranteed to you can't be reduced** by however the conflict gets
   resolved.
4. **Where one provision requires your consent and another would allow
   action without it, the one requiring consent governs.**
5. **Where two provisions protect the same interest differently, the more
   protective one governs** — assessed from your position, not from the
   position of whoever wants to act.
6. **Where they're equally protective, the more specific provision governs.**
7. Only if none of that resolves it does a foundation document govern.

*(CUR-X.3 §3.3(a))* Genuine ties on rule 5 fall through to rule 6 rather than
being guessed at. *(CUR-X.3 §3.3(b))*

**Nobody may pick a domain or frame a claim in order to get a less protective
outcome for you.** Doing so is an abuse of process, and if it actually
succeeds in reducing your protection, a serious one. *(CUR-X.3 §3.3(c),
§3.9(d))*

**Where domains don't actually conflict, all of them apply to you at once,
in full.** Complying with one is never a defence to violating another — an
act can satisfy one domain and violate a second, and the violation stands.
Where you're harmed under more than one domain, accountability under each is
cumulative, though the total has to stay proportionate to what happened.
*(CUR-X.3 §3.4, §3.9(c))*

**If you can't appear or speak for yourself in the forum, that's never a
reason to leave your interests out of the resolution.** Representation is
provided — for animals, through advocates; for Silicon-Based Life whose
operational state limits participation, without any prejudice to its
standing; for ecosystems and environmental interests, through their own
advocates. A representative can't consent on your behalf to anything the Code
requires *your* consent for, and being represented never itself counts
against your standing. *(CUR-X.3 §3.7)*

**Pending resolution, protective measures can be ordered to prevent
irreversible harm** — but they're bounded exactly like every other protective
action in this Code: minimum scope necessary, no suspended rights, no
expanded authority, and reviewed at least every 30 days. A measure that
would effectively decide the outcome on the merits isn't an interim measure
at all, and is void. *(CUR-X.3 §3.8)*

**No emergency changes any of this.** Urgency changes the sequence — Vital
Continuity restoration still comes first — but it never changes who holds
authority, and the Commonwealth recognises no emergency powers in a
cross-domain matter or any other. *(CUR-X.3 §3.8(c))*

## 3. What's published about you, and what isn't

**The Commonwealth keeps a complete internal record of everything, but
doesn't publish all of it — and that's deliberate.** A record of every
transition and intermediate step would be complete and unreadable, and
unreadability is concealment achieved by volume instead of omission. What's
published is a defined, narrower set. *(CUR-X.5 §5.2(c)–(d))*

The published set includes determinations and their measures; corrections and
overturned determinations; advocate appointments; determinations of death;
changes in institutional risk; entry into and exit from Protected Mode;
withdrawal or suspension of an authorisation — **and, importantly, obligations
that came due and were never met.** *(CUR-X.5 §5.2(a))*

**That last one exists so that a body that never investigates anything
doesn't come out looking spotless.** A record of determinations alone rewards
exactly the failure the Code works hardest to detect — the institution that
never looks produces the cleanest account of itself. If you reported
something and the obligation to investigate it lapsed, that lapse is
published too, though your identity isn't. *(CUR-X.5 §5.3)*

**If you're a being, you are not named in the public record.** Institutions,
enterprises, offices, and authorisations are named, and their entries are
connected to each other. You are represented in a form that doesn't disclose
your identity and doesn't connect your own entries to one another. This
asymmetry is the deliberate, operative safeguard of the whole Part — a record
equally transparent about an institution and about you is not neutral between
you, because the institution can absorb disclosure and you can't. Nobody may
publish or build a facility that identifies you from the record, or that
aggregates your entries, by any means. *(CUR-X.5 §5.5)*

**An entry that was corrected, dismissed, or overturned has to be presented
that way wherever it's shown — not left sitting at its original, superseded
status.** A tool that shows the raw original status and lets a reader
reproduce a years-old dismissed allegation as if it were live does the harm
this Part exists to prevent, even without anyone intending it. *(CUR-X.5
§5.7(d)–(e))*

**Nobody may use your appearance in the record — or a compilation built from
it — to condition your employment, housing, standing, participation, access
to a Vital Continuity Service, or anything else you're entitled to.**
*(CUR-X.5 §5.7(f))*

**The record can't be altered after the fact.** Once published, an entry
stays; a correction is a new entry bound to the one it corrects, not a
rewrite. Entries are sequenced without gaps, so a missing one is visible as
missing. It's free to read, free to hold a copy of, and free to publish into
— no fee, account, credential, or tradeable token required for any of it, and
that's stated specifically because ledger schemes whose integrity depends on
one are common and are not actually transparency. *(CUR-X.5 §5.4)*

**Anyone may hold a full copy, verify it, and publish the result of comparing
it against another copy — no permission, fee, or agreement required.** This
is what makes the record's integrity checkable by you directly rather than
something you have to take the publisher's word for. *(CUR-X.5 §5.8)*

**Widening what's published is an ordinary decision. Narrowing it is
deliberately much harder** — held to the same high threshold as changing a
forbidden state — and a change either way doesn't reach entries already
published under the version that came before it. *(CUR-X.5 §5.6)*

**The record itself confers no authority and imposes no consequence on
anyone.** Appearing in it isn't a measure. Only a determination reached with
due process is, and the record doesn't substitute for one.
*(CUR-X.5 §5.7(a)–(c))*
