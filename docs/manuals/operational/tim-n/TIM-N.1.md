1. Purpose
This protocol establishes a safe, trauma-informed, low-barrier process for any being to report an incident of suspected telepathic invasion, whether as a target, a witness, or a being who fears they may have unintentionally invaded another.

Core Principles:

Belief is not required — but serious, respectful attention is.

Proof is not required — documentation is for pattern detection, not conviction.

Good faith is presumed — reports made without malice shall not be punished, even if later determined to be mistaken.

Confidentiality is default — no report shall be shared without explicit consent of the reporting being.

2. Who May Report
Role	Description	Standing
Target	A being who believes they experienced telepathic invasion	Full reporting rights
Witness	A being who observed signs of telepathic invasion (e.g., sudden behavioral change, reported distress)	Limited reporting rights (cannot claim harm on behalf of target without target consent)
Unintentional Emitter	A being who fears they may have accidentally invaded another	Full reporting rights; protected from prosecution for the content of the report (see Section 6)
Guardian/Advocate	A being acting on behalf of a target who is unable to report (e.g., child, cognitively impaired, non-verbal)	Full reporting rights with documentation of incapacity

3.2 Required Information (Minimal)
To submit a report, only the following is required:

Date and time of incident (approximate is acceptable)

Description of what happened (one sentence or one page—both acceptable)

Contact method for follow-up (anonymous reporting is permitted, but limits ability to respond)

All other information is optional. Do not let perfect be the enemy of done.

3.3 Optional Information (Helpful but Not Required)
Field	Why It Helps
Location of incident	Pattern detection
Description of invading being (if known)	Species, appearance, claimed identity (e.g., "voice called itself Jesus")
Duration of incident	Single event vs. ongoing
Prior relationship to invader (if any)	Context for assessment
Physical or psychological effects	Documentation of harm
Witnesses (names or descriptions)	Corroboration
Any steps already taken	Avoid duplication
4. What Happens After a Report
Step	Action	Timeline
1. Acknowledgment	Reporter receives confirmation that report was received	Within 48 hours
2. Initial Assessment	Report is reviewed for urgency (ongoing invasion, imminent threat to self or others)	Within 5 business days
3. Urgent Response (if needed)	Immediate protective measures: telepathic restraining order (NIM.5), cognitive shielding assistance, isolation of invader if identified and within jurisdiction	Within 24 hours of determination
4. Standard Response (if not urgent)	Reporter is offered: referral to support services, assistance documenting further, option to pursue formal accountability (CUR-N.4), or no further action (reporter's choice)	Within 30 days
5. Pattern Detection	Anonymized, aggregated data is entered into the Commonwealth Telepathic Incident Database	Continuous
No report shall be dismissed solely because:

The reporter cannot prove the incident

The incident seems "unbelievable" or "impossible"

The invading being is not known or not identified

The reporter has a history of mental health treatment or previous reports

5. Unintentional Emission: Good Faith Protection
If you are reporting because you fear you may have accidentally invaded another being's mind, the following protections apply:

(a) Your report shall be treated as a request for help, not an admission of guilt.

(b) You shall not be prosecuted under CUR-N.2 solely on the basis of your report.

(c) The Commonwealth shall offer you:

Free, confidential training in mental boundary control (see TIM-N.2)

Cognitive shielding assessment (see NIM.6)

Referral to peer support (see TIM-N.3)

(d) If it is later determined that your unintentional emission caused harm, you shall be subject to remedial, not punitive, measures—including training requirements, restitution if appropriate, and monitoring—unless the emission was reckless or malicious.

(e) This protection does not extend to:

Deliberate, malicious invasion

Invasion after training was offered and refused

Invasion that continues after awareness

6. Confidentiality and Anonymity
Request	Honored?	Limits
Anonymous report (no contact info)	Yes	Cannot receive follow-up, protection, or remedy
Confidential report (identity held but not shared)	Yes	May be disclosed only if: (a) reporter consents, (b) required by law, (c) imminent threat to life
Pseudonymous reporting (use a code name)	Yes	Must provide secure way to contact you for follow-up
The Commonwealth shall not out, expose, or retaliate against any reporter for making a good faith report under this protocol.
