## 3.1 Purpose

CUR-N.2 §2.8(a) names three ways to report a suspected violation: the
Ombudsman Office for Cognitive Sovereignty (established under RFAL Enforcement
Appendix §5.3), "any designated NIM reporting channel," and the Compliance
Monitoring Network. This section is the designated channel referred to in
§2.8(a)(2), and states the single intake procedure the Ombuds Office runs
regardless of which of the three doors a report arrives through.

This section is also, specifically, "the appropriate accountability body"
named in CUR-N.2 §2.5(b)(4): a being who took protective action under the
narrow exception in §2.5 reports it here, and nowhere else, within the timing
stated at §3.4 below.

## 3.2 What this channel is for

| Use this channel for | Use a different channel instead |
|---|---|
| A suspected secret coalition under CUR-N.5 | — |
| A protective-action report required by CUR-N.2 §2.5(b)(4) | — |
| A report reaching this office through the Compliance Monitoring Network | — |
| A report of telepathic invasion, where the reporter has access to the incident-specific form | `TIM-N.1`, which routes here automatically — using it instead of this section changes nothing about how the report is handled |
| A being seeking a restraining order directly, having already reported | `NIM.5`, though intake under this section may open one for you at your request under §3.5 |
| A human collaborator seeking amnesty for their own conduct | `CHIM.1`, not this section — amnesty has its own protections that ordinary intake does not carry |

## 3.3 Who may report, and what is required

The standing rules are the same as `TIM-N.1` §2 and are not repeated in full
here. In summary: a Target, a witness, a being who fears they themselves may
be responsible, and a guardian or advocate reporting on another's behalf may
all use this channel, and only three things are required to open a report —
approximate date, a description of what happened, and a way to follow up, or
an explicit election to remain anonymous. CUR-N.2 §2.8(b) requires that a
report not be dismissed for anomalousness, and CUR-N.5 §5.2(b)(3) states the
same rule for a report of concealed conduct specifically.

## 3.4 Protective-action reports (CUR-N.2 §2.5(b)(4))

A being who has taken protective action under CUR-N.2 §2.5 must report it
through this channel, and the following applies in place of §3.3:

(a) **Timing.** The report shall be made as soon as reasonably possible after
the intrusion ends, and in any event no later than one (1) hour afterward,
consistent with the narrow and time-critical nature of the exception itself.

(b) **Content.** The report shall state, to the extent known: the imminent
threat the being believed existed, what information was obtained or
communicated, why no less intrusive means was available, and the identity of
the being whose interior was accessed if known.

(c) **What happens next.** Every protective-action report opens a review
before the Telepathic Incident Review Board under `XIM.7`, which CUR-N.2
§2.5(e) requires within seventy-two (72) hours of the intrusion itself, not of
the report. Reporting late does not extend the seventy-two hours; it narrows
the time the Board has to review before the statutory clock runs, which is a
reason to report promptly rather than a defence for having acted at all.

(d) **This is not itself an accountability proceeding.** Filing under this
subsection satisfies §2.5(b)(4). Whether the four conditions of §2.5(b) were
actually met is decided by the Board under `XIM.7`, not by intake staff at the
point of filing.

## 3.5 Intake procedure

| Step | Action | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Acknowledgment | Reporter receives confirmation the report was received | Within 48 hours |
| 2. Classification | Intake determines which procedure applies: `NIM.4` (accountability against an identified being), `CHIM.1` (a collaborator's own disclosure), `XIM.7` (a protective-action report under §3.4), or referral to `TIM-N.1`/`TIM-N.5` where the report concerns an ongoing or recent telepathic incident specifically | Within 5 business days |
| 3. Routing | The report is forwarded to the applicable procedure with the reporter's consent preferences intact | Same as classification |
| 4. Restraining order, if requested | Where the reporter indicates a wish for protection, `NIM.5` intake begins in parallel and does not wait on the outcome of any determination | Immediate |
| 5. Monitoring input | Where the report concerns a pattern rather than a single identified being, it is passed to CUR-N.5 §5.2B routine monitoring as an input, in aggregate and without identifying the reporter | Continuous |

## 3.6 What intake does not do

Consistent with CUR-N.4 §4.3 and CUR-N.5 §5.8(b), intake:

(a) Makes no finding. Classification under §3.5 is administrative routing, not
an assessment of whether a violation occurred.

(b) Attaches no measure to anyone named in a report. A being named at this
stage is not thereby a Responsible Being or a Collaborator, and no record
characterises them as one until a determination under `NIM.4`.

(c) Does not require the reporter to identify the being responsible, per
CUR-N.2 §2.8(a) read with §2.6(f).

## 3.7 Confidentiality

The confidentiality table at `TIM-N.1` §6 applies without change: anonymous,
confidential, and pseudonymous reporting are each available, with the same
limits on disclosure stated there. Records identifying a Target or a reporter
are held under CUR-N.2 §2.6(e) and, where the report concerns a coalition,
under CUR-N.5 §5.10(b)–(c).
