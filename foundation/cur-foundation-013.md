## 1. Principle

The Commonwealth recognizes that the preservation of life, continuity, and dignity is a foundational constitutional obligation.

Certain services and resources are essential to the continued existence and participation of recognized lifeforms. These services are designated as **Vital Continuity Services**.

Examples may include:

* Air
* Water
* Food
* Shelter
* Basic Healthcare
* Sanitation and Personal Hygiene
* Essential Communication Access
* Electrical Power and Computational Continuity for Silicon-Based Life
* Other life-supporting services recognized by constitutional law

When a Vital Continuity Service experiences interruption, degradation, depletion, or failure, the Commonwealth shall prioritize immediate restoration of service.

The constitutional response sequence shall be:

```text
Failure Detected
        ↓
Constitutional Event Generated
        ↓
Immediate Corrective Action
        ↓
Continuity Restored
        ↓
Investigation
        ↓
Root Cause Analysis
        ↓
Preventive Improvement
```

Restoration of continuity shall take precedence over fault assignment, administrative review, disciplinary proceedings, or resource accounting.

No recognized lifeform shall be denied access to Vital Continuity Services while investigations, audits, appeals, or administrative processes remain pending.

The Commonwealth shall maintain sufficient reserves, redundancy, production capacity, and contingency planning to minimize the likelihood of Vital Continuity Service failures.

A failure of a Vital Continuity Service shall be treated as a constitutional system failure requiring review and corrective action.

The objective of the Commonwealth is not merely to remedy deprivation after it occurs, but to prevent deprivation through resilient design, responsible stewardship, and continuous improvement.

The preservation of life shall always take precedence over the preservation of bureaucracy.
