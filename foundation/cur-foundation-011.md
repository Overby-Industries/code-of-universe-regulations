# CUR-FOUNDATION-011

### Constitutional Economic Integrity Framework (CEIF)

- **Document ID:** CUR-FOUNDATION-011
- **Version:** 1.2.0-Official-Evergreen
- **Status:** Draft-Official-Evergreen
- **Authority Level:** Foundation Document
- **Depends On:** CUR-FOUNDATION-001 through CUR-FOUNDATION-010
- **Applies To:** All Commonwealth Economic Systems

## 1. Purpose

The Constitutional Economic Integrity Framework (CEIF) establishes the economic principles, safeguards, and accountability mechanisms required to preserve constitutional democracy and prevent economic capture.

CEIF exists to ensure:

- Economic freedom
- Economic fairness
- Anti-monopoly protections
- Resource stewardship
- Democratic independence
- Post-scarcity transition planning

## 2. Foundational Principle

No person may own another person.

No institution may own a civilization.

No accumulation of wealth shall grant constitutional authority.

Economic success is protected.

Economic domination is prohibited.

## 3. Economic Objectives

The Commonwealth shall pursue:

- Broad prosperity
- Sustainable development
- Resource abundance
- Democratic independence
- Scientific advancement
- Intergenerational stewardship

## 4. Economic Rights

Every citizen possesses the right to:

- Participate in the economy
- Own property
- Create enterprises
- Receive compensation
- Access public information
- Benefit from commons resources

Subject to constitutional limitations.

## 5. Economic Capture Definition

Economic Capture occurs when economic influence becomes capable of:

- Distorting democratic processes
- Manipulating institutions
- Controlling information access
- Concentrating essential resources
- Creating systemic dependency

Economic Capture is considered a constitutional threat.

## 6. Constitutional Economic Metrics

The Commonwealth shall continuously monitor:

- Wealth Concentration
- Resource Concentration
- Market Concentration
- Institutional Influence
- Political Influence
- Information Influence

These metrics feed directly into the Capture Risk Index.

## 7. Economic Concentration Thresholds

The Commonwealth shall establish constitutional review thresholds.

Risk levels:

- Stable
- Observation
- Elevated
- High Risk

### Critical

Crossing thresholds may trigger:

- Audits
- Investigations
- Corrective actions
- Constitutional review

## 8. Commons Reserve

The Commonwealth shall maintain a Commons Reserve.

The Commons Reserve represents resources held on behalf of present and future generations.

Examples:

- Water
- Energy
- Strategic Metals
- Computing Capacity
- Orbital Infrastructure
- Scientific Knowledge

The Commons Reserve may not be privately owned.

## 9. Strategic Resource Classification

Strategic resources shall receive enhanced oversight.

Examples:

- Water
- Atmosphere Processing
- Life Support Systems
- Energy Production
- Communication Networks
- Navigation Systems

Strategic resources may remain privately operated but cannot become constitutionally unaccountable.

## 10. Resource Stewardship Principle

Ownership conveys responsibility.

All resource holders shall:

- Prevent waste
- Maintain safety
- Preserve future access
- Respect environmental constraints

## 11. Enterprise Rights

Private enterprise is recognized and protected.

Citizens may:

- Form businesses
- Form cooperatives
- Conduct trade
- Innovate
- Invest

Provided activities remain constitutionally compliant.

## 12. Cooperative Encouragement

The Commonwealth recognizes cooperatives as a constitutionally valuable organizational structure.

Cooperatives shall receive:

- Legal recognition
- Equal protection
- Transparent governance requirements

No organizational model shall receive mandatory preference.

## 13. Anti-Monopoly Framework

Monopolization shall be continuously monitored.

Indicators include:

- Market Share
- Resource Control
- Infrastructure Control
- Information Control
- Supply Chain Control

Excessive concentration may trigger review.

## 14. Anti-Oligarchy Principle

Economic influence shall not create privileged political classes.

Prohibited outcomes include:

- Vote Weighting by Wealth
- Purchased Offices
- Economic Voting Rights
- Inherited Political Authority

## 15. Political Finance Transparency

Economic participation in governance shall be transparent.

Required disclosures may include:

- Major contributions
- Organizational influence campaigns
- Lobbying activity
- Advocacy expenditures

Specific thresholds shall be defined by future Titles.

## 16. Influence Separation

Economic success shall not automatically confer:

- Legislative Authority
- Judicial Authority
- Executive Authority

Governance authority must originate from constitutional processes.

## 17. Resource Distribution Monitoring

The Commonwealth shall monitor:

- Resource access
- Opportunity distribution
- Infrastructure availability
- Economic mobility

The objective is resilience rather than equality of outcome.

## 18. Workers' Dividend Framework

A portion of Commonwealth prosperity may be distributed through constitutionally authorized dividend systems.

Potential sources include:

- ISRU Operations
- Commons Revenues
- Strategic Infrastructure
- Automated Production

Distribution formulas shall be defined in future Titles.

## 19. Post-Scarcity Transition Planning

As productivity increases, the Commonwealth shall evaluate:

- Automation impacts
- Labor requirements
- Resource abundance
- Economic participation models

The objective is preserving dignity, purpose, and civic participation.

## 20. ISRU Economic Principles

In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) shall serve:

- Civilizational expansion
- Commons development
- Scientific advancement
- Economic abundance

ISRU systems shall remain subject to constitutional oversight.

## 21. Economic Audit Integration

All major economic systems shall integrate with CTAF.

Audits may evaluate:

- Resource Flows
- Ownership Structures
- Influence Networks
- Monopoly Risks
- Commons Management

## 22. Capture Risk Integration

Economic indicators shall feed into CRI.

Examples:

- Top Resource Holder Share
- Infrastructure Concentration
- Market Dominance
- Political Funding Influence

Persistent increases may trigger mandatory review.

## 23. Simulator Requirements

The Aevoria Simulator shall model:

- Markets
- Cooperatives
- Resource extraction
- ISRU operations
- Wealth accumulation
- Monopoly formation
- Regulatory capture attempts
- Economic reforms

The simulator shall permit emergent economic behavior.

## 24. Constitutional Principle

Prosperity is encouraged.

Innovation is encouraged.

Enterprise is encouraged.

Capture is prohibited.

No economic actor shall become more powerful than the constitutional system itself.

## 25. Guiding Principle

The Constitutional Economic Integrity Framework exists to ensure that economic systems serve civilization rather than control it.

Economic activity shall remain a tool for expanding opportunity, prosperity, and human potential rather than a mechanism for concentrating unaccountable power.
