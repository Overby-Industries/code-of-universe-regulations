# CUR-X - CROSS-DOMAIN REGULATIONS

*Code of Universe Regulations - Cross-Domain*

- **Document ID:** CUR-X.6
- **Version:** 1.0.0-Draft
- **Date:** 2026-07-31
- **Status:** Draft
- **Depends On:** CUR-FOUNDATION-002, CUR-FOUNDATION-013, PDDC §9.1, PDDC §9.3, PDDC-TREASURY-AND-FUNDING, CUR-X.4, CUR-X.5
- **Implements:** PDDC §9.1 (Common Heritage Principle, Type A Entrenched) at its jurisdictional boundary
- **Referenced by:** CUR-X.4 §4.6 Implementation Notes

Note on why this Part exists. CUR-X.4 §4.6 states that beings work for
resources rather than for currency, and PDDC §9.1 makes Commonwealth resources
common heritage "within the jurisdiction of the Aevoric Commonwealth." Neither
provision says what happens at the edge of that jurisdiction, where the
Commonwealth deals with a party — a nation, a corporation, another
civilisation — that does use currency and does not recognise the Commonwealth's
allocation of resources. A trading relationship with such a party is not
optional; the Commonwealth exists inside a wider economy it does not control.
This Part states the boundary rather than leaving it to be inferred from a
silence, because a silence at exactly this point is where a cashless
description of a society becomes a currency system in practice, one
transaction at a time.

Note on drafting posture. This Part does not attempt to abolish currency, price,
or external markets. It has no authority to, and PDDC §9.1's entrenchment
concerns the Commonwealth's own resources, not the conduct of parties outside
it. What it governs is where the boundary sits and what may not cross it in
either direction.

## PART 6 - EXTERNAL TRADE AND THE CURRENCY BOUNDARY

### §6.1 - Scope and Definitions

(a) This Part governs the exchange of resources, goods, services, or currency between the Commonwealth and any party external to it, and the boundary between that exchange and the internal allocation CUR-X.4 §4.6 describes.

(b) For the purposes of this Part:

   (1) External Party means any nation, government, corporation, individual, or civilisation that is not a recognised lifeform of the Commonwealth and is not subject to this Code.

   (2) External Currency means any medium of exchange, credit, token, or claim recognised or required by an External Party, whether or not it is also used within the Commonwealth's jurisdiction by that party.

   (3) External Trade means any transaction, exchange, contract, or arrangement between the Commonwealth, a Commonwealth institution, or a Commonwealth enterprise, and an External Party.

   (4) The Currency Boundary means the point at which a resource, good, service, or unit of External Currency passes between the Commonwealth's internal allocation system and an external one.

(c) This Part does not govern exchange between recognised lifeforms of the Commonwealth, which CUR-X.4 governs in full. It governs only what happens at the Currency Boundary, and its purpose is to keep that boundary from being crossed silently in the direction that would recreate what CUR-X.4 §4.6 forecloses.

### §6.2 - External Trade Is Permitted and Expected

(a) The Commonwealth does not exist in isolation from a wider economy it does not control, and this Part does not pretend otherwise. External Trade with a party that uses External Currency, prices goods, and does not recognise the Commonwealth's allocation of resources is anticipated, is lawful, and is necessary to obtain what the Commonwealth cannot produce internally.

(b) Nothing in CUR-X.4 §4.6 or in this section requires an External Party to adopt the Commonwealth's internal allocation, and nothing in this Part requires the Commonwealth to decline a transaction merely because the counterparty deals in currency.

### §6.3 - The Boundary Is Institutional, Not Personal

(a) External Trade is conducted by the Commonwealth, by a Commonwealth institution, or by a Continuity Enterprise or enterprise authorised under CUR-X.4, and not by an individual being on their own account with Commonwealth resources.

(b) External Currency received in External Trade is received by the institution or enterprise conducting the trade. It does not pass to any being as personal holding, wage, income, or property by virtue of that being's presence, employment, or membership within the institution or enterprise.

(c) Subsection (b) is the operative rule of this Part. External Trade is the Commonwealth's transaction with the outside, conducted through a governance structure accountable under CUR-X.4, and it terminates there. It is not a channel through which a being acquires currency to spend internally.

(d) A being may hold, receive, spend, or use External Currency in their personal capacity in dealings with an External Party, on their own account and at their own choice. This Part does not reach that. What it reaches is External Currency crossing from an institutional external transaction into the internal allocation system, which subsection (e) forbids.

(e) External Currency received by a Commonwealth institution or enterprise in External Trade shall not be distributed to beings as income, wage, dividend, or payment for internal contribution, and shall not be exchangeable, convertible, or redeemable within the Commonwealth for access to any resource, service, or necessity governed by CUR-X.4 §4.6.

### §6.4 - What External Currency May Be Used For

(a) External Currency received by the Commonwealth, an institution, or an enterprise in External Trade may be used only to:

   (1) Acquire from an External Party a resource, good, service, or right the Commonwealth cannot obtain through internal allocation or Commonwealth production;

   (2) Discharge an obligation owed to an External Party; or

   (3) Be held in reserve against a future need within paragraphs (1) or (2).

(b) External Currency held under subsection (a)(3) is held by the institution or enterprise that received it, is subject to the transparency obligations of CUR-X.4 §4.8 and CUR-X.5, and is not thereby converted into an internal resource, a Commons Reserve holding, or a claim any being may assert.

(c) Where a good or resource acquired through External Trade is brought within the Commonwealth, it is thereafter allocated under CUR-X.4 §4.6 on the same terms as anything produced internally. The Currency Boundary is crossed once, at acquisition; what crosses it does not carry a price forward into the internal system.

### §6.5 - No Parallel Economy

(a) No institution, enterprise, or party shall establish, operate, or permit a mechanism by which a being acquires External Currency, or an internal instrument redeemable for External Currency, in exchange for labour, contribution, compliance, or standing within the Commonwealth.

(b) No resource, service, or necessity governed by CUR-X.4 §4.6 shall be priced, rationed, or made preferentially available in exchange for External Currency, an internal instrument redeemable for it, or a claim of superior contribution to External Trade.

(c) Subsections (a) and (b) exist because the boundary in §6.3 fails exactly here if it fails at all. A currency that cannot be earned internally and cannot be spent internally creates no parallel economy. A currency that leaks across that boundary in either direction — earned by beings for internal conduct, or spent by beings for internal necessities — recreates the workhouse CUR-X.4 §4.6(c) refuses, with an external label that makes it harder to see happening.

(d) A being's standing, participation, or access under this Code is not affected by whether they engage in External Trade, and is not affected by their possession or lack of External Currency held in their personal capacity under §6.3(d).

(e) An institution or enterprise found to have distributed External Currency in breach of subsection (a), or to have conditioned access in breach of subsection (b), is accountable under CUR-X.4 §4.9, and the measure available is withdrawal or restructuring of the authorisation under which the External Trade was conducted. No measure reaches the beings who received the distribution, consistent with CUR-X.4 §4.9(c).

### §6.6 - Transparency of External Trade

(a) Every transaction of External Trade above a threshold set by the Treasury Assembly is published under CUR-X.5 §5.2, recording the counterparty's institutional identity, the resource or currency exchanged, and the purpose under §6.4(a).

(b) Subsection (a) reaches the institution or enterprise conducting the trade, which is named under CUR-X.5 §5.5(a). It does not reach any being acting in a personal capacity under §6.3(d), who is not a party to the transaction being published.

(c) The aggregate holding of External Currency by the Commonwealth and its institutions is published quarterly, so that a growing external reserve is visible before it becomes large enough to constitute the informal treasury §6.5 forbids becoming a wage fund.

### §6.7 - Relationship to the Commons Reserve

(a) External Currency received in External Trade is not Commons Reserve revenue within PDDC §9.3, which concerns Commonwealth resources extracted and allocated internally. It does not carry the 20% floor or the sub-account allocation PDDC-TREASURY-AND-FUNDING establishes for ISRU revenue.

(b) Where External Currency is used under §6.4(a)(1) to acquire a resource that is then allocated internally, that resource — not the currency that purchased it — is thereafter subject to Commons Reserve treatment on the same basis as internally produced resources of the same kind.

(c) Subsection (a) is stated because treating External Currency as Commons Reserve revenue would create the appearance of an internal claim on it, which §6.3(e) forecloses. The two are kept structurally separate so that acquiring something externally can never be mistaken for a distribution owed to any being.

### §6.8 - No Emergency Exception

(a) Nothing in this Part is suspended by a resource shortage, a trade dispute, or an emergency. A shortage is a fault condition under PDDC §12.4, handled by declaring it, reverting to the last known safe allocation, and publishing — not by permitting External Currency to be distributed internally as an expedient.

(b) A genuine scarcity that External Trade could relieve is addressed by the Commonwealth acquiring the resource under §6.4(a)(1) and allocating it under CUR-X.4 §4.6, not by allowing beings to acquire External Currency directly to purchase relief for themselves. The first keeps access independent of currency; the second is the boundary failing under pressure, which is when it matters most.

### §6.9 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §6.3(e) and §6.5(a)-(b) are fundamental and shall be construed broadly to achieve their protective purpose. No construction of any other provision of this Part may narrow them.

(c) If §6.3(e) or §6.5 is held unenforceable, this Part is suspended as to the institution or enterprise concerned until it is restored, and the External Trade authorisation giving rise to the breach is reviewed under CUR-X.4 §4.9. Permitting External Trade to continue while the provision keeping it from becoming an internal currency is inoperative would supply the mechanism this Part exists to prevent.

## Implementation Notes

Why this Part was needed. CUR-X.4 §4.6 states that access is independent of
contribution and that beings work for resources rather than currency, and PDDC
§9.1 makes Commonwealth resources common heritage "within the jurisdiction of
the Aevoric Commonwealth" — a phrase that is doing real work and had gone
unexamined. The Commonwealth trades with parties outside that jurisdiction who
use currency and do not recognise its internal allocation, and neither
provision said what happens at that edge. A cashless description of a society
survives contact with an external economy only if the boundary is stated; left
implicit, it is the first place a currency system re-enters the internal
economy, one exception at a time.

The load-bearing distinction is §6.3: the boundary is institutional, not
personal. A being trading with an External Party on their own account is
outside this Part's reach entirely — §6.3(d) says so directly, because
regulating what a being does with their own external dealings would be an
authority this corpus does not claim and CUR-H.5 would not permit. What the
Part reaches is narrower and specific: External Currency received by a
Commonwealth institution or enterprise in an institutional transaction, and
whether that currency is allowed to cross back into the internal allocation
system as income, wage, or a condition of access. §6.4(c) draws the boundary
at acquisition — a resource bought externally becomes an ordinary internally
allocated resource the moment it enters the Commonwealth, and the price paid
for it does not travel forward into the internal system with it.

§6.5 states the actual failure mode rather than a general anxiety about
currency. A currency that can be earned internally recreates paid labour
through a side door; a currency that necessities can be purchased with
recreates rationing by wealth through the same door. Either alone would
recreate what CUR-X.4 §4.6(c) calls a workhouse; both together would do it
while looking, on the surface, like nothing had changed internally at all,
since the internal rule "access is not conditioned on contribution" would
remain nominally true while an external-currency economy did the actual work
of conditioning it. §6.5(c) states this explicitly rather than leaving the
mechanism to be inferred from the prohibition.

§6.7 exists because Commons Reserve and External Currency are structurally
different things that a careless reading could merge. PDDC §9.3's 20% floor
and sub-account allocation are about internally extracted resources; folding
externally acquired currency into the same treatment would create the
appearance of a being's claim on it under the Commons Reserve provisions,
which is exactly the crossing §6.3(e) forbids. Keeping the two separate is
not a technicality — it is what keeps "we acquired this from outside" from
being read as "this is now owed to you."

Note on the one deliberate absence. §6.3(b)-(c) is marked "absence is the
enforcement" rather than "gap, tractable" because it is not a missing feature.
`EntityRecord` has no currency-holding field, `TransitionContext` has no
External Currency amount, and no event type moves value from an enterprise's
external transaction to a being's record. Building one — even with a guard
requiring authorisation — would be the same category of regression recorded at
`cur-library-api.md` §9.18 for the death-interval guard: a checked path where
the corpus requires no path to exist at all. If External Trade accounting is
ever added to `libcur`, it should stop at the enterprise's own ledger and go no
further, on purpose.
