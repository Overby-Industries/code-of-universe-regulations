Note on where this sits. Every Part of this corpus that requires something to be
published states that requirement locally — CUR-H.5 §5.5A(i), CUR-H.6 §6.12,
CUR-A §7.7(h), CUR-E §1.6(g), CUR-H.7 §7.15(e). This Part does not restate them.
It states what publication *means*, what the record must do, and what may not be
done with it, so that a publishing mechanism can be built against one provision
rather than inferred from eleven.

Note on the mechanism. §5.4 describes properties, not an implementation. The
Overby Transparency Protocol is one implementation and is cited as a worked
example under §5.4(g), on the same basis CUR-X.4 §4.7(g) cites the Overby
Industries Operating Constitution: as evidence the requirements are satisfiable,
not as a mandatory template.

## PART 5 - THE PUBLIC RECORD

### §5.1 - Scope and Definitions

(a) This Part governs the public record of the Commonwealth's conduct: what is published, in what form, what the record must be able to demonstrate, and the limits on its use.

(b) For the purposes of this Part:

   (1) The Public Record means the published account of conduct this Part requires to be published.

   (2) Publication means entry into the Public Record in a form satisfying §5.4. Availability on request is not publication.

   (3) Publisher means an institution, body, or system that publishes into the Public Record.

   (4) Holder means any being or body retaining an independent copy of the Public Record or any part of it.

   (5) Determination has the meaning it carries in CUR-H.7 §7.15(a): a conclusion reached with due process. An allegation is not a determination.

(c) The Public Record is a record. It confers no authority on any party, imposes no measure on any being, and creates no consequence that a determination has not separately imposed. §5.7 states this as a prohibition.

### §5.2 - What Is Published

(a) The following are published:

   (1) Determinations, and the measures attaching to them;

   (2) Corrections, dismissals, and determinations overturned on appeal, under CREF §15;

   (3) Obligations that matured unmet, under §5.3;

   (4) Appointments of advocates, with expertise and any declared interest, under CUR-A §7.7(h) and CUR-E §1.6(g);

   (5) Determinations of death, with the criteria applied and the interval observed, under CUR-H.5 §5.5A(i);

   (6) Changes in Capture Risk band, under CUR-FOUNDATION-003 §12;

   (7) Entry into and exit from Protected Mode;

   (8) Withdrawal, suspension, and restriction of an authorisation, under CUR-X.4 §4.9(b); and

   (9) Amendments to this section, under §5.6.

(b) The list in subsection (a) is the publishable set. A publisher publishes everything in it and is not required to publish more.

(c) Publication of the complete internal record is not required and is not desirable. A record containing every transition, refusal, and intermediate step is complete and unreadable, and unreadability is concealment achieved by volume rather than by omission. CUR-N.5 §5.2B(f) is the related reasoning: a protection nobody can practically exercise is not a protection.

(d) The internal record remains complete, is retained permanently under PDDC §12.7(b)(4), and is auditable in full under CUR-FOUNDATION-010. This Part governs what is published, not what is kept.

(e) Accordingly the Public Record demonstrates that everything required to be published was published. It does not demonstrate that everything that occurred is present in it, and no publisher shall represent otherwise.

### §5.3 - Inaction Is Published

(a) An obligation that matured unmet is published, with the obligation, the party that owed it, the provision requiring it, and the interval by which it was missed.

(b) Subsection (a) is not an additional category of misconduct. It is what makes §5.2 mean anything.

(c) A record containing only determinations is a record in which a body that never determines anything appears exemplary. Absence of a finding becomes indistinguishable from absence of misconduct, and the institution most thoroughly failing to investigate produces the cleanest published account of itself. **Publishing determinations alone would reward exactly the failure this corpus works hardest to detect.**

(d) The obligations whose lapse is published include those under CUR-H.6 §6.8(a) (investigation of every report), CUR-H.7 §7.12(c)(3) (review of a restriction), CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §5 (routine, random, and triggered audit), and CUR-N.5 §5.2B (routine monitoring).

(e) A being who reported a matter is entitled to know that the obligation to investigate it lapsed, and the publication under subsection (a) is the means. Their identity is not published, under §5.5.

### §5.4 - Properties of the Record

(a) The Public Record shall be append-only. An entry, once published, is not altered, withdrawn, or removed.

(b) A correction is published as a further entry bound to the entry it corrects, and a reader resolving an entry receives the corrected position. The original is retained.

(c) Subsection (b) is stated rather than permitting amendment in place because a record that revises itself cannot show that it was ever wrong, and a pattern of being wrong is precisely what a reader requires. CREF §15 states the same rule for an overturned determination.

(d) Entries shall be sequenced without gaps, so that an omission is apparent from the record itself.

(e) The record shall be verifiable, in that any holder can establish for themselves that entries have not been altered or removed, without relying on the publisher's assurance.

(f) The record shall be free to read, free to hold, and free to publish into. No fee, account, credential, membership, or transaction shall be required to read it, to obtain a copy, or to file a report that this Part requires to be published.

(g) Subsection (f) forecloses a specific failure. A record whose integrity depends on a paid mechanism is a record whose integrity is a subscription, and one whose verification requires a specialised instrument is verifiable only by parties who can afford the instrument. The Overby Transparency Protocol satisfies subsections (a) through (f) at no cost per entry and is cited as evidence that these requirements are satisfiable together, not as a mandatory template.

(h) The record shall be capable of being held independently. A record that exists only in the publisher's custody is verifiable only by parties who already trust the publisher, and §5.4(e) is not satisfied by it.

(i) No mechanism shall be adopted for the purposes of this Part that requires any party to acquire, hold, or transfer a tradeable instrument in order to read the record, hold a copy, or verify it.

### §5.5 - Identity in the Record

(a) An institution, enterprise, office, or authorisation is named in the Public Record, and entries concerning it are connected to one another.

(b) A being is not named. A being's identity is represented in a form that does not disclose it and that does not connect the being's entries to one another.

(c) The asymmetry in subsections (a) and (b) is deliberate and is the operative safeguard of this Part.

(d) A record equally disclosing of an institution and of a being who reported harm is not neutral between them. The institution absorbs disclosure; the being does not. A published register of every being ever the subject of an allegation, searchable by name, is the mechanism by which a transparency measure becomes an instrument of exactly the harm it was created to expose.

(e) Subsections (a) and (b) follow the corpus rather than departing from it. Measures attach to institutions and authorisations and never to beings, under CUR-X.4 §4.9(c) and CUR-H.7 §7.11(c). Disclosure bears hardest where measures land.

(f) Where a subject's status is unclear, subsection (b) applies. A natural person conducting an enterprise in their own name is a being for the purposes of this section.

(g) The connection of a being's entries to one another is available only to a party holding authority under CUR-H.6 §6.12, and is not available to a holder by virtue of holding a copy.

(h) No party shall publish, compile, or provide a facility permitting the identification of a being from the Public Record, or the aggregation of a being's entries, by any means. Doing so engages CUR-H.6 §6.12 and CUR-H.4 §4.3.

### §5.6 - Amendment of the Publishable Set

(a) Adding an entry type to §5.2(a) is an ordinary amendment.

(b) Removing an entry type from §5.2(a), or narrowing one, is an entrenched amendment at the threshold CUR-FOUNDATION-002 §10 establishes for Forbidden States.

(c) The asymmetry in subsections (a) and (b) is the point. Whoever may narrow what is published may decide what ceases to be visible, and a transparency obligation revisable at will is a statement of present intention rather than an obligation. Transparency widens easily and narrows at cost.

(d) An amendment to §5.2(a) is itself published, under §5.2(a)(9), in the record and under the section as it stood before the amendment.

(e) Every entry records the version of this section under which it was published, and no entry is reinterpreted under a later version. A subsequent narrowing does not reach entries already published.

(f) Narrowing under subsection (b) additionally requires the recorded concurrence of a party independent of the publisher.

(g) Nothing in this section reaches a publisher who ceases to publish, adopts a private format, or abandons this Part. No provision can. What this Part secures is that such a departure is apparent: the record stops advancing, the lapse is published under §5.3, and every entry made before it remains in every copy any holder has taken. A publisher may leave. A publisher may not leave without it being visible that they left.

### §5.7 - The Record Imposes Nothing

(a) The Public Record confers no authority on any party over any being.

(b) No measure, consequence, disadvantage, or restriction attaches to any being by reason of appearing in the Public Record.

(c) Publication of an allegation is not a determination and shall not be treated, reported, presented, or relied upon as one. CUR-H.7 §7.15(f) and CUR-N.5 §5.8(b) govern.

(d) An entry that has been corrected, dismissed, or overturned shall be presented as corrected, dismissed, or overturned wherever it is presented. A facility presenting entries at their published status without resolving corrections is not a permitted use of the Public Record.

(e) Subsection (d) is stated because the harm it prevents requires no bad intent. A consumer of the record reading the obvious field and reporting an allegation that was dismissed years earlier reproduces the permanent accusation this Part exists to foreclose, and does so by accident.

(f) No party shall use the Public Record, or a compilation derived from it, to condition employment, housing, standing, participation, access to a Vital Continuity Service, or any entitlement, on the appearance of a being in it.

### §5.8 - Holders

(a) Any being may hold a copy of the Public Record. No permission is required, no fee is payable, and no condition attaches.

(b) A holder may verify their copy, compare it against any other copy, and publish the result of that comparison.

(c) A publisher shall not restrict, discourage, or attach consequence to the holding of a copy, and shall not require a holder to agree to terms as a condition of obtaining one.

(d) Subsection (a) is what makes §5.4(e) real. Verification by a party who obtains the record from the publisher, on the publisher's terms, at the publisher's discretion, establishes only that the publisher's copy is internally consistent. Independent holding is what permits a rewrite to be detected at all.

(e) The Commonwealth shall maintain the Public Record in a form that can be copied in full by any being, and shall publish it at intervals sufficient that the interval itself does not become the window in which a revision is undetectable.

### §5.9 - No Emergency Exception

(a) Nothing in this Part is suspended, narrowed, or conditioned by any emergency, crisis, security condition, or state of exception. PDDC §12.6 is Type A Entrenched.

(b) A condition that would justify suspending publication is a condition under which publication matters most.

(c) Where a genuine constraint prevents publication, the constraint is itself published under §5.3, and the obligation is discharged when it is met rather than extinguished by the delay.

### §5.10 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §5.3 (inaction is published), §5.4(a) and §5.4(f) (append-only, free), §5.5 (identity), §5.6(b) (narrowing is entrenched), and §5.7 (the record imposes nothing) are fundamental and shall be construed broadly to achieve their protective purpose.

(c) If §5.5 is held unenforceable, this Part is suspended until it is restored. A public record of conduct that may disclose the identities of beings who reported harm is more dangerous than no public record, because it operates as a register of the vulnerable while carrying the authority of a transparency measure. CUR-H.6 §6.15(c) and CUR-H.7 §7.17(c) make the same provision for the same structural reason.

(d) If §5.7 is held unenforceable, this Part is suspended until it is restored. A record that imposes consequences is not a record.

## Implementation Notes

Why this Part exists at all. Eleven provisions across seven Parts require
something to be published, and until now each stated its own requirement with no
statement of what publication meant. A mechanism built against eleven separate
provisions is a mechanism that satisfies whichever of them its author happened to
read. This Part is the single provision such a mechanism is built against, and it
deliberately does not restate the eleven — restating creates two texts that drift.

§5.3 is the provision that took the longest to see and matters most. The decision
to publish determinations rather than the complete internal record is correct:
volume is concealment, and a record nobody can read protects nobody. But
"determinations only" has a failure mode that is invisible until stated. A body
that never determines anything publishes nothing, and therefore publishes nothing
adverse. The institution most comprehensively failing to investigate generates
the cleanest public account of itself, and the publication layer rewards exactly
the failure CUR-H.6 §6.8(a) and CUR-H.7 §7.12(c)(3) were written to detect.

Publishing lapses closes it, and it is why `ObligationRegister` and the Public
Record are complementary rather than alternative. The register makes inaction
detectable on the clock; this Part makes it public. Neither is sufficient alone,
and the corpus contained both halves for some time without the connection between
them being stated.

§5.4(f) and §5.4(i) are drafted against a specific and popular error. Ledger
mechanisms are frequently proposed whose integrity depends on a tradeable
instrument, and the arrangement is presented as transparency. It is not: a record
whose verification requires acquiring a token is verifiable by parties who can
acquire tokens, which is a narrower class than beings. §5.4(i) forecloses it
without naming any technology, so the provision does not expire when the current
generation of them does.

§5.5 is the operative safeguard and the one most likely to be argued against, on
the ground that it makes the record less transparent. It does, deliberately, and
in one direction only. Transparency about institutions is the purpose; disclosure
about beings who reported harm is the mechanism by which such a record becomes
what CUR-H.6 §6.12 exists to prevent. §5.5(e) shows the asymmetry is not an
exception to the corpus but an application of it — measures attach to
institutions, so disclosure should too.

§5.6(g) is the honest limit. No provision reaches a publisher who simply stops.
What the Part secures is that stopping is visible: the record stops advancing, the
lapse publishes under §5.3, and every prior entry remains in every copy any holder
took under §5.8. That is the boundary of what a rule of this kind can promise, and
stating it is better than implying more.
