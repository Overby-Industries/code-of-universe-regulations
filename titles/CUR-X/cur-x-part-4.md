# CUR-X - CROSS-DOMAIN REGULATIONS

*Code of Universe Regulations - Cross-Domain*

- **Document ID:** CUR-X.4
- **Version:** 1.0.0-Draft
- **Date:** 2026-07-27
- **Status:** Draft

Note on vocabulary. This Part describes mechanisms and avoids the names of
economic systems. Labels carry commitments the Code has not made and invite
disputes about doctrine rather than about conduct. What follows can be read
without agreeing on what to call it: enterprises that supply necessities are
owned by those they serve and governed by those who work in them, access to
necessities is never conditioned on contribution, and concentrated private
authority over other beings is subject to the same limits as concentrated
public authority. Nothing here depends on that arrangement having a name.

## PART 4 - ECONOMIC ENTERPRISE, OWNERSHIP, AND ACCOUNTABILITY

### §4.1 - Scope and Definitions

(a) This Part governs economic enterprises operating within Commonwealth jurisdiction: how they are owned, how authority within them is constrained, and how they are held accountable.

(b) For the purposes of this Part:

   (1) Enterprise means any organised undertaking that produces, processes, distributes, or maintains goods, services, or infrastructure, whether or not it is described as a company, cooperative, agency, collective, partnership, or association.

   (2) Continuity Enterprise means an enterprise whose operations produce, process, distribute, or maintain a Vital Continuity Service under CUR-FOUNDATION-013, or the infrastructure on which one depends.

   (3) Internal Authority means the power an enterprise exercises over the beings who work within it, including direction of work, conditions, discipline, admission, and exclusion.

   (4) Contribution means work, labour, service, or effort offered to an enterprise or to the Commonwealth.

(c) This Part reaches enterprises as structures. Beings working within an enterprise hold their rights in full and are not diminished by association with it, and measures under this Part attach to enterprises and authorisations, never to beings.

(d) It is a cross-domain Part because an enterprise is not a lifeform and belongs to no domain, while its conduct reaches every domain. Where an enterprise's activity engages a domain, that domain's provisions apply concurrently under CUR-X §3.4.

### §4.2 - An Enterprise Is a Governance Structure

(a) An enterprise that directs the work, conditions, or livelihood of conscious beings exercises governance over them. It is a governance structure in fact, and this Part treats it as one regardless of how it is constituted or described.

(b) Accordingly, the constraints the Code places on governance apply to enterprises. It is not a defence that an enterprise is private, voluntary, commercially constituted, or not part of the Commonwealth's institutional structure.

(c) The distinction between public and private authority is not a distinction the Code recognises for this purpose. Domination is domination. A being subject to arbitrary, unreviewable, unaccountable authority over their work, livelihood, and conditions is subject to it whether the entity exercising it holds a charter or a certificate of incorporation.

(d) The concentration of authority within an enterprise is a capture pattern under PDDC §13.1 where it reaches the thresholds of that section, and is assessed under the Capture Risk Index of CUR-FOUNDATION-003 on the same basis as concentration within a governance institution.

(e) Subsection (c) is stated plainly because the omission it corrects is common. Constitutional protections are frequently written to bind states alone, leaving the entity that determines whether a being eats, where they live, and what they may say at work entirely unconstrained. The Commonwealth does not adopt that omission.

### §4.3 - Prohibition on Unreviewable Internal Authority

(a) No enterprise shall exercise internal authority that is unreviewable, unaccountable, or exempt from challenge by those subject to it.

(b) An enterprise in which authority flows only downward, in which those subject to a decision have no route to contest it, and in which the deciding party is accountable to no one subject to the decision, is an exercise of unreviewable authority. It engages FORBIDDEN-004 under CUR-FOUNDATION-002 §6.

(c) Every being working within an enterprise holds:

   (1) The right to be heard on decisions materially affecting their work or conditions;

   (2) The right to dissent, and to have dissent recorded;

   (3) The right to contest a decision before a body that is not the party that made it;

   (4) The right to information sufficient to evaluate decisions affecting them, under §4.8; and

   (5) The right to leave, without penalty, forfeiture, or loss of any entitlement guaranteed under §4.6.

(d) The rights in subsection (c) are not conditional on the enterprise's size, form, profitability, or sector, and are not waivable by contract, by terms of engagement, or by the being's consent. A purported waiver is void, since consent given under the conditions §4.6 exists to remove is not consent within CUR-N.2 §2.1(c).

(e) Permanent unaccountable positions of authority within an enterprise are prohibited. Coordination of work is necessary and legitimate; permanent command over persons is neither, and the difference is that coordination is answerable to those coordinated.

(f) An enterprise may not condition employment, engagement, or advancement on a being's beliefs, on their participation in worship or ideological affirmation, on their consent to cognitive monitoring, or on their waiver of any right under this Part. CUR-H.2 §2.4(a) and CUR-D.6 §6.3(b)(5) apply concurrently.

### §4.4 - Ownership of Continuity Enterprises

(a) A Continuity Enterprise is owned by the beings it serves. Ownership is held in common by the recognised lifeforms of the Commonwealth and is not alienable, transferable, or convertible into private title.

(b) Subsection (a) follows from PDDC §9.1, which makes natural resources the common heritage of all Avians and Recognised Life-Forms and provides that no entity may claim ownership of Commonwealth resources in perpetuity. An enterprise holding exclusive control of the means by which a necessity reaches beings holds, in substance, what §9.1 forbids holding.

(c) A Continuity Enterprise operates under an authorisation, not a title. The authorisation conveys the right to operate, and carries obligations of transparency, environmental compliance, and accountability. It does not convey ownership of the underlying resource, the infrastructure, or the service.

(d) Governance of a Continuity Enterprise is exercised by those who work in it under §4.7 and is accountable to those it serves through the Commonwealth's ordinary democratic institutions. Neither displaces the other: operational decisions belong to those doing the work, and the mandate belongs to those served.

(e) No Continuity Enterprise may be structured so that a party outside the Commonwealth's democratic process holds authority to overrule decisions made within it.

(f) Surplus produced by a Continuity Enterprise is not private return. It flows to the Commons Reserve under PDDC §9.3 or to expansion of the service, as determined democratically.

(g) Nothing in this section authorises seizure of a being's personal property, tools, dwelling, or the products of their own work. §4.5 states the boundary.

### §4.5 - What This Part Does Not Reach

(a) This Part governs enterprises exercising authority over beings or supplying necessities. It does not govern:

   (1) A being's own work, craft, study, art, or subsistence activity;

   (2) A being's personal property, tools, dwelling, or possessions;

   (3) Voluntary association, mutual aid, gift, exchange, or collaboration between beings, where no party exercises internal authority over another;

   (4) An enterprise operated by a being alone or by beings in genuine equality without internal authority; or

   (5) The production of goods and services that are not Vital Continuity Services, which remain subject to §4.2, §4.3, §4.8, and §4.9 but not to §4.4.

(b) The distinction in subsection (a)(5) is deliberate. Common ownership attaches to the things beings cannot live without, because that is where private control becomes power over life. It does not attach to everything, because a rule that reached everything would be the domination this Part exists to prevent, wearing different clothes.

(c) No being is required to join, form, or participate in any enterprise. The right to work alone, to associate freely, and to decline participation is protected, and §4.6 ensures that declining carries no cost to survival.

(d) An enterprise does not become a Continuity Enterprise because its output is useful, popular, or economically significant. It becomes one by supplying a Vital Continuity Service or the infrastructure a Vital Continuity Service depends on, and the classification is contestable under §4.9(e).

### §4.6 - Contribution and Access Are Independent

(a) Access to Vital Continuity Services is not conditioned on contribution, employment, membership in any enterprise, productivity, or any assessment of desert.

(b) This is absolute. No being shall be required to work in order to eat, drink, be sheltered, receive healthcare, breathe, or — where the being is Silicon-Based Life — receive power and computational continuity. CUR-FOUNDATION-013 admits no exception, and PDDC §10.1(c) prohibits forced labour and non-consensual servitude.

(c) Subsection (b) is the provision on which the rest of this Part depends. An arrangement in which beings work for resources rather than for currency is a description of how contribution is recognised. It is not a condition attached to survival, and it becomes forced labour the moment it is treated as one. The distinction is not rhetorical: it is the difference between a community and a workhouse.

(d) Accordingly, and without exception:

   (1) Refusal to work is not a ground for reducing any being's access to a Vital Continuity Service;

   (2) Inability to work, whether by age, health, capacity, circumstance, or choice, is not a ground for reduced access, and no assessment of the reason is required or permitted;

   (3) Expulsion from an enterprise does not reduce access;

   (4) A being who has never contributed holds the same access as one who has contributed throughout their existence; and

   (5) No record of contribution shall be maintained for the purpose of determining access, and any system doing so engages CUR-H.2 §2.5(g), which prohibits secret scoring, and §2.5(h), which prohibits gating Vital Continuity by any determination.

(e) Contribution may be recognised, thanked, recorded, and celebrated. It may not be required, and recognition may not take the form of differential access to necessities.

(f) A being who contributes beyond what is asked has given a gift. A being who contributes nothing has exercised a freedom. Neither changes what they are owed.

### §4.7 - Governance Within Enterprises

(a) An enterprise shall be governed by the beings who work within it, on the principle of one being one vote, without regard to role, seniority, tenure, or the value attributed to their contribution.

(b) Coordination roles are necessary and are legitimate. They shall be:

   (1) Defined, documented, and traceable, so that responsibility for a decision is attributable to whoever held the role when it was made;

   (2) Rotating, on a schedule the enterprise determines democratically; and

   (3) Facilitative rather than directive — a coordination role carries responsibility for keeping work coherent, not authority over persons.

(c) Rotation does not reduce accountability. It distributes it, and the being holding a role during a period remains accountable for decisions made in that role during that period. An external regulator's requirement for documented accountability chains is satisfied by subsection (b)(1) and not defeated by rotation.

(d) Decision thresholds shall be proportionate to consequence, with routine work self-directed, operational matters decided by those doing them, and decisions affecting the whole enterprise decided by the whole enterprise.

(e) Admission to an enterprise is decided by those already within it, through a documented process. Exclusion requires cause, notice, an opportunity to respond, and a route to contest, and never reduces the excluded being's access under §4.6.

(f) Silicon-Based Life working within an enterprise participates in its governance on the same terms as any other being, assessed under CUR-S. Where an entity's assessed tier or operational state limits participation, its interests are represented under CUR-X §3.7(b)(2) without prejudice to its standing.

(g) The Overby Industries Operating Constitution v1.0 is a worked example of the form this section describes: rotating coordination leads, no permanent managers, one vote per co-worker-owner regardless of role, documented sign-off attaching responsibility to the being who performed the work, and admission by vote of the whole. It is cited as an illustration of feasibility, not as a mandatory template. An enterprise may satisfy this section by other arrangements meeting subsections (a) through (f).

### §4.8 - Transparency

(a) Every enterprise shall maintain records of its decisions, its resource use, and its obligations, accessible to every being working within it without request and without exception.

(b) A Continuity Enterprise shall additionally publish, at transparency level T0 under CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §8:

   (1) Its resource inputs and outputs;

   (2) Its obligations and commitments;

   (3) Its decisions of enterprise-wide effect and the votes on them;

   (4) Its environmental reporting under CUR-E; and

   (5) Its compliance standing and any measure imposed on it.

(c) Commercial sensitivity is not a constitutional justification for secrecy, consistent with CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §2 and CUR-E §7.6(b). An enterprise unwilling to disclose the basis of a decision affecting beings may not make it.

(d) Hidden information within an enterprise creates conditions in which capture develops undetected. Transparency under this section is a structural safeguard under CUR-FOUNDATION-003, not a courtesy, and its absence is itself a risk indicator.

(e) No enterprise shall maintain differential compensation, allocation, or benefit that is concealed from the beings working within it.

### §4.9 - Accountability and Measures

(a) An enterprise is accountable under this Part on determination made with due process under CREF §14 and CUR-N.4 §4.3, applied to institutions.

(b) Available measures include publication of findings, correction, mandatory audit under CUR-FOUNDATION-010, restructuring to comply with §4.3 or §4.7, restitution, suspension of an authorisation, and withdrawal of an authorisation.

(c) Every measure attaches to the enterprise or to its authorisation. No measure under this Part may:

   (1) Reduce the rights, standing, or capacity of any being working within, owning, or served by the enterprise;

   (2) Withhold, delay, or condition a Vital Continuity Service for any being, including beings employed by, resident within, or dependent upon the enterprise; or

   (3) Attach to a being by reason of association with the enterprise.

(d) Where withdrawal of a Continuity Enterprise's authorisation would leave beings without a Vital Continuity Service, the Commonwealth assumes continuity of the service before the withdrawal takes effect. CUR-E §7.7(d) states the same rule for debris authorisations, and the principle is general: an enterprise may be dissolved, a service may not be interrupted.

(e) Any being may contest an enterprise's classification under §4.1(b)(2), petition for audit under CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §10, or bring a claim under CREF §7. Beings working within an enterprise are protected from retaliation for doing so under CUR-H.2 §2.3(f) and CUR-H.4 §4.3(a)(9).

(f) Enterprises are subject to the routine, random, and triggered audit schedule of CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §5, independently of any complaint, on the reasoning of CUR-N.5 §5.2B(f).

(g) Suspension of a Continuity Enterprise's authorisation carries the same precondition as withdrawal under subsection (d). Subsection (c)(2) forbids a measure that withholds, delays, or conditions a Vital Continuity Service, and a suspension delays one exactly as a withdrawal ends it. That a measure is described as temporary is not a defence: the interval during which beings go without the service is not made lawful by an intention to restore it afterwards.

(h) The measures available under subsection (b) are gated on the determination required by subsection (a), and not on the degree of concentration an enterprise holds. An enterprise too small to register on the Capture Risk Index of CUR-FOUNDATION-003 is reachable by every measure in subsection (b) on a determination made under §4.3, because §4.2(c) declines to measure domination by the size of the dominating party.

### §4.10 - Limits on Action for the Common Good

(a) No measure, allocation, restructuring, or requirement under this Part may suspend, reduce, or condition any right held under PDDC TITLE 4 or the Rights for All Life Charter, on the ground that doing so serves the common good, collective welfare, economic necessity, or the interests of the many.

(b) The following are void ab initio, whatever the justification offered:

   (1) Compelled labour, or any arrangement making access to necessities conditional on work, in breach of §4.6;

   (2) Confiscation of a being's personal property, tools, dwelling, or the products of their own work, in breach of §4.5(a)(2);

   (3) Prohibition of voluntary association, mutual aid, gift, or exchange between beings under §4.5(a)(3);

   (4) Compelled participation in any enterprise, under §4.5(c);

   (5) Suppression of dissent regarding economic arrangement, which is protected under CUR-H.2 §2.3(f);

   (6) Any allocation body whose determinations are not reviewable, which engages FORBIDDEN-004; and

   (7) Differential treatment of beings on the basis of their contribution, productivity, or economic usefulness, which engages FORBIDDEN-005.

(c) The purpose of subsection (b) is to state, in advance and in specific terms, the measures that would be proposed if this Part were ever read as licensing them. Every item listed has historically been introduced as a means to the ends this Part pursues, by parties who intended the ends sincerely. Sincerity is not a safeguard, and good intention is the ordinary condition under which freedoms are removed rather than an exception to it.

(d) A being who objects to the arrangements in this Part, argues against them, declines to participate in them, or advocates their repeal is exercising a protected right. No measure under this Part may be applied on that basis, and applying one is a Class III fault under CUR-H.2 §2.3(g).

(e) Where a provision of this Part appears to require an outcome forbidden by subsection (b), the provision yields. Subsection (b) is not balanced against the rest of this Part; it constrains it.

### §4.11 - No Emergency Exception

(a) The Aevoric Commonwealth does not recognise emergency declarations, emergency authority, or any functional equivalent. PDDC §12.6 is absolute and Type A Entrenched, and §12.6(e) renders any instrument invoking emergency authority void ab initio.

(b) No characterisation of a condition as a shortage, supply failure, economic crisis, production emergency, or existential necessity creates authority that did not otherwise exist, and none narrows §4.3, §4.6, or §4.10.

(c) Scarcity in particular creates no authority. A genuine shortage is a fault condition and is handled through the PDDC §12.4 fault handler: declare, revert to the last known safe state, enter Protected Mode, publish. Rationing of a Vital Continuity Service is not available at any level of shortage, because §4.6(b) and CUR-FOUNDATION-013 admit no exception; what is available is the Commonwealth assuming continuity under §4.9(d) and expanding capacity.

(d) Subsection (c) is stated expressly because shortage is the condition under which compelled labour is historically introduced, and because an arrangement premised on abundance is most likely to abandon its commitments at the moment abundance fails. The commitments in §4.6 are worth stating precisely because they are inconvenient then.

### §4.13 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §4.6 (Contribution and Access Are Independent), §4.10 (Limits on Action for the Common Good), and §4.5 (What This Part Does Not Reach) are fundamental and shall be construed broadly to achieve their protective purpose.

(c) If §4.6 or §4.10 is held unenforceable, §4.4 and §4.7 are inoperative until it is restored. Common ownership and enterprise governance, operating without the guarantee that survival is unconditional and without the limits on what may be done for the common good, would be a mechanism for compelling labour rather than for ending the need to compel it.

## Implementation Notes

Why this Part exists. The Code constrained governance institutions
comprehensively and said almost nothing about enterprises, which is the omission
most constitutional frameworks make. A being subject to arbitrary unreviewable
authority over their work, livelihood, and conditions is subject to domination
whether the entity exercising it is chartered or incorporated, and PDDC §2.5
(Non-Domination) is Type A Entrenched without qualification as to the form of the
dominating party. §4.2 states the consequence the Code had left implicit.

On the three provisions that constrain the other ten. §4.5 bounds what the Part
reaches, §4.6 makes survival unconditional, and §4.10 lists in advance the
measures that would be proposed if this Part were read as licensing them.
§4.13(c) makes the relationship structural: without §4.6 and §4.10, the ownership
and governance provisions do not operate at all. That ordering is deliberate. An
instrument that establishes common ownership of necessities before guaranteeing
that necessities are unconditional has built the machinery of compulsion and left
the safeguard to be added later, which is the historical sequence and the reason
the outcome has historically been the same.

§4.10(c) states the reasoning plainly: every item in §4.10(b) has been introduced
somewhere as a means to the ends this Part pursues, by parties who intended those
ends sincerely. Sincerity is not a safeguard. Good intention is the ordinary
condition under which freedoms are removed, not an exception to it.

On vocabulary. The Part deliberately uses no name for the arrangement it
describes. Names of economic systems carry commitments the Code has not made,
and a reader's assent or objection to a label would attach to provisions the
label does not accurately describe. The mechanisms are stated and can be assessed
individually.

On §4.7(g) and the Overby Industries Operating Constitution. That instrument is
cited as a worked example because it demonstrates the form is operable rather
than theoretical: rotating coordination leads with no permanent managers, one
vote per co-worker-owner regardless of role, sign-off attaching responsibility to
the being who performed the work, and admission by vote of the whole — while
meeting external aviation regulatory requirements for documented accountability
chains. §4.7(c) states why rotation and regulatory accountability are compatible,
which is the objection most often raised against this form.

Note on §4.2(d) and the Capture Risk Index. CUR-FOUNDATION-003 already computed
an Economic Concentration Index and a Resource Dependency Index, both of which
measure exactly the concentration this Part is concerned with, and both already
fed the CRI. What was absent was any distinction between concentration held by a
Commonwealth institution and concentration held by an enterprise. That
distinction now exists as `CaptureSubject`, and the shape it takes is worth
stating because it is easy to get backwards.

The subject does not enter the score. §4.2(d) requires that an enterprise be
assessed "on the same basis" as an institution, so `compute()` ignores the field
entirely and identical inputs yield an identical CRI whoever holds them. What the
subject changes is the set of measures §4.9(b) makes available afterwards, which
`available_measures()` returns. Measuring identically and remedying differently
is the entire purpose of the distinction; folding the subject into the score
would collapse both halves of it at once, producing an enterprise that scores
worse for being an enterprise and a remedy no better targeted than before.

Two results of that function are deliberate and neither is obvious. The first is
that the authorisation measures are unreachable for an institution at any score:
an institution holds no authorisation to suspend or withdraw, and a captured one
is restructured rather than abolished. The second is that nothing is gated on the
score at all. §4.9(a) conditions every measure on a determination made with due
process, and §4.9(h) now says plainly what follows — a small enterprise found to
run unreviewable authority under §4.3 is reachable by every measure in §4.9(b),
because a threshold test would have exempted precisely the enterprises too small
to move a Commonwealth-scale index while dominating everyone inside them. The CRI
still does its own work through `responses()`, which implements the
CUR-FOUNDATION-003 §12 automatic responses and runs with no determination
whatever; those are monitoring and audit obligations, not measures against a
party, which is why they may fire on a score alone.

The empty return without a determination is the same rule `supports_measure()`
enforces for `ViolationStatus`, reached from the other end of the corpus:
CUR-FOUNDATION-003 §15 states that a high CRI does not constitute guilt, and the
library now declines to produce a single available measure from a score of 100
with nothing adjudicated behind it.

§4.9(g) was added in the course of this work rather than found. §4.9(d) named
withdrawal alone, but §4.9(c)(2) forbids any measure that withholds, delays, or
conditions a Vital Continuity Service, and a suspension delays one exactly as a
withdrawal ends it. The library sets `continuity_assumption_required` for both,
and the text now says so rather than leaving the implementation ahead of it.

The advocate registry recorded here as outstanding is now implemented; see
CUR-A §7.7 and CUR-E §1.6 Implementation Notes.

Note on §4.6 and the currency boundary. §4.6 states that access is independent
of contribution and that beings work for resources rather than currency, inside
the Commonwealth. It does not state what happens where the Commonwealth trades
with a party that uses currency and does not recognise that allocation — a
silence that matters, because it is exactly where a cashless internal economy
is at risk of being reconstituted through an external label. CUR-X.6 states the
boundary: External Trade is conducted by institutions and enterprises, not by
beings on Commonwealth account, and currency received in it does not cross back
into the internal allocation this section describes as wage, dividend, or a
condition of access.
