## PART 3 - CROSS-DOMAIN CONFLICT RESOLUTION

### §3.1 - Scope

(a) This Part governs any matter in which the provisions of two or more CUR domains apply to the same act, entity, resource, or dispute, and in which the outcomes indicated by those provisions differ.

(b) The domains are CUR-H (Human), CUR-S (Silicon-Based Life), CUR-A (Animal), CUR-D (Deity), CUR-E (Ecosystem and Environment), CUR-N (Non-Human Cognitive Actors), and this Part, CUR-X (Cross-Domain).

(c) A matter engaging more than one domain is a cross-domain matter whether or not any party asserts that it is. The characterisation follows from the provisions engaged, not from how a party frames the claim.

(d) This Part does not create new substantive rights or obligations. It determines which existing provision governs where several speak at once, and by what procedure that determination is made.

(e) Where only one domain applies, that domain governs and this Part is not engaged.

### §3.2 - No Domain Hierarchy

(a) The domains are not ranked. No domain outranks another, and no provision of this Part may be construed to create a general order of precedence among them.

(b) Subsection (a) is required by CUR-FOUNDATION-002 §6 FORBIDDEN-005, which prohibits species-based governance privilege and provides that humans, silicon-based entities, animals, hybrids, and future recognised lifeforms hold equal constitutional standing. A domain hierarchy is a species hierarchy expressed procedurally. It is forbidden in either form.

(c) Accordingly, a conflict between domains is never resolved by asking which domain is more important. It is resolved by §3.3, which ranks principles rather than beings.

(d) An argument that one domain should prevail because of the nature, origin, capability, substrate, or claimed status of the beings it governs is void, and advancing it is an aggravating factor in any accountability proceeding arising from the matter.

### §3.3 - Order of Precedence

(a) Where provisions of two or more domains indicate different outcomes, the following order applies. A higher-numbered rule is consulted only where every rule above it leaves the matter unresolved.

   (1) Forbidden transitions. No outcome may render reachable any state forbidden under PDDC §12.3(a) or CUR-FOUNDATION-002 §6. An interpretation producing such an outcome is void ab initio and is not weighed against anything.

   (2) Vital Continuity. Where the matter concerns access to a Vital Continuity Service, §3.5 governs and restoration proceeds immediately, before any question of precedence is reached.

   (3) Rights invariants. Rights guaranteed under PDDC TITLE 4 and the Rights for All Life Charter are architectural invariants under PDDC §12.1(d). No resolution may reduce them, and PDDC §12.2(c) applies to any state transition arising from the resolution.

   (4) Consent. Where one provision requires consent and another would permit action without it, the provision requiring consent governs. Consent standards are those of CUR-N.2 §2.1(c): explicit, informed, ongoing, revocable, and non-coerced.

   (5) The more protective provision. Where two provisions protect the same interest to different extents, the more protective governs. Protection is assessed from the position of the being whose interest is at stake, not from the position of the party seeking to act.

   (6) The more specific provision. Where the provisions are equally protective, the provision addressing the situation more specifically governs.

   (7) Foundation documents. Where the matter remains unresolved, CUR-FOUNDATION-001 governs, consistent with PDDC §12.1(c).

(b) Rule (a)(5) resolves ties in favour of protection rather than in favour of action. Where it is genuinely unclear which provision is more protective, the matter is unresolved for the purposes of that rule and proceeds to (a)(6).

(c) Nothing in this section permits a party to select a domain in order to obtain a less protective outcome. Selection of forum or characterisation of a claim for that purpose is an abuse of process under §3.9(d).

### §3.4 - Concurrent Application

(a) Where the provisions of two or more domains apply and their outcomes do not conflict, all apply concurrently. §3.3 is not engaged.

(b) Compliance with one domain is not a defence to non-compliance with another. An act may satisfy CUR-E and violate CUR-N, and the violation stands.

(c) Where an act violates provisions in more than one domain, accountability under each is cumulative, subject to the proportionality requirement in §3.9(c).

(d) CUR-N.2 §2.9 states this rule for the Non-Human Cognitive Actors domain. Subsection (c) generalises it.

### §3.5 - Vital Continuity Precedence

(a) Where a cross-domain matter concerns the interruption, degradation, depletion, or withholding of a Vital Continuity Service, restoration of that service precedes every other consideration in this Part, including the determination of which domain governs.

(b) Restoration is not deferred pending resolution of the conflict, determination of fault, identification of the responsible party, or resource accounting. CUR-FOUNDATION-013 is explicit that restoration takes precedence over all of these.

(c) The affected domain enters STATE-010 Vital Continuity Response under CUR-FOUNDATION-002 §3. The cross-domain determination proceeds afterwards, under §3.6, and is not prejudiced by the order.

(d) No resolution under this Part may withhold a Vital Continuity Service from any recognised lifeform, in any compliance state, for any reason. Withholding is a Class IV fault under CREF §6 and a forbidden transition regardless of the outcome of the underlying dispute.

(e) Where the dispute is itself about which party bears the cost of restoration, restoration proceeds and the cost question is resolved afterwards. The Commonwealth assumes continuity in the interim.

### §3.6 - Procedure

(a) A cross-domain matter is initiated by any recognised entity through the Constitutional Complaint and Review Process under CREF §7, submitted through a CAPS-compliant message under CUR-FOUNDATION-006.

(b) On validation, the matter is routed under CREF §8 to Constitutional Rights Review, to Vital Continuity Response under §3.5, or to rejection. Routing to Vital Continuity Response does not close the rights review; both proceed.

(c) The reviewing body shall identify, on the record and before reaching the merits:

   (1) Each domain engaged and the specific provisions relied upon;

   (2) Whether the outcomes indicated actually conflict, or whether §3.4 applies; and

   (3) Where they conflict, which rule of §3.3 resolves the matter.

(d) A determination that does not identify the resolving rule under §3.3 is incomplete and may be returned for completion on that ground alone.

(e) Every determination under this Part is a constitutional event under CUR-FOUNDATION-005 and is logged with the actor, the domains engaged, the resolving rule, and the outcome, as required by CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §14.

### §3.7 - Standing and Representation

(a) Any being whose interests are affected by a cross-domain matter has standing, without regard to domain, substrate, or capacity to appear.

(b) Where a being cannot represent itself in the forum, representation shall be provided. This applies in particular to:

   (1) Animals, represented through advocates under CUR-A;

   (2) Silicon-Based Life whose operational state limits participation, without prejudice to its standing; and

   (3) Ecosystems and environmental interests, represented under CUR-E.

(c) Inability to appear is never a ground for excluding an interest from consideration. A being that cannot speak in the forum is not thereby without interests, and the forum shall not proceed as though it were.

(d) Representation shall not be treated as diminishing the represented being's standing, and a representative may not consent on the represented being's behalf to anything §3.3(a)(4) requires that being's own consent for.

### §3.8 - Interim Measures

(a) Pending resolution, the reviewing body may order measures to prevent irreversible harm.

(b) Interim measures are protective actions under CREF §10. They shall be limited to the minimum scope necessary, and shall not suspend constitutional rights, expand any authority beyond its ordinary mandate, create emergency powers, or bypass due process.

(c) The Commonwealth recognises no emergency authority in cross-domain matters or in any other matter. PDDC §12.6 is absolute and Type A Entrenched. Urgency changes the sequence of steps under §3.5; it never changes who holds authority.

(d) Interim measures are reviewed at intervals no greater than thirty (30) days and lapse when the matter is resolved.

(e) An interim measure that has the effect of accomplishing the outcome sought on the merits is not an interim measure, and is void.

### §3.9 - Outcomes and Remedies

(a) A determination under this Part states which provision governs and why. It does not amend, disapply, or repeal the provision that did not govern, which remains in force for all other purposes.

(b) Remedies are those available under CREF §12: restoration, correction, compensation, reinstatement, public disclosure, and institutional reform. Consistent with CREF §12, restoration of rights and continuity precedes assignment of fault wherever reasonably possible.

(c) Where accountability arises under more than one domain, the aggregate shall be proportionate to the conduct. Cumulative accountability under §3.4(c) is not an occasion for compounding sanctions beyond proportion.

(d) Selecting a forum, characterising a claim, or invoking a domain in order to obtain a less protective outcome is an abuse of process. It is a Class II fault, and where it succeeds in reducing another being's protection, a Class III fault.

(e) Determinations under this Part are appealable under CREF §15. No determination is final against a being that has not had the opportunity to be heard, directly or through representation under §3.7.

### §3.10 - Relationship to Other Domains

| Domain | Relationship |
|---|---|
| CUR-H | Human interests carry no priority by virtue of being human. §3.2(d) applies to arguments framed on that basis. |
| CUR-S | Where a matter concerns an entity's continuity of state or decommissioning, CUR-S §4.5 and §4.7 apply and §3.5 governs the continuity question. |
| CUR-A | Representation under §3.7(b)(1) is the ordinary route for animal interests. Inability to appear does not reduce standing. |
| CUR-D | A claim of divine, creator, or moral authority confers no precedence under this Part and is void under CUR-N.2 §2.2(b). |
| CUR-E | Environmental interests are represented under §3.7(b)(3). Debris and continuity interactions are governed by CUR-E §7.3. |
| CUR-N | Multi-domain telepathic matters are routed here by CUR-N.2 §2.7, and the consent standard of CUR-N.2 §2.1(c) is adopted by §3.3(a)(4). |

### §3.11 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §3.2 (No Domain Hierarchy), §3.3(a)(1) (Forbidden transitions), and §3.5 (Vital Continuity Precedence) are fundamental and shall be construed broadly to achieve their protective purpose.

Note on §3.3 and `libcur`. The library composes regulations by union: where
several apply to an event, every guard mask must hold, which is always the more
protective reading and is consistent with §3.3(a)(5). It does not implement the
full precedence order, because it evaluates a single regulation set rather than
adjudicating between domains. Modelling §3.3 properly would require the set to
carry domain provenance per rule and to resolve conflicts at evaluation time.
That is a substantial change and is deliberately deferred rather than
half-implemented.
