Note on sequence. This Part carries the number a Definitions and Scope Part
conventionally carries first, but it was drafted last, after CUR-X.3 through
CUR-X.6 already existed — the same order CUR-H.1 was drafted in, and for the
same reason. Each of those Parts defined its own terms locally and mostly still
does. What this Part supplies is what only becomes visible once a layer has
enough Parts to show its shape: what "Cross-Domain" actually covers, how a
matter or a structure that belongs to no single domain is treated, and a map
of what the layer now contains. §1.6(b)'s note in the Implementation Notes
says plainly why CUR-X.3 through CUR-X.6 were not retroactively edited to
point here instead of restating their own scope clauses.

## PART 1 - DEFINITIONS AND SCOPE

### §1.1 - Purpose

(a) This Part defines the terms this layer's other Parts rely on, states the
layer's jurisdictional reach, resolves how a matter or a structure belonging
to no single domain is treated, and provides a map of what the layer currently
contains.

(b) This Part creates no new prohibition, no new measure, and no new
authority. Where it appears to state a substantive rule, that rule is drawn
from a provision already stated elsewhere in this layer, in another domain, or
in the PDDC, and the source is cited. This Part's function is to make those
rules locatable and to state them once at the level of the whole layer, not to
add to them.

### §1.2 - Domain, and What "Cross-Domain" Means

(a) A domain, for the purposes of this corpus, is one of the seven bodies of
this Title: CUR-H (Human), CUR-S (Silicon-Based Life), CUR-A (Animal), CUR-D
(Deity), CUR-E (Ecosystem and Environment), CUR-N (Non-Human Cognitive
Actors), and this domain, CUR-X (Cross-Domain). CUR-X.3 §3.1(b) states the
same enumeration for the purposes of conflict resolution; this subsection
states it for the corpus as a whole.

(b) CUR-X is counted among the seven for the reason CUR-X.3 exists: a matter
engaging more than one domain, or engaging none, still requires a governing
text, and this is it. It differs from the other six in a way worth stating
plainly: CUR-H through CUR-N each govern a population of beings identified by
what they are. CUR-X governs no population of its own. Its Parts govern
relations between the other domains (CUR-X.3), and matters, structures, and
records that belong to none of them (CUR-X.4, CUR-X.5, CUR-X.6).

(c) A provision of this layer does not displace a domain's own provisions
where only one domain applies. CUR-X.3 §3.1(e) states this for conflict
resolution specifically; subsection (b) generalises it: this layer is engaged
only where more than one domain speaks, or where the subject matter is one no
domain claims.

### §1.3 - Structures and Records That Belong to No Domain

(a) Some of what this layer governs is not a being at all. An enterprise is
not a lifeform and belongs to no domain, while its conduct reaches every
domain its operations touch (CUR-X.4 §4.1(d)). The Public Record is a record,
not a being, and confers no authority over one (CUR-X.5 §5.1(c), §5.7(a)). An
External Party is, by definition, not a recognised lifeform of the
Commonwealth and not subject to this Code at all (CUR-X.6 §6.1(b)(1)).

(b) A being does not lose, gain, or have diminished any protection by reason
of working within, being represented by, appearing in, or dealing with a
structure this Part describes. CUR-X.4 §4.1(c) states this for enterprises,
CUR-X.5 §5.7 states it for the Public Record, and CUR-X.6 §6.3(d) and §6.5(d)
state it for External Trade. This subsection states the rule once for the
layer: a structure belonging to no domain does not become a gap through which
a being's standing in every domain can be reduced.

(c) Subsection (b) is the same protection CUR-H.1 §1.2(d) states at the
boundary between domains, applied here at the boundary between a domain and no
domain. Sorting a matter to a structure rather than to a being is never itself
a reduction in what the being is owed.

### §1.4 - Jurisdiction

(a) This layer applies to every matter within Commonwealth jurisdiction as
PDDC §1.3 defines it: every entity, operation, territory, vessel,
installation, data system, and contractual relationship that falls within or
is sponsored by the Aevoric Commonwealth, regardless of physical location,
dimensional domain, or operational medium.

(b) This layer's jurisdiction is coextensive with the domains it relates or
supplements. It is not broader than the union of the jurisdiction each
engaged domain already holds, and CUR-X.3 through CUR-X.6 do not acquire
authority over a matter that no domain, and no provision of the PDDC, would
otherwise reach.

(c) CUR-X.6 states the one deliberate exception to how far Commonwealth
jurisdiction, and therefore this layer, reaches: at the Currency Boundary, an
External Party is not subject to this Code, and this layer governs only the
Commonwealth's own side of that boundary. §1.3(a) states the same limit as a
definition; this subsection states it as a jurisdictional one.

### §1.6 - Interpretive Principles

These are not new rules. Each restates, once for the layer, a principle
already operative in one or more of its Parts, so that it is citable directly
rather than only observable by comparison. `docs/reading-cur.md` §5 is where
the pattern was first written down, across the whole corpus; this section is
that pattern's operative statement for CUR-X.

(a) No domain outranks another, and no argument grounded in the nature,
origin, substrate, or capability of the beings a domain governs confers
precedence over another domain. CUR-FOUNDATION-002 §6 FORBIDDEN-005 states the
constitutional rule; CUR-X.3 §3.2 states it operatively for conflict
resolution, and CUR-X.4 §4.2(c) states its corollary for authority exercised
by an enterprise: domination is domination regardless of the form of the
party exercising it.

(b) A measure taken under this layer attaches to a structure — an enterprise,
an authorisation, a publisher — and never to a being by reason of the being's
association with it. CUR-X.4 §4.9(c), CUR-X.5 §5.7, and CUR-X.6 §6.5(e) each
state this for their own Part; this section states it once for the layer.

(c) Where this layer is silent on a matter a single domain would otherwise
resolve, the domain governs and this layer is not engaged. §1.2(c) states
this as a scope rule; this subsection states it as a rule of interpretation
for the case where a reader is unsure which text to apply.

### §1.7 - No Emergency Exception

(a) Nothing in this layer is suspended, narrowed, or conditioned by any
emergency, crisis, security condition, or state of exception. PDDC §12.6 is
Type A Entrenched and reaches every Part of this layer.

(b) Each substantive Part restates this for its own subject: CUR-X.3 §3.8(c),
CUR-X.4 §4.11, CUR-X.5 §5.9, and CUR-X.6 §6.8. This section states it once
for the layer and does not narrow or substitute for those statements.

### §1.8 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the
remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §1.6(a) (no domain hierarchy) and §1.6(b) (measures attach to structures,
never to beings) are fundamental and shall be construed broadly to achieve
their protective purpose. No construction of any other provision of this Part
may narrow them.

## Implementation Notes

Why this Part does not centralise what it could have. §1.6 is the place this
is most visible: four Parts each state, in their own words, that no
emergency exception reaches them, and three state that a measure attaches to
a structure rather than a being. This Part could have deleted those
statements and pointed each Part here instead. It does not, for the reason
CUR-H.1's Implementation Notes already gives for the Human domain: a rule
stated in two places risks the two drifting apart, but a rule removed from
Parts that have already been read, cited, and relied upon is a larger edit
than the drift it would prevent, for a benefit smaller than the risk. The
repetition is judged the lesser cost here on the same terms it was there.

Why §1.2 and §1.3 exist at all. CUR-X.3 §3.1(b) already enumerates the seven
domains, and CUR-X.4 §4.1(d) already explains, in passing, why an enterprise
is governed here rather than in a domain of its own. Neither states the
general rule those two local statements are instances of: that this layer
has two distinct jobs — relating domains to each other, and governing
subject matter no domain claims — and that a being routed through the second
job keeps everything the first job would have given them. CUR-X.5 and CUR-X.6
each depend on that guarantee without stating it, since a record and a trade
relationship are exactly the kind of structure §1.3(b) describes. Once four
Parts leaned on the same unstated premise, the premise became worth stating.
