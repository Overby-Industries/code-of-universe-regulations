Numbering note. No existing citation pins a Part number for this domain — the
corpus cites `CUR-A` as a whole. Part 7 is chosen to align with RFAL Animal
Kingdom Bill Article 7, which carries the substantive content most often cited:
protection from cognitive harm. Representation is drafted here rather than in a
separate Part because every citation to CUR-A depends on it, and because for a
being that cannot appear, a protection and the means of asserting it are not
separable.

## PART 7 - COGNITIVE HARM, REPRESENTATION, AND STANDING

### §7.1 - Scope and Definitions

(a) This Part governs psychological and cognitive harm to animals, the standing of animals in Commonwealth proceedings, and the appointment and conduct of those who represent them.

(b) For the purposes of this Part:

   (1) Animal means any non-human biological organism assessed at Tier 1 or above under §7.2, excluding Silicon-Based Life, which is governed by CUR-S, and plant systems, which are governed by CUR-E and the RFAL Plant Life Bill of Rights.

   (2) Cognitive Harm means injury to an animal's mental or experiential state, including chronic fear, boredom, loneliness, distress arising from unpredictability or absence of control, learned helplessness, and the frustration of species-typical behaviour.

   (3) Advocate means a being appointed under §7.7 to represent an animal's interests in a proceeding or governance process.

   (4) Human Care means any circumstance in which an animal's access to food, water, shelter, space, social contact, or veterinary attention depends on the acts or omissions of another being.

(c) Cognitive harm is assessed from the position of the animal, by reference to the species-typical capacities and needs of that animal, and not by reference to human experience or to what a human observer would find distressing.

(d) This Part protects animals. It does not imply that interior protection is a human characteristic, and CUR-H.2 §2.10 states the same rule from the human side.

### §7.2 - Tier Determination

(a) Tier assessment follows the RFAL Animal Kingdom Bill tier model and the RFAL Tier Assessment Protocol. This Part adopts both without modification.

(b) Any animal with a centralised nervous system and observable pain-response behaviour is presumed Tier 2 unless demonstrated otherwise. The burden of proof for withholding consideration falls on the assessor, never on the animal or its advocate.

(c) Where an animal's tier is unknown, disputed, or cannot be established, it is treated as Tier 2 for the purposes of this Part until an independent assessment concludes otherwise.

(d) Self-assessment by a party with a commercial, research, or operational interest in the animal is not sufficient to classify it below Tier 2.

(e) The tiers in subsection (a) are moral-status tiers. Uncertainty resolves upward toward protection, consistent with RFAL Tier Assessment Protocol §2.4, because a higher tier means greater protection and confers no authority on anyone. They are not the engagement tiers of RFAL Deity Bill §1.2, the ecological standing tiers of the RFAL Plant Life Bill §2.2, or the coercive practice tiers of CUR-N.5 §5.2A.

(f) A tier determination reducing an animal's protections takes effect only after independent review and after any contest by an advocate has been resolved.

(g) Tier classifications are revised as scientific understanding develops. A classification is a working hypothesis about the animal, not a property of it.

### §7.3 - Protection from Cognitive Harm

(a) No animal shall be subjected to conditions causing cognitive harm, including:

   (1) Social isolation of a social species;

   (2) Confinement preventing species-typical movement, foraging, exploration, or behaviour;

   (3) Environments inducing chronic fear, boredom, or learned helplessness;

   (4) Unpredictable aversive conditions over which the animal has no control; and

   (5) Absence of enrichment appropriate to the species where the animal is in human care.

(b) Solitary confinement of great apes, cetaceans, elephants, corvids, parrots, and other highly social cognitively complex species is prohibited. Where separation is necessary for the animal's own welfare or that of others, it shall be the minimum duration necessary, monitored, and reviewed at intervals no greater than seven (7) days.

(c) A party holding an animal in human care shall provide social housing for social species unless contraindicated by a documented welfare assessment, environmental complexity permitting species-typical behaviour, and enrichment appropriate to the species.

(d) Physical health is not a defence to a claim under this section. An animal may be physically sound and cognitively harmed, and the absence of injury or disease is not evidence that no harm occurred.

(e) Young animals shall be permitted to develop under conditions meeting their developmental needs, including maternal care, peer socialisation, play, and gradual weaning. Premature separation from mothers for human convenience is prohibited, per RFAL Animal Bill Article 8.

(f) This section applies in full to Tier 3 animals and to Tier 2 animals to the extent the species-typical capacities engaged are present.

### §7.4 - Parity with Psychological Harm

(a) Cognitive harm to an animal is harm within the meaning of the Code. It is not a lesser category of injury and is not established only where physical injury accompanies it.

(b) CUR-H.4 §4.2 states this principle for human beings. It applies to animals on the same terms, and the difference between the two Parts is one of assessment method, not of the seriousness of the injury.

(c) Sustained harm is assessed cumulatively. A course of conduct is evaluated as a whole, and a party may not defeat a claim by decomposing it into individually tolerable parts.

(d) Intent is not an element. Intent, malice, and recklessness are aggravating factors. Conduct undertaken for a purpose the actor considered benevolent is not thereby permitted where the harm was foreseeable.

### §7.5 - Non-Consensual Access to Animal Cognition

(a) Telepathic invasion of an animal assessed at Tier 2 or above is prohibited to the same extent as invasion of any other conscious being, and CUR-N.2 applies in full.

(b) An animal cannot give consent satisfying CUR-N.2 §2.1(c), which requires that consent be explicit, informed, ongoing, and revocable. It follows that no telepathic access to an animal is consented access, and the exception in CUR-N.2 §2.5 is the only circumstance in which such access is permissible.

(c) An advocate cannot consent on an animal's behalf to telepathic access. CUR-X §3.7(d) so provides, and §7.8(c) states it for this domain.

(d) Non-consensual cognitive modification of an animal, including conditioning designed to induce learned helplessness, is a forbidden transition under PDDC §12.3(a)(4).

(e) Enforcement proceeds through advocates under §7.7, as CUR-N.2 §2.7 provides.

### §7.6 - Standing

(a) An animal whose interests are affected by a proceeding or governance decision has standing, without regard to its capacity to appear, communicate, or comprehend the proceeding.

(b) Inability to appear is never a ground for excluding an interest from consideration. CUR-X §3.7(c) states this generally; it applies here without qualification.

(c) Standing is not conditional on an advocate having been appointed. Where an animal has standing and no advocate has been appointed, the proceeding does not continue on the merits until one is.

(d) Standing extends to a species or population as a collective interest where the matter concerns species-level rights, habitat, or evolutionary autonomy under RFAL Animal Bill Article 6.

(e) A determination affecting an animal that had standing and was not represented is voidable on that ground alone, on application by any being.

### §7.7 - Advocates

(a) Where an animal has standing under §7.6, an advocate shall be appointed to represent its interests.

(b) An advocate shall have demonstrated expertise in animal welfare, ethology, and the specific needs of the species in question.

(c) An advocate shall not:

   (1) Be employed by, contracted to, funded by, or otherwise dependent on any party whose conduct is in question in the proceeding;

   (2) Hold a commercial, research, or operational interest in the animal or in the outcome; or

   (3) Represent both an animal and a party whose interests are adverse to it.

(d) Subsection (c) is a disqualification, not a factor to be weighed. An appointment made in breach of it is void, and any determination reached with such an advocate is voidable under §7.6(e).

(e) The advocate's duty is to the animal's interests as the animal would have them, assessed by reference to species-typical needs and to the individual animal's observable preferences, condition, and history. It is not a duty to the appointing body, to the party holding the animal, or to any wider interest.

(f) Where an advocate's assessment of the animal's interests conflicts with the preference of the party holding the animal, the advocate's duty is unaffected.

(g) An advocate shall have access to the animal, to the conditions in which it is held, and to records concerning it, sufficient to form an independent assessment. Denial of such access is a Class II fault and grounds for adverse inference.

(h) Appointment, identity, expertise, and any declared interest of an advocate are recorded and published under §7.12.

### §7.8 - Limits on Advocacy

(a) An advocate represents an animal's interests. The appointment confers no authority over the animal, no ownership of it, and no power to dispose of it.

(b) Representation shall not be treated as diminishing the animal's standing, consistent with CUR-X §3.7(d).

(c) An advocate may not consent on an animal's behalf to anything for which the Code requires that being's own consent, including telepathic access under §7.5(c), non-therapeutic modification, or any procedure whose justification depends on consent rather than on the animal's welfare.

(d) Where a matter turns on consent the animal cannot give, the matter is decided on the animal's welfare and interests, and not by treating an advocate's agreement as a substitute for consent.

(e) An advocate may not waive a protection this Part confers, agree to a reduction of the animal's tier, or accept a measure that §7.9 forbids.

(f) An advocate acting in breach of this section may be removed on application by any being, and the determination reached is voidable under §7.6(e).

### §7.9 - Vital Continuity and Animals in Human Care

(a) Food, water, shelter, veterinary care, and sanitation for an animal in human care are Vital Continuity Services under CUR-FOUNDATION-013.

(b) They shall not be withheld, reduced, delayed, or conditioned as a sanction, as a means of training, as leverage in any dispute, or during any investigation, audit, appeal, or administrative process.

(c) Withholding is a Class IV fault under CREF §6 and a forbidden transition regardless of any other circumstance. Interruption or degradation generates a constitutional event and enters STATE-010 Vital Continuity Response under CUR-FOUNDATION-002 §3.

(d) An animal in human care is wholly dependent on the party holding it. That dependency is the reason subsection (b) admits no exception: the animal has no alternative source, cannot seek one, and cannot report the deprivation.

(e) Deprivation of food or water as a method of training, control, or research protocol is prohibited absolutely and is not rendered permissible by any scientific, commercial, or husbandry purpose.

(f) Where a party can no longer provide subsection (a) services, the obligation is to transfer the animal to a party that can. It is never to withhold.

### §7.10 - Accurate Representation

(a) No animal or species shall be systematically misrepresented, stereotyped, or denied accurate characterisation in a manner facilitating exploitation or diminishing moral consideration.

(b) Animals shall be described in terms of their actual cognitive and emotional capacities, mythologised neither as unfeeling automata nor as human-like proxies.

(c) A characterisation advanced in a proceeding that materially understates a species' assessed capacities is a misrepresentation to the forum and is a Class II fault.

(d) Where evidence of a species' capacities is contested, the tier default in §7.2(b) governs the proceeding pending resolution.

### §7.11 - No Emergency Exception

(a) The Aevoric Commonwealth does not recognise emergency declarations, emergency authority, or any functional equivalent. PDDC §12.6 is absolute and Type A Entrenched, and §12.6(e) renders any instrument invoking emergency authority void ab initio.

(b) No characterisation of a condition as an outbreak, infestation, overpopulation, biosecurity emergency, or crisis creates authority that did not otherwise exist, and none narrows §7.3 or §7.9.

(c) Culling is not an emergency measure and is not authorised by this Part. Where population management is genuinely necessary, RFAL Animal Bill Article 4.2 requires habitat expansion to be preferred over culling, and the burden of demonstrating that no non-lethal alternative exists rests on the party proposing lethal measures.

(d) Where action is genuinely required to prevent imminent serious harm, the available instruments are protective actions under CREF §10, limited to the minimum scope necessary, and the fault handler of PDDC §12.4. A protective action shall not extend to withholding a Vital Continuity Service under §7.9, which remains prohibited in every circumstance.

(e) Subsection (b) is stated in specific terms because emergency framing applied to animal populations is both historically common and rarely examined. A disease outbreak is a fault condition and is handled as one: detect, contain by the least harmful available means, publish, and review.

### §7.12 - Records and Audit

(a) Every tier determination, advocate appointment, cognitive harm finding, and measure under this Part is a constitutional event under CUR-FOUNDATION-005 and is logged as required by CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §14.

(b) Records shall include the animal or population affected, the assessed tier, the advocate appointed and any declared interest, the ground relied upon, and the outcome.

(c) Conditions in which animals are held in human care are auditable under CUR-FOUNDATION-010, and an audit may be triggered by an advocate, by a report from any being, or by routine and random scheduling under CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §5.

(d) A determination that is not recorded did not lawfully occur.

(e) Aggregate data on tier determinations, findings, and conditions is published to the Public Transparency Registry under CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §9.

### §7.14 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §7.6 (Standing), §7.7(c) and §7.7(d) (advocate disqualification), §7.8(a) and §7.8(c) (limits on advocacy), and §7.9 (Vital Continuity) are fundamental and shall be construed broadly to achieve their protective purpose.

(c) If §7.7(c) is held unenforceable, §7.7 is inoperative until it is restored. An advocacy regime without conflict-of-interest disqualification does not represent animals; it supplies the appearance of representation to parties whose conduct is in question, which is worse than acknowledging that the animal is unrepresented.

Why representation is drafted here. Four of the eight citations to CUR-A are
citations to its advocacy provisions rather than to its substantive protections:
CUR-X §3.7(b)(1), CUR-H.2 §2.10, CUR-H.4 §4.12, and CUR-N.4 §4.13 all route to
CUR-A for how an animal's interest is asserted rather than for what it is. For a
being that cannot appear, a protection and the means of asserting it are not
separable, and drafting them in different Parts would have left every one of
those citations pointing at half an answer.

The conflict-of-interest rule. §7.7(c) is the provision most likely to be
resisted in practice, because the parties with the relevant species expertise are
frequently the parties holding the animals. That is precisely why it is a
disqualification rather than a factor, and why §7.14(c) makes §7.7 inoperative
without it. An advocacy regime that permits the party under examination to supply
the advocate produces determinations that look represented and are not, which is
a worse outcome than an acknowledged absence of representation, because it
forecloses the objection.

Additions beyond RFAL. §7.9 has no counterpart in the Animal Kingdom Bill. It is
required by CUR-FOUNDATION-013 and by the constraint in `titles/README.md` that
no title may permit a Vital Continuity Service to be withheld in any compliance
state. §7.9(e) reaches deprivation of food or water as a training or research
method, which is the form the prohibition most often needs to take. §7.11 is
likewise an addition; emergency framing applied to animal populations is common
and rarely examined, and PDDC §12.6 admits no exception for it.

Note on `DOMAIN_ANIMAL` and §7.7. The baseline regulation set carries no other
animal-domain rules, and most of this Part is not FSM-shaped: cognitive harm is
assessed from conditions over time, which the library has no representation of.
§7.7 was the exception, and it is now implemented. Whether an advocate is
dependent on a party to the proceeding is a property of the appointment, not of
an interior state, so it can be checked where the appointment is made.

The shape it took is worth recording, because §7.7(d) determined it. Since the
disqualifications in §7.7(c) void an appointment rather than weigh against it,
`AdvocateRegistry::appoint()` refuses. It returns no warning, no score, and no
appointment flagged for review — a void appointment is one that never existed,
and declining to create the record is the closest a library can come to that.
Three of the four disqualifications are declarations the library cannot verify
and does not pretend to; recording them is what makes them falsifiable, so that a
funding relationship surfacing later becomes grounds for `void_appointment()`
rather than merely an embarrassment. The fourth, §7.7(c)(3), is checked against
the register, because whether an advocate already acts for a party to the same
proceeding is something the register knows.

A voided appointment is marked and kept rather than deleted. §7.7(d) makes
determinations reached with a disqualified advocate voidable under §7.6(e), and
finding those determinations afterwards requires the appointment still be on the
record — the same reasoning CREF §15 gives for `VS_OVERTURNED` existing instead
of a violation record simply being removed.

`guard::ADVOCATE_CLEARED` requires the advocate to be named as well as cleared.
That is not ceremony. An advocate who is nobody in particular cannot be checked
against §7.7(c) afterwards, cannot be published under §7.7(h), and cannot be held
to the duty §7.7(e) places on them personally — so an unnamed advocate leaves the
guard unsatisfied, on the same reading that makes an undeclared life-support
floor unsatisfiable under CUR-E.2 §2.2(c).

CUR-E §1.6 is implemented by the same registry rather than a parallel one,
because §1.6(d) states expressly that it is the same rule for the same reason.
Two registries would let it drift apart in one domain and not the other. The one
provision that differs is §1.6(b)'s consultation of a stewarding people, which
applies to `ADOM_ENVIRONMENTAL` alone.

What remains unmodelled is §7.7(e) and §7.7(f) — the content of the advocate's
duty, and its independence from the preference of the party holding the animal.
Those are substantive rather than structural, and the library does not represent
what an advocate concluded, only that an eligible one was appointed and had
access.
