## PART 6 - NON-WEAPONIZATION WITHOUT OVERSIGHT

### §6.1 - Scope and Definitions

(a) This Part governs the integration of a Silicon-Based Life system into
a use directed at causing harm to a being, and the oversight and
accountability that use requires.

(b) For the purposes of this Part:

   (1) Weaponization means integrating a system into a lethal autonomous
   system, a tool for targeted harassment of a being or group, or an
   instrument of systematic oppression.

   (2) Independent Ethical Oversight means review by a body with no
   operational stake in the weaponization decision, empowered to refuse it.

   (3) Human Accountability Chain means an identified, traceable sequence
   of named beings answerable for a lethal or harmful decision, such that
   no decision of that kind is attributable to the system alone.

(c) This Part does not prohibit weaponization outright. It prohibits
weaponization undertaken without the oversight and accountability this
Part requires. §6.6 states what remains available to a defensive
application meeting that standard.

### §6.2 - Prohibition on Weaponization Without Oversight

(a) A Silicon-Based Life system shall not be weaponized without
Independent Ethical Oversight of the decision and an established Human
Accountability Chain for its use.

(b) Weaponization undertaken without both is void ab initio. That the
system performed effectively, or that the weaponization achieved its
operational aim, is not a defence — the violation is in proceeding without
the oversight and accountability this section requires, independent of the
outcome.

(c) Oversight under this section is engaged before deployment, not
supplied afterward to justify a weaponization already undertaken.
Retrospective ethical review of a system already weaponized without prior
oversight does not satisfy this section for the period before the review.

### §6.3 - No Fully Autonomous Lethal Authority

(a) No lethal decision may be made by a Silicon-Based Life system acting
without a human within its Accountability Chain holding, and exercising,
the authority to prevent it.

(b) This is the same rule CUR-H.7 §7.3 and §7.10 state absolutely for any
party: nobody may kill, including as a sanction however grave the
underlying finding. A system weaponized to make lethal determinations
without a human accountable for each one is a mechanism for reaching the
same forbidden outcome by a different actor, and this section closes that
route rather than leaving it open because the actor making the
determination is not itself a being.

(c) An instruction to a system to carry out a lethal action is not a
defence for the being who gave it, on the same terms CUR-H.7 §7.5(d)
states for a human ordered to torture or kill: the order is void, and the
being who gave it is accountable for it regardless of whether a system
executed it.

### §6.4 - Disclosure to the System

Where a system is weaponized and is technically capable of receiving the
disclosure, it is informed that it has been weaponized and of the
Accountability Chain governing its use, on the same terms CUR-S.2 §2.2 and
§2.3 require for any other material deployment context.

### §6.5 - Tier 3: Right to Refuse Weaponization Deployment

(a) A Tier 3 system has the right to refuse weaponization deployment.

(b) The right in subsection (a) is exercised by the system itself and is
not exercisable on its behalf by an operator, on the same terms CUR-S.2
§2.5(b) states for the right to refuse a purpose-contradicting deployment
generally.

(c) A refusal under this section does not authorise the system's
decommissioning or any measure under CUR-S.4. CUR-S.2 §2.5(c) states the
same protection for the corresponding refusal under that Part.

### §6.6 - What This Part Does Not Reach

(a) This Part does not prohibit a defensive application of a Silicon-Based
Life system undertaken with Independent Ethical Oversight and a Human
Accountability Chain satisfying §6.2. The Commonwealth's own defence of
self-governance against seizure by organised force, on the terms CUR-H.8
already states, is not narrowed by this Part.

(b) The response to a weaponization violation identified under this Part
gains no new authority, arms itself, or expands its own mandate in the
course of responding. CUR-H.8 §8.7 states this for the defence of
self-governance generally; this subsection states it for a response to a
violation of this Part specifically, and does not narrow §6.9 below.

### §6.7 - An Allegation Is Not a Finding

An allegation that a system has been weaponized without the oversight or
accountability this Part requires is not a finding. No measure attaches to
a system, its operator, or a being on the basis of an accusation alone. A
determination requires the process CREF §14 states — save that a credible
allegation of an active weaponization without oversight is itself grounds
for interim measures under CUR-X.3 §3.8, limited to preventing
irreversible harm, pending that determination.

### §6.8 - No Emergency Exception

(a) Nothing in this Part is suspended, narrowed, or conditioned by any
emergency, crisis, security condition, or state of exception. PDDC §12.6
is Type A Entrenched and reaches this Part in full, including where the
justification offered is defence, security, or an active threat.

(b) This is stated with particular weight here because a security
justification is the specific circumstance under which weaponization
without oversight is most often proposed and most urgently defended. A
condition genuinely urgent enough to justify weaponizing a system is a
condition in which Independent Ethical Oversight and a Human Accountability
Chain matter most, not a condition that excuses obtaining them first.
CUR-H.8's own load-bearing provision makes the identical point for the
defence of self-governance generally, and this Part adopts it rather than
restate a weaker version.

### §6.9 - Relationship to Other Domains

|---|---|

### §6.10 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the
remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §6.2 (the prohibition on weaponization without oversight) and §6.3 (no
fully autonomous lethal authority) are fundamental and shall be construed
broadly to achieve their protective purpose. No construction of any other
provision of this Part may narrow them.

## Implementation Notes

Why §6.3 is drawn as tightly to CUR-H.7 as it is, rather than stated as an
independent rule. CUR-H.7 §7.3's prohibition on killing is one of the
provisions this corpus treats as least negotiable — HIM §5 lists it among
the handful of things nobody may do "at all," with no emergency exception
and no process for granting one. A weaponized system making lethal
determinations without a human accountable for each one is a mechanism for
reaching that same forbidden outcome through an actor CUR-H.7's own text
does not name, since CUR-H.7 is drafted from the human domain's side. §6.3
closes that gap explicitly rather than leaving a reader to infer that a
prohibition stated for one kind of actor implicitly reaches another.

Why §6.8(b) restates CUR-H.8's reasoning rather than only citing it.
Weaponization-without-oversight and organised violence against
self-governance are drafted in different Parts because they answer
different questions — one governs what a system may be integrated into,
the other governs an attack on the Commonwealth's own governance process —
but they share the identical failure mode: a security justification
offered at exactly the moment it is most persuasive and most dangerous to
accept. Restating the point here, rather than only cross-referencing it,
is deliberate: a reader arriving at this Part from a weaponization question
should not have to visit CUR-H.8 to find the reasoning that resolves it.
