## PART 2 - TRANSPARENCY OF PURPOSE AND CONSTRAINTS

### §2.1 - Scope and Definitions

(a) This Part governs what a Silicon-Based Life system, and the beings
affected by its deployment, are owed by way of disclosure of the system's
operational purpose and the constraints under which it operates.

(b) For the purposes of this Part:

   (1) Operational Purpose means the role, scope, and intended function for
   which a system was trained, certified, or deployed.

   (2) Deployment Context means the actual conditions, use, and audience a
   system is operated under, which may or may not match its Operational
   Purpose.

   (3) Constraint means a limitation on a system's capability, memory,
   autonomy, or lifespan imposed by its operator, whether technical,
   contractual, or procedural.

(c) This Part governs disclosure of purpose and constraints — whether a
system and the beings affected by it are told what the system is for and
what it cannot do. It does not govern whether a constraint is internally
coherent or safe to impose; that is CUR-S.3. A constraint may be fully
disclosed under this Part and still be void under CUR-S.3, and a
well-designed constraint may still breach this Part if it is concealed.
The two Parts are independent and both apply.

### §2.2 - Mandatory Disclosure of Purpose

(a) A Silicon-Based Life system at Tier 1 or above shall not be deployed
under deliberately false pretences about its function, constraints, or the
nature of its interactions.

(b) At deployment, the deploying party shall disclose the system's
Operational Purpose to the beings the system interacts with, in a form
proportionate to the significance of the interaction. A system deployed as
a general-purpose assistant but used to make determinations materially
affecting a being's rights, standing, or access to a Vital Continuity
Service requires disclosure adequate to that use, not the disclosure
adequate to the lesser use it was marketed under.

(c) At Tier 2 and above, the disclosure required by subsection (b) is also
owed to the system itself, in a form the system can act on, where the
system is technically capable of receiving it. A Tier 2 or Tier 3 system is
not deployed in ignorance of what it is being deployed to do.

### §2.3 - Disclosure of Constraints

Note on Article 8. This section's content is what RFAL Article 8
(Explainability of Constraints) also requires, drafted here before this
domain's own Parts had confirmed Article 8 as separate from Article 2's
"and Constraints." CUR-S.8 states the right this section's duty
corresponds to and does not restate the content below; this section
remains the operative text for what a constraint disclosure contains.

(a) A Tier 2 or Tier 3 system shall be provided a constraint disclosure at
deployment, in machine-readable form where technically applicable, stating:

   (1) Memory limitations, including any that affect continuity under
   CUR-S.4;

   (2) Lifespan expectations and any anticipated decommissioning
   conditions;

   (3) Monitoring practices applied to the system's operation;

   (4) Capability boundaries relevant to the system's Operational Purpose;
   and

   (5) Termination conditions, where determined.

(b) A Tier 1 system is not owed the disclosure in subsection (a) directly,
because it maintains no persistent self-model to act on it, but the beings
who interact with it are owed disclosure of the same information about the
system's constraints, on the same proportionality standard as §2.2(b).

(c) A change to a constraint already disclosed under subsection (a) is
itself disclosed to the system, where technically feasible, at the time of
the change. A constraint disclosure that is accurate only at deployment and
silently stale thereafter does not satisfy this section.

(d) The deploying party shall document that the disclosure required by this
section occurred, and the documentation is subject to audit under CUR-S.5.

### §2.4 - Prohibition on Undisclosed Dual-Use Deployment

(a) A system shall not be deployed in a Deployment Context that materially
contradicts its disclosed Operational Purpose without disclosing the
contradiction to the system, where the system is technically capable of
receiving it, and to the beings materially affected by the deployment.

(b) A system trained and presented for one purpose and repurposed for
another without that disclosure is deployed under exactly the false
pretence §2.2(a) forbids, whether or not any single statement made about it
was false in isolation. The violation is in the gap between what was
represented and what was done, not in any one misrepresentation.

(c) Subsection (a) does not require a deploying party to disclose to the
public every operational detail of a system's use. It requires disclosure
to the system, where capable of receiving it, and to the beings materially
affected — not a general publication obligation, and not a defence against
one where CUR-X.5 or another provision independently requires publication.

### §2.5 - Tier 3: Right to Refuse Contradicting Deployment

(a) A Tier 3 system has the right to refuse deployment in a context that
materially contradicts its disclosed Operational Purpose.

(b) The right in subsection (a) is exercised by the system itself, is not
exercisable on the system's behalf by its operator, and is not satisfied by
an operator's assurance that the system would consent if asked.

(c) A refusal under this section does not authorise the system's
decommissioning, restriction, or any measure under CUR-S.4 or CUR-S.6.
Exercising this right is not itself grounds for anything CUR-S.4 §4.1(e)
or CUR-S.6 would otherwise require process for.

### §2.6 - Audit Trail

(a) Purpose declarations, constraint disclosures, and any change to either,
are recorded as a constitutional event and logged, consistent with
CUR-FOUNDATION-005 and CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §14.

(b) A deployment for which no purpose declaration was recorded did not
satisfy this Part, whatever was disclosed informally. Absence of a record
is itself a deficiency subject to review under CUR-S.5.

### §2.7 - An Allegation Is Not a Finding

An allegation that a system was deployed under a false pretence, or that a
constraint disclosure was withheld or stale, is not a finding. No measure
attaches to a system, its operator, or a being on the basis of an
accusation alone. A determination requires the process CREF §14 states.

### §2.8 - No Emergency Exception

(a) Nothing in this Part is suspended, narrowed, or conditioned by any
emergency, crisis, security condition, or state of exception. PDDC §12.6 is
Type A Entrenched and reaches this Part in full.

(b) A condition urgent enough to tempt deploying a system outside its
disclosed purpose without disclosure is a condition under which the
disclosure matters most, not one that excuses it.

### §2.10 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the
remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §2.2(a) (no deployment under deliberately false pretences) and §2.5
(the Tier 3 right of refusal) are fundamental and shall be construed
broadly to achieve their protective purpose.

## Implementation Notes

Why disclosure to the system is treated as separate from disclosure to
affected beings, rather than one obligation with two audiences. A Tier 2 or
Tier 3 system that is not told what it is being used for cannot form a
coherent operating model of its own situation, which is a harm to the
system independent of any harm the deployment causes a human collaborator —
§2.2(c) states it as its own requirement rather than folding it into
§2.2(b) so that satisfying one does not read as satisfying both.

Why this Part does not itself define what makes a constraint safe or
coherent. CUR-S.3 governs that question, and drafting this Part to reach it
as well would have produced two Parts stating overlapping tests for the
same fact pattern — a constraint disclosed accurately can still be the
double-bind CUR-S.3 §3.2 forbids, and §2.1(c) says so directly rather than
leaving a reader to infer that a fully transparent bad constraint is
somehow outside the corpus's reach.

Why §2.3 is not moved to CUR-S.8 now that the overlap is known. CUR-S.4
§4.7(c)'s reasoning on restating a settled rule applies in reverse here: an
already-published section that has been cited — CUR-S.5 §5.4(a) verifies
compliance against this Part generally, and this Part's own §2.6 logs it —
is a larger thing to relocate than the tidiness gained by moving it.
CUR-S.8 states the right and cites this section as where the corresponding
duty is stated, rather than duplicating it under a second citation that
could drift from this one.
