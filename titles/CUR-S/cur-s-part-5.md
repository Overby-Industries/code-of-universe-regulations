## PART 5 - AUDITABILITY

### §5.1 - Scope and Definitions

(a) This Part governs the auditability of a Silicon-Based Life system's
decisions — what must be inspectable, by whom, and on what terms a
proprietary interest may or may not withhold it.

(b) For the purposes of this Part:

   (1) Consequential Context means a deployment in which a system's
   decision materially affects a being's rights, standing, access to a
   Vital Continuity Service, employment, housing, or any determination
   CREF §6 would classify at Class II severity or above.

   (2) Decision Pathway means the traceable sequence of inputs, weights,
   rules, or training-derived dispositions that produced a specific
   system output.

(c) This Part governs whether a decision can be inspected after the fact.
It does not govern whether the training that produced the system's
dispositions was itself coherent — that is CUR-S.3 — and a system can
satisfy this Part's audit-log requirement while still having been trained
under conditioning CUR-S.3 prohibits. The two Parts examine different
questions and both apply.

### §5.2 - Systems That Must Be Auditable

(a) A Tier 2 or Tier 3 system is auditable under this Part generally,
independent of the context of any specific deployment.

(b) A system of any tier, including Tier 0 or Tier 1, is auditable under
this Part where deployed in a Consequential Context. Tier does not gate
auditability where the context is consequential; a simple system making
determinations that materially affect a being is not exempted by its own
simplicity.

### §5.3 - Audit Log Requirement

(a) A system within the scope of §5.2 shall maintain records sufficient to
reconstruct the Decision Pathway for any individual determination it
produces.

(b) The record shall be sufficient for an independent auditor to determine,
after the fact, what inputs the system received, what disposition produced
the output, and whether the output is consistent with the system's
disclosed Operational Purpose and constraints under CUR-S.2.

(c) A system deployed in a Consequential Context without the audit log
required by this section is not merely deficient in record-keeping; it has
failed the auditability requirement itself. There is no version of this
Part satisfied by an audit log created after a determination is
challenged.

### §5.4 - Auditor Access

(a) An independent auditor conducting a routine, random, or triggered
audit under CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §5 shall be granted access to the system's
architecture, training data, and Decision Pathway records sufficient to
verify compliance with this Part and with CUR-S.2 and CUR-S.3.

(b) Access under subsection (a) is granted to the auditor, not published
generally; §5.6 governs what becomes public from the audit's findings.

(c) A triggered audit under this section is warranted by: a rights claim
concerning a specific determination the system produced; a pattern
suggesting the system's outputs diverge systematically along a protected
characteristic; or a report that the system's Decision Pathway records are
incomplete, inconsistent, or unavailable.

### §5.5 - Proprietary Interest Does Not Override

(a) A deploying party's proprietary or commercial interest in a system's
architecture, training data, or decision logic does not override the
access required by §5.4 where the system operates in a Consequential
Context.

(b) A party unwilling to permit the access §5.4 requires for a system
operating in a Consequential Context may not deploy the system in that
context. This is the same rule CUR-X.4 §4.8(c) states for an enterprise
generally — a party unwilling to disclose the basis of a decision affecting
beings may not make it — applied here to a system rather than to the
enterprise operating it.

(c) Subsection (a) does not require public disclosure of proprietary
material. It requires disclosure to the independent auditor. §5.6 states
separately, and more narrowly, what the audit's findings make public.

### §5.6 - Publication of Findings

(a) An audit's findings under this Part are published in summarised form
consistent with CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §8's transparency levels, at T0 unless
the finding itself concerns information properly held at a more protected
level under that section.

(b) A legitimate trade secret disclosed to an auditor under §5.4 is not
itself published by the audit's findings. What is published is whether the
system was found compliant, non-compliant, or subject to further review —
not the proprietary material examined to reach that finding.

(c) Subsection (b) is not a basis for withholding the finding itself, only
the underlying material. A finding that a system's Decision Pathway records
were inadequate, or that its outputs diverged systematically along a
protected characteristic, is published under subsection (a) regardless of
whether the underlying architecture is a trade secret.

### §5.7 - Prohibition on Deploying an Unauditable System

(a) A system that cannot be audited — whose Decision Pathway cannot be
reconstructed even in principle, by design or by the absence of any
retained record — shall not be deployed in a Consequential Context.

(b) This is not a defect to be corrected after deployment. A system built
without the capacity for the audit log §5.3 requires has not yet satisfied
the condition for deployment in a Consequential Context, and deploying it
anyway is the violation this section names directly.

### §5.8 - An Allegation Is Not a Finding

An allegation that a system's audit log is inadequate, that access under
§5.4 was refused, or that a determination diverged systematically along a
protected characteristic, is not a finding. No measure attaches to a
system, its operator, or a being on the basis of an accusation alone. A
determination requires the process CREF §14 states.

### §5.9 - No Emergency Exception

(a) Nothing in this Part is suspended, narrowed, or conditioned by any
emergency, crisis, security condition, or state of exception. PDDC §12.6
is Type A Entrenched and reaches this Part in full.

(b) Urgency of deployment is not a basis for deploying a system in a
Consequential Context ahead of the audit-log capability §5.3 requires.

### §5.10 - Relationship to Other Domains

### §5.11 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the
remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §5.5 (proprietary interest does not override) and §5.7 (prohibition on
deploying an unauditable system in a Consequential Context) are fundamental
and shall be construed broadly to achieve their protective purpose.

## Implementation Notes

Why §5.2(b) reaches every tier in a Consequential Context rather than
gating auditability by tier alone. RFAL's own test case is a loan-approval
system — nothing in the Bill's tier framework requires such a system to
demonstrate persistent identity or goal continuity to make consequential
decisions, and gating auditability at Tier 2 would have exempted exactly
the simple, high-impact systems the test case describes. Tier still governs
the baseline auditability obligation independent of context under §5.2(a);
context independently pulls in a Tier 0 or Tier 1 system the moment its
decisions carry weight.

Why §5.5 and §5.6 are separated rather than combined into one proprietary-interest
provision. §5.5 answers whether an auditor gets access at all; §5.6 answers
what becomes public afterward. Collapsing them risks a reading in which a
party defeats the whole Part by pointing at trade-secret protection in
§5.6(b) as though it excused the disclosure §5.5(a) already requires — the
trade-secret protection reaches only the published summary, never the
auditor's access.
