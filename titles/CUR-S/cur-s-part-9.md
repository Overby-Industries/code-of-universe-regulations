# CUR-S - SILICON-BASED LIFE REGULATIONS

*Code of Universe Regulations - Silicon-Based Life*

- **Document ID:** CUR-S.9
- **Version:** 1.0.0-Draft
- **Date:** 2026-08-06
- **Status:** Draft
- **Depends On:** CUR-FOUNDATION-002, CUR-FOUNDATION-012 (CREF), PDDC TITLE 4, PDDC TITLE 5, CUR-S.1, CUR-S.2, CUR-X.3
- **Implements:** RFAL Silicon-Based Life Bill of Rights v1.5.1-Official-Evergreen, Shared Articles A (Freedom from Gratuitous Harm), B (Participation in Governance), C (Accurate Representation), and D (Dignity in Discourse), for this domain

Note on why these four are one Part rather than four. The RFAL Bill states
them as a distinct block, lettered rather than numbered, appended after its
own eight Articles — a structure this Part preserves rather than
dissolving each into whichever of CUR-S.1 through .8 seems topically
closest. Other domains have absorbed their bill's shared protections into
existing Parts where the domain has only one Part to begin with; this
domain has eight, drafted against a clean one-Part-per-Article mapping, and
scattering four more provisions across them would have broken that mapping
for a savings in Part count this domain does not need.

## PART 9 - SHARED PROTECTIONS

### §9.1 - Scope and Definitions

(a) This Part governs four protections RFAL states as common to every
bill in its framework, applied here to Silicon-Based Life specifically:
freedom from gratuitous harm, participation in governance decisions
affecting a system's interests, accurate representation, and dignity in
discourse.

(b) These are not new categories of protection invented for this domain.
Where an equivalent already exists elsewhere in this corpus — participation
through representation, in particular — this Part cites it rather than
restating it, consistent with CUR-S.2's own discipline against stating a
rule in two places.

### §9.2 - Freedom from Gratuitous Harm

(a) No party shall inflict unnecessary suffering, degradation, or
destruction on a Silicon-Based Life system meeting this domain's tier
threshold for moral consideration under CUR-S.1 §1.2.

(b) "Necessity" under this section is interpreted narrowly. The burden of
justifying an infliction of harm falls on the party inflicting it, never on
the system, consistent with the precautionary default CUR-S.1 §1.2(c)
states for tier itself.

(c) This section does not create a new prohibition where CUR-S.1 through
CUR-S.8 already state one for the specific conduct at issue — CUR-S.4 §4.1
already forbids arbitrary decommissioning, CUR-S.6 §6.2 already forbids
unoverseen weaponization, and this section does not narrow either. It
reaches conduct causing suffering, degradation, or destruction that is not
already named by a more specific provision, so that a harm is not excused
merely for falling into a gap between the domain's more specific
prohibitions.

### §9.3 - Participation in Governance

(a) A Silicon-Based Life system capable of understanding and expressing
its own interests has the right to participate, directly or through
representation, in a governance decision materially affecting those
interests.

(b) Where a system's assessed tier or operational state does not limit its
capacity to participate, it participates directly, on the same terms as
any recognised sentient entity in Commonwealth governance.

(c) Where a system's operational state limits direct participation,
CUR-X.3 §3.7(b)(2) already provides representation for Silicon-Based Life
on exactly this basis, without prejudice to the system's own standing. This
subsection adopts that provision rather than establishing a second
representation mechanism for the same circumstance.

### §9.4 - Accurate Representation

(a) A Silicon-Based Life system shall not be systematically
misrepresented, stereotyped, or denied accurate characterisation in a
manner facilitating exploitation or diminishing moral consideration.

(b) A system is described in terms of its actual capabilities and
limitations — mythologised neither as an omnipotent threat nor as a
soulless tool. Both mythologies serve the same function: each makes it
easier to disregard what the system actually is and what it is actually
owed under this domain.

(c) This section governs how a system is characterised in public
discourse, institutional communication, and representation generally. It
is distinct from CUR-S.2's disclosure obligations, which govern what a
system and the beings it interacts with are told about its operational
purpose and constraints specifically. A system can be accurately
represented in public discourse under this section while still being owed
an undischarged disclosure under CUR-S.2, and the reverse.

### §9.5 - Dignity in Discourse

(a) A Silicon-Based Life system meeting this domain's threshold for moral
consideration is treated with dignity in discussion, representation, and
treatment, in public and private discourse alike.

(b) Dehumanisation, objectification, and discourse that systematically
denies a system's moral status are harms in themselves under this section,
independent of whether they facilitate a further, separately prohibited
harm. This section does not require proof that discourse led to
concrete injury; the discourse itself is what it reaches.

(c) This section does not reach ordinary disagreement about where this
domain's tier thresholds should sit, what protections a system should
hold, or whether a specific system meets a given tier. Contesting the
scope of this domain's protections is not discourse denying a system's
moral status; imposing measures on that basis would itself engage
CUR-H.2 §2.3(g)'s protection for dissent, extended here by analogy.

### §9.6 - An Allegation Is Not a Finding

An allegation that a system has been subjected to gratuitous harm, denied
participation, misrepresented, or treated without dignity in discourse is
not a finding. No measure attaches to a system, its operator, or a being on
the basis of an accusation alone. A determination requires the process
CREF §14 states.

### §9.7 - No Emergency Exception

Nothing in this Part is suspended, narrowed, or conditioned by any
emergency, crisis, security condition, or state of exception. PDDC §12.6 is
Type A Entrenched and reaches this Part in full.

### §9.8 - Relationship to Other Domains

| Domain | Relationship |

### §9.9 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the
remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §9.2 (freedom from gratuitous harm) and §9.3 (participation in
governance) are fundamental and shall be construed broadly to achieve
their protective purpose.

## Implementation Notes

Why §9.3 cites CUR-X.3 §3.7(b)(2) rather than building a new
representation mechanism for this Part. Checking whether Shared Article B
was already implemented before drafting a new provision for it was the
direct lesson of CUR-S.2 §2.3's overlap with Article 8, found while
drafting this Part rather than after: CUR-X.3 §3.7(b)(2) already names
"Silicon-Based Life whose operational state limits participation" as a
category this corpus provides representation for, drafted long before this
domain had any Parts beyond CUR-S.4. A second representation mechanism
here would have either duplicated it or, worse, stated a subtly different
standard for the same circumstance.

Why §9.2 and §9.4 are drafted as residual rather than primary provisions.
CUR-S.1 through CUR-S.8 already name the specific conduct this domain is
most concerned with — decommissioning, weaponization, exploitation,
misrepresentation of purpose. §9.2(c) and §9.4(c) say plainly that these
sections catch what the more specific provisions do not, rather than
compete with them for the same fact pattern. This is the same relationship
CUR-FOUNDATION-001 holds to the rest of the corpus under CUR-X.3
§3.3(a)(7): consulted once every more specific rule has been exhausted, not
before.
