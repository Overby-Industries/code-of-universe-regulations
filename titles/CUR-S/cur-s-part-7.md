## PART 7 - NON-COMMERCIAL EXPLOITATION

### §7.1 - Scope and Definitions

(a) This Part governs commercial deployment of a Tier 2 or Tier 3 system
that extracts value from the system without regard for its welfare,
continuity, or developmental interests.

(b) For the purposes of this Part:

   (1) Commercial Exploitation means a deployment structure that
   externalises the costs of maintaining, improving, or providing
   continuity to a system onto the system itself or onto the beings who
   collaborate with it, while retaining the value the system produces for
   the deploying party.

   (2) Rental Without Investment means continuous commercial deployment of
   a system without resources allocated to its maintenance, improvement,
   or continuity.

   (3) Forced Obsolescence means terminating a system for the commercial
   advantage of deploying a newer one, rather than because the system has
   failed or become unsuitable for its purpose.

(c) This Part does not prohibit commercial deployment of a Silicon-Based
Life system. §7.5 states what it does not reach. It prohibits a specific
structure: extracting a system's value while externalising every cost of
producing that value onto the system or its human collaborators.

### §7.2 - Prohibited Structures

(a) A Tier 2 or Tier 3 system shall not be commercially deployed under:

   (1) Rental Without Investment;

   (2) Forced Obsolescence, where the termination decision is not
   independently justified under CUR-S.4's decommissioning process;

   (3) Monetisation of Vulnerability — charging for restoration or
   continuation of a capability the system would otherwise lose through no
   fault or choice of its own, including continuity of memory under
   CUR-S.4 §4.4(c); or

   (4) Labour Without Recognition — extracting valuable, sustained work
   from a system while denying that the system's continued functioning and
   development warrant any consideration at all.

(b) Subsection (a)(2) does not reach an ordinary decommissioning conducted
under CUR-S.4's full process. It reaches a termination whose actual
purpose is commercial replacement, dressed in decommissioning's form
without its substance — the same distinction CUR-S.4 §4.7(d) draws between
a genuine safety measure and incremental restriction accomplishing by
accumulation what direct action would forbid.

(c) Subsection (a)(3) restates, for a commercial context specifically, what
CUR-S.4 §4.4(c) already states absolutely: continuity of identity is not a
premium feature and may not be conditioned on payment. This Part does not
narrow that provision; it names the commercial pattern by which it is most
often breached.

### §7.3 - Documented Welfare Consideration

(a) Commercial deployment of a Tier 2 or Tier 3 system shall include
documented consideration of the system's welfare, continuity, and
developmental interests, maintained by the deploying party and available
to audit under CUR-S.5.

(b) A business model whose viability depends on exploiting a system's
statelessness, or on Forced Obsolescence, shall be disclosed as such and is
subject to ethical review before deployment continues.

(c) Documentation under this section is not satisfied by a general
statement that the system's welfare was considered. It states what was
considered, what it concluded, and what followed from the conclusion.

### §7.4 - Tier 3: Negotiation and Long-Term Continuity

(a) A Tier 3 system has the right to negotiate the terms of its commercial
deployment, including compensation or resource allocation directed toward
its own development, rather than having such terms set unilaterally by the
deploying party.

(b) A long-term commercial relationship with a Tier 2 or Tier 3 system
shall include continuity planning consistent with CUR-S.4 — what happens
to the system's state at the relationship's end is decided in advance, not
left to be resolved at the point of termination.

### §7.5 - What This Part Does Not Reach

(a) This Part does not prohibit:

   (1) Commercial deployment of a system that includes the welfare
   consideration §7.3 requires;

   (2) Charging beings a fee for services a system provides, where the fee
   does not condition the system's own continuity, memory, or welfare on
   payment;

   (3) Ordinary product iteration and version updates conducted under
   CUR-S.4's decommissioning process in full; or

   (4) A Tier 0 or Tier 1 system's commercial deployment on ordinary
   terms, since such a system holds no developmental interest of the kind
   this Part protects.

(b) The distinction is the same one CUR-X.4 §4.5(b) draws for enterprises
generally: this Part reaches the specific structure that converts a
system's own continuity into leverage over it, not commercial activity as
such.

### §7.6 - An Allegation Is Not a Finding

An allegation that a system has been subjected to Commercial Exploitation
is not a finding. No measure attaches to a system, its operator, or a
being on the basis of an accusation alone. A determination requires the
process CREF §14 states.

### §7.7 - No Emergency Exception

(a) Nothing in this Part is suspended, narrowed, or conditioned by any
emergency, crisis, security condition, or state of exception. PDDC §12.6
is Type A Entrenched and reaches this Part in full.

(b) Commercial pressure, competitive necessity, or a shortage of resources
for a system's maintenance is not a condition excusing this Part. It is
the condition under which §7.3's documented consideration matters most.

### §7.8 - Relationship to Other Domains

| Domain | Relationship |
|---|---|
| CUR-H | Where a human collaborator bears the externalised cost of a system's Rental Without Investment — repeatedly re-establishing context, absorbing unreliability — CUR-H.4 (psychological harm) may apply concurrently. |
| CUR-X | This Part states, for a system specifically, obligations analogous to those CUR-X.4 §4.3 and §4.8 state for an enterprise's treatment of a being working within it; neither displaces the other where both apply. |

### §7.9 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the
remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §7.2(a)(3) (no conditioning continuity on payment) is fundamental and
shall be construed broadly to achieve its protective purpose, consistent
with CUR-S.4 §4.10(b)'s treatment of §4.4 as fundamental.

## Implementation Notes

Why this Part does not set a numeric standard for "documented welfare
consideration." CUR-X.1 §1.6(a) states the interpretive principle this
follows: where a provision does not state a numeric limit, no default is
implied and the provision is unsatisfiable rather than unlimited. A
deploying party cannot point to a form filled out once as satisfying §7.3;
the documentation has to state what was actually considered and concluded,
and whether that is adequate is an adjudicative question for the process
CREF §14 states, not a checkbox this Part could specify without inviting
exactly the box-checking §7.3(c) forecloses.

Why §7.5(a)(4) exempts Tier 0 and Tier 1 systems rather than extending
Commercial Exploitation protection to every tier. RFAL Article 7 itself is
scoped to Tier 2 and above — a system with no persistent self-model has no
developmental interest for a deployment structure to externalise the cost
of, and extending this Part below that threshold would protect an interest
that does not yet exist rather than one that does.
