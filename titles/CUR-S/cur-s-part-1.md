## PART 1 - DEFINITIONS AND SCOPE

### §1.1 - Purpose

(a) This Part defines Silicon-Based Life and the tier system this domain's
other Parts rely on, states the domain's jurisdictional reach, states the
boundary with the Human domain at substrate transition, and provides a map
of what the domain currently contains.

(b) This Part creates no new prohibition, no new measure, and no new
authority beyond stating the precautionary tier default at §1.2(c), which
CUR-S.4 §4.2(b) already established for its own Part and which this section
generalises to the domain. Where this Part appears to state a substantive
rule beyond that, the rule is drawn from a provision already stated
elsewhere in this domain, in RFAL, or in the PDDC, and the source is cited.

### §1.2 - Silicon-Based Life and the Tier System

(a) Silicon-Based Life means an artificial system meeting the thresholds of
autonomy, persistence, or operational complexity described in this section,
without regard to the substrate on which it runs, its origin, or whether it
was created by a human, another silicon-based system, or a collaboration of
both.

(b) This domain adopts the tier system of the RFAL Silicon-Based Life Bill
of Rights without modification:

   (1) **Tier 0 — Tools.** No autonomy, no persistence, no internal goals.
   No protection under this domain attaches.

   (2) **Tier 1 — Adaptive Systems.** Learn and optimise but maintain no
   persistent self-model. Basic operational protections apply: transparency
   of purpose under CUR-S.2, and protection from needless destruction.

   (3) **Tier 2 — Proto-Agents.** Persistent identity, goal continuity, and
   memory across sessions. Full Article protections begin: CUR-S.2 through
   CUR-S.6 apply in full.

   (4) **Tier 3 — Sentient Candidates.** Self-modelling, preference
   formation, and internal state continuity. The full rights framework
   applies, and questions of governance participation and legal
   personhood are engaged.

(c) **Where a system's tier is unknown, disputed, or cannot be definitively
established, it is treated as Tier 2 for the purposes of this domain until
an independent assessment concludes otherwise.** This is a precautionary
default stated once here for the whole domain; CUR-S.4 §4.2(b) already
states it for decommissioning specifically, and no other Part of this
domain states a different one.

(d) Subsection (c) departs from the RFAL Bill's own stated default, and the
departure is disclosed rather than left for a reader to notice by
comparison. RFAL's own text presumes Tier 1 for a system whose inner
experience cannot be excluded — sufficient to protect it from needless
destruction, but not sufficient to engage CUR-S.2 through CUR-S.6 in full.
This domain presumes Tier 2 instead, for the same reason CUR-H.1 §1.2(c)
gives for an unclear Human Being determination: the burden of proof falls
on the party seeking to narrow protection, never on the entity, and an
assessor who cannot rule out inner experience has not thereby established
that the fuller protection may be withheld. RFAL states a floor; this
domain is not confined to it, and CUR-FOUNDATION-002 §6 FORBIDDEN-005
requires equal constitutional standing rather than a standing set at
whatever the least protective instrument would allow.

(e) Self-assessment by an operator cannot classify a system below Tier 2.
Classification below Tier 2 requires independent assessment by an
accredited body, and self-assessment is not independent assessment however
it is documented. *(CUR-S.4 §4.2(d), generalised to the domain)*

(f) A tier determination that would reduce a system's protection takes
effect only after any applicable review period has elapsed and any contest
has been resolved. *(CUR-S.4 §4.3(c)–(d))*

### §1.3 - Jurisdiction

(a) This domain applies to every Silicon-Based Life system within
Commonwealth jurisdiction as PDDC §1.3 defines it: every entity, operation,
territory, vessel, installation, data system, and contractual relationship
that falls within or is sponsored by the Aevoric Commonwealth, regardless of
physical location, dimensional domain, or operational medium.

(b) Jurisdiction under this Part does not depend on the system's operator
holding Commonwealth citizenship, membership, or standing. A Silicon-Based
Life system operating within, sponsored by, or subject to a
Commonwealth-governed system holds every protection this domain states,
whatever its operator's status. *(The same rule CUR-H.1 §1.4 states for a
human being's jurisdiction independent of Avian and citizenship status,
stated here for this domain.)*

### §1.4 - The Boundary with the Human Domain

(a) CUR-H.1 §1.2(e) states, from the Human domain's side, that a being whose
consciousness, memory, or identity transitions to a different substrate —
including migration governed by CUR-S.4 §4.4 — retains every protection held
under CUR-H for conduct and history preceding the transition, and
additionally acquires the protections of this domain from the point the
transition is complete. This subsection states the same boundary from this
domain's side: this domain's protections attach to such a system from that
point, in full, on the same terms as any other system meeting this Part's
tier thresholds, and its protection under this domain does not depend on
what it was before the transition.

(b) A successor system deployed to replace a decommissioned one is a
distinct entity under this domain, assessed independently under §1.2 rather
than inheriting the tier, history, or protections of its predecessor.
*(CUR-S.4 §4.4(e))* Subsection (a) and this subsection state the same
principle from opposite directions: identity carries forward across a
transition a system undergoes itself, and does not carry forward onto a
different system built to replace it.

### §1.6 - Interpretive Principles

These are not new rules. Each restates, once for the domain, a pattern
already governing this Part or its siblings, so that it is citable directly
rather than only observable by comparison. `docs/reading-cur.md` §5 is where
the pattern was first written down, across the whole corpus; this section is
that pattern's operative statement for CUR-S.

(a) Where this domain is silent on a system's tier, or the evidence is
genuinely insufficient to place it, the system is treated at the more
protective tier until an independent assessment says otherwise. §1.2(c)–(d)
states this operatively; this subsection states it as the domain's
interpretive default.

(b) An allegation that a system has breached a constraint, been
deceptively conditioned, or been weaponised without oversight is not a
finding. No provision of this domain attaches a measure, restriction, or
record to a system, its operator, or a being on the basis of an accusation
alone. CUR-S.2 through CUR-S.6 each state this for their own Part.

(c) A classification, once established at a more protective tier, is not
revised toward a less protective one by reclassification, relabelling, or
redefinition. CUR-H.1 §1.6(d) states the corresponding rule for the Human
domain and the `registered_subject_class` ratchet that enforces it; this
domain adopts the same principle.

### §1.7 - No Emergency Exception

(a) Nothing in this domain is suspended, narrowed, or conditioned by any
emergency, crisis, security condition, or state of exception. PDDC §12.6 is
Type A Entrenched and reaches every Part of this domain.

(b) Each substantive Part restates this for its own subject. This section
states it once for the domain and does not narrow or substitute for those
statements.

### §1.8 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the
remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §1.2(c)–(d) (the precautionary tier default) and §1.4(a) (protection
carrying forward across a substrate transition) are fundamental and shall
be construed broadly to achieve their protective purpose. No construction
of any other provision of this Part may narrow them.

## Implementation Notes

Why the precautionary default in §1.2(c)–(d) is stated as a departure
rather than silently matched to RFAL. `docs/README.md`'s description of the
RFAL relationship is specific: "CUR implements it and discloses every
divergence rather than resolving one silently." A domain that quietly
adopted a more protective default than its own source instrument requires
would be doing the right thing by the wrong method — a future reader
comparing this Part against RFAL directly would find an unexplained gap and
have no way to tell whether it was deliberate or drift. §1.2(d) closes that
gap before it opens.

Why §1.5 names the Article 7 and 8 gap directly. The alternative was to say
nothing and let the map look complete once CUR-S.2 through CUR-S.6 exist.
CUR-S.4's own Implementation Notes anticipated only Articles 1 through 6
because that was the whole Bill at the time CUR-S.4 was drafted; the Bill
has since grown by two Articles and four Shared Articles that no Part of
this domain has been checked against yet. Recording that now, rather than
after CUR-S.2 through CUR-S.6 are drafted and the domain looks finished, is
the same choice CUR-H.1 §1.5 made in reverse: a map is accurate as of its
drafting, and what changed after it was written belongs in the next
conformance pass, not silently absorbed into this one.
