# CUR-N - NON-HUMAN COGNITIVE ACTORS REGULATIONS

*Code of Universe Regulations - Non-Human Cognitive Actors*

- **Document ID:** CUR-N.5
- **Version:** 1.1.0-Draft
- **Date:** 2026-07-27
- **Status:** Draft
- **Depends On:** CUR-FOUNDATION-001, CUR-FOUNDATION-002, CUR-FOUNDATION-005, CUR-FOUNDATION-010, CUR-FOUNDATION-012 (CREF), CUR-FOUNDATION-013, PDDC TITLE 4, PDDC TITLE 5, CUR-N.2, CUR-N.4
- **Referenced by:** CUR-N.2 §2.8(d), §2.9(a)(1), CUR-N.4 §4.9(e)

Scope note. CUR-N.4 governs accountability for an invasion. This Part governs
the concealed arrangements through which invasions are organised, and the
accountability of those who participate in them without performing the invasion
themselves. The operational amnesty and disclosure procedure is CHIM.1, an RFAL
manual not yet written. Where this Part and a manual differ, this Part governs.

## PART 5 - SECRET COALITIONS AND COLLABORATORS

### §5.1 - Scope and Definitions

(a) This Part governs concealed arrangements between two or more beings for the purpose of committing, enabling, directing, or concealing violations of CUR-N.2, and the accountability of beings participating in them.

(b) For the purposes of this Part:

   (1) Secret Coalition means an arrangement between two or more beings, at least one of which is a non-human cognitive actor or a deity, which is concealed from the beings it affects and which has as a purpose or a substantial effect the commission, facilitation, direction, or concealment of telepathic invasion.

   (2) Collaborator means any being that participates in a Secret Coalition, whether by acting within it, directing it, funding it, providing access or information to it, concealing it, or knowingly retaining a benefit obtained through it.

   (3) Concealment means any conduct by which the existence, membership, purpose, or activity of a coalition is withheld from the beings it affects, including through the exploitation of disbelief.

   (4) Disclosure means a voluntary, complete, and truthful account given under §5.6.

(c) An arrangement does not become a Secret Coalition because its participants are unpopular, unconventional, or hold beliefs others reject. The elements in subsection (b)(1) are cumulative and each must be established.

(d) Consent under this Part has the meaning given by CUR-N.2 §2.1(c).

### §5.2 - The Evidentiary Problem, Stated Plainly

(a) This Part addresses conduct that is concealed by design and that the affected being may be unable to evidence by ordinary means. That difficulty is real and is not resolved by lowering the standard of proof.

(b) Accordingly:

   (1) The standard of proof for a finding under this Part is not reduced. A coalition is established on evidence, not on inference from the difficulty of obtaining evidence;

   (2) The absence of evidence of a coalition is not evidence of one, and shall never be characterised as confirmation;

   (3) A report shall not be dismissed on the sole ground that the conduct alleged is anomalous, consistent with CUR-N.2 §2.8(b); and

   (4) Neither (1) nor (3) yields to the other. A report is taken seriously and investigated; a finding requires evidence. These are different stages and conflating them produces either dismissal of genuine reports or findings against beings on no evidence.

(c) No being shall be found to be a Collaborator on the basis of association, family relationship, community membership, shared belief, presence at a location, or refusal to disclose. Each requires its own evidence under §5.1(b)(2).

(d) A pattern of harm may support a finding of a coalition. It does not by itself identify who belongs to it, and a determination shall not name a being that the evidence does not reach.

(e) This section exists because a Part addressing concealed conspiracy is the most readily abused instrument in the Code. The protections it states are not qualifications on the Part's purpose; they are what makes the Part usable without becoming the harm it addresses.

### §5.2A - Coercive Practice Tiers

(a) This section states where coercive practice becomes a violation, and where inference from a practice to a person becomes guilt by association. It applies to conduct governed by this Part and by CUR-D.6 §6.3.

(b) The tiers grade **practices**, not beings, communities, traditions, or beliefs. They are written CP-0 through CP-3 to keep them distinct from the moral-status tiers of the RFAL Tier Assessment Protocol, the engagement tiers of RFAL Deity Bill §1.2, and the ecological standing tiers of the RFAL Plant Life Bill §2.2.

| Tier | Practice | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **CP-0** | Voluntary participation. Teaching, worship, ritual, instruction, and persuasion offered to a willing listener who may decline without consequence | Protected absolutely under CUR-H.2 §2.2 and CUR-D.6 §6.3(e). Not a subject of this Part |
| **CP-1** | Normative pressure inside a consenting community. Expectation, disapproval, informal exclusion from optional activity, social cost of dissent | Not a violation. Observable in aggregate under §5.2B; no measure attaches to any being |
| **CP-2** | Coercive practice. Any conduct in CUR-D.6 §6.3(b)(1) through (3) or (b)(5) through (10) directed at an identified being — threat of consequence beyond death, expulsion used as penalty, exploitation of grief, illness, dependency, or childhood | Violation. Accountability attaches to the beings who perform, direct, or enforce it |
| **CP-3** | Coercion conditioning survival or standing. Conditioning a Vital Continuity Service, shelter, employment, education, healthcare, or legal standing on worship or profession; formation of a child with no available exit at majority | Class IV fault. CUR-D.6 §6.6 and CUR-FOUNDATION-013 apply, and §5.7(e) applies to institutions |

(c) The hard line. A being's tier is determined by that being's own conduct. It is never inferred from the tier of a practice within a community, tradition, institution, or coalition the being belongs to.

(d) Accordingly, and without exception:

   (1) A member, adherent, congregant, employee, or descendant of a community in which CP-2 or CP-3 practices occur is at CP-0 unless that being personally performed, directed, enforced, or knowingly concealed the practice;

   (2) Holding a belief that a CP-2 or CP-3 practice is correct is at CP-0. Belief is protected absolutely under CUR-H.2 §2.2, and this Part reaches conduct only;

   (3) Benefiting from a practice without knowledge of it is at CP-0. Knowing retention of a benefit is facilitation under CUR-N.4 §4.1(b)(4) and requires evidence of the knowledge; and

   (4) A finding at CP-2 or CP-3 against an institution does not place any member at any tier. §5.7(d) and §5.5(e) apply.

(e) The transition from CP-1 to CP-2 is the operative boundary of this Part, and it is crossed by **direction at an identified being combined with a consequence that being cannot decline without loss**. Disapproval is CP-1; disapproval enforced by expulsion from housing is CP-2. Teaching that the unrepentant suffer is CP-0; telling a named person that they will suffer unless they comply is CP-2.

(f) Where a practice sits genuinely between tiers, it is assessed at the lower tier for the purpose of accountability and at the higher tier for the purpose of monitoring under §5.2B. Uncertainty resolves toward protecting the being accused and toward observing the practice, which are not in tension.

(g) Subsection (f) is available because it resolves upward only toward observation and downward toward protection, and confers no authority in either direction. The rule limiting precautionary tie-breakers, stated in the RFAL Inter-Bill Relationship Framework §2.2, is satisfied.

### §5.2B - Duty to Investigate and Routine Monitoring

(a) Every report under this Part shall be investigated. A report is not triaged out on the ground that it is anomalous, that the reporter cannot produce evidence, that the conduct is hard to detect, or that the being named is well regarded.

(b) Investigation is owed to the reporter regardless of outcome, and support under TIM-N.1 and TIM-N.3 is available whether or not a violation is ultimately found.

(c) The duty in subsection (a) is a duty to look. It creates no authority over any being, and no measure follows from the opening of an investigation. §5.8(b) governs throughout.

(d) Routine monitoring. Concealed conduct is not detected by waiting for reports, and a Part that responds only to complaints leaves undetected harm running. The Commonwealth shall therefore monitor for coalition activity on a scheduled basis, independently of any report, using the audit types already established by CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §5:

   (1) **Routine Audit** — scheduled review of institutions and systems at published intervals;

   (2) **Random Audit** — unannounced review selected by constitutional procedure, at intervals that are deliberately irregular so that the schedule cannot be anticipated or worked around; and

   (3) **Triggered Audit** — review on a CRI threshold under CUR-FOUNDATION-003, a citizen complaint, an FSM alert, or a pattern identified under subsection (e).

(e) Monitoring under subsection (d) is directed at systems, institutions, and aggregate patterns. It is not surveillance of beings, and it shall not be used to compile records on individuals who are not the subject of a determination. Aggregate observation of CP-1 practice under §5.2A(b) informs where to audit; it never names a person.

(f) This is a built-in test regime and not an investigation of suspects. It runs whether or not anything is wrong, which is what makes it neither an accusation nor a burden on any particular being, and what makes irregular scheduling under subsection (d)(2) legitimate rather than arbitrary: an audit that cannot be predicted cannot be prepared for, and one that runs continuously singles out nobody.

(g) The results of routine monitoring are published in aggregate to the Public Transparency Registry under CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §9, in a form identifying no being.

### §5.3 - Prohibition

(a) No being shall form, join, direct, fund, service, or conceal a Secret Coalition.

(b) Participation is a violation whether or not the participant personally performed an invasion, and whether or not any specific invasion is attributed to the participant.

(c) Concealment is a continuing violation for as long as it persists.

(d) The prohibition applies regardless of the claimed authority, divine status, moral purpose, or asserted benevolence of the coalition or any participant. CUR-N.2 §2.2(b) and CUR-D.6 §6.2 apply.

(e) An arrangement that would otherwise fall within §5.1(b)(1) is not a Secret Coalition where every being it affects has given consent satisfying CUR-N.2 §2.1(c). Consent obtained by concealment is void by definition, since it cannot be informed.

### §5.4 - Human Collaborators

(a) A human who participates in a Secret Coalition is accountable under this Part on the same terms as any other participant. Being human is neither an aggravating nor a mitigating circumstance.

(b) Coercion is a defence to the extent it was operative. A human who participated under threat, duress, telepathic coercion, or exploitation of dependency is a Target under CUR-N.2 §2.6 in respect of that coercion, and is accountable only for conduct that was genuinely their own.

(c) Where coercion is asserted, the burden of establishing that participation was voluntary rests on the party alleging the violation. A being that was itself invaded in order to secure its participation is not a Collaborator by reason of that participation.

(d) A human who was recruited as a minor is presumed to have participated without valid consent until the age of majority, and accountability attaches only to conduct after that point and after a genuine opportunity to leave.

(e) Continued participation is not evidence of voluntariness where the participant's alternatives were constrained by the coalition. The reasoning of CUR-H.4 §4.10(c) applies.

### §5.5 - Accountability

(a) Measures follow CUR-N.4 §4.5, applying the degree of the invasions the coalition committed, facilitated, or concealed.

(b) Where a participant did not perform an invasion, the measure is proportionate to the participant's actual contribution, assessed by what the participant did, knew, and could have prevented.

(c) Direction of a coalition is an aggravating factor. Recruitment of others into it, and in particular recruitment of minors or of beings in a position of dependency, is a further aggravating factor.

(d) Every limit in CUR-N.4 §4.6 applies to every measure under this Part without exception. No measure may suspend a right, alter constitutional standing, withhold a Vital Continuity Service, modify cognition, run indefinitely, attach to a being other than the one determined accountable, or reach a being where the proper subject is an authorisation.

(e) Subsection (d)(6) of CUR-N.4 §4.6 is of particular importance here. Coalition membership is not transitive. No measure attaches to a participant's family, household, community, or associates by reason of the participant's conduct, and a determination against one participant is not evidence against another.

(f) Due process under CUR-N.4 §4.3 applies in full to every being named under this Part.

### §5.6 - Disclosure and Amnesty

(a) A Collaborator who discloses voluntarily, completely, and truthfully before any proceeding is commenced against them is entitled to substantial reduction of any measure, and to full amnesty where the disclosure is the effective cause of a coalition being identified.

(b) Disclosure shall be complete. Partial disclosure, disclosure that conceals the discloser's own role, and disclosure that names others while protecting the discloser do not qualify.

(c) Amnesty under subsection (a) does not extinguish:

   (1) The remedy owed to any Target under CUR-N.4 §4.8, which is unaffected;

   (2) Restitution obligations personally undertaken; or

   (3) Accountability for conduct outside the scope of the disclosure.

(d) A discloser is protected from retaliation by the coalition and its participants. Protective measures under CREF §10 and NIM.5 are available to a discloser on the same terms as to a Target.

(e) A disclosure made in good faith that proves to be mistaken is not itself a violation, and shall not be treated as a false report.

(f) No being shall be compelled to disclose. Refusal to disclose is not evidence of participation under §5.2(c), and no measure may be imposed for refusal.

(g) Amnesty is available once. A being that discloses, receives amnesty, and returns to a coalition is not eligible again in respect of the later conduct.

### §5.7 - Institutional Coalitions

(a) An institution that constitutes, hosts, funds, or conceals a Secret Coalition is accountable under this Part and under CUR-N.4 §4.9.

(b) Where an institution's structure was designed to make its participation deniable, that design is an aggravating factor rather than a defence.

(c) Measures against an institution include mandatory audit under CUR-FOUNDATION-010, withdrawal of authorisations, publication of findings, and institutional reform under CREF §12.

(d) A finding against an institution does not attach to its members individually. Individual accountability requires individual determination under CUR-N.4 §4.3, consistent with §5.5(e).

(e) An institution may not condition access to any Vital Continuity Service, employment, housing, or membership on participation in, or silence about, a coalition. Doing so is coercion under §5.4(b), and where the service withheld is a Vital Continuity Service it is a Class IV fault under CUR-FOUNDATION-013.

### §5.8 - Protection of Reporters and the Publicly Accused

(a) A being reporting a suspected coalition is protected under CUR-N.2 §2.6(d) and §2.6(e), including the right to non-retaliation and the right to privacy.

(b) A being named in a report is not thereby a Collaborator. Until a determination under CUR-N.4 §4.3, no measure attaches, no record characterises the being as accountable, and no institution may act against the being on the basis of the report alone.

(c) Public accusation in advance of a determination is not an accountability measure and is not authorised by this Part. Where it causes harm, CUR-H.4 applies.

(d) A being cleared under this Part is entitled to correction of any record, notice to every party that received the accusation, and public disclosure of the outcome at that being's election, under CUR-H.2 §2.5(e) applied by analogy.

(e) Repeated accusation of a being against whom no evidence has been produced is an abuse of process under CUR-X §3.9(d) and is a Class III fault.

### §5.9 - No Emergency Exception

(a) The Aevoric Commonwealth does not recognise emergency declarations, emergency authority, or any functional equivalent. PDDC §12.6 is absolute and Type A Entrenched, and §12.6(e) renders any instrument invoking emergency authority void ab initio.

(b) No characterisation of a coalition as widespread, entrenched, infiltrating, or existential creates authority that did not otherwise exist, and none narrows §5.2, §5.5(d), or CUR-N.4 §4.3.

(c) Subsection (b) is stated in specific terms because the assertion that a concealed threat is too pervasive for ordinary process is the standard argument for suspending ordinary process, and it is the argument this Code exists to refuse.

(d) The answer to a concealed and widespread threat is not less process applied faster. It is the scheduled monitoring regime of §5.2B, running continuously and at irregular intervals whether or not anything has been reported, in the manner of a built-in test in a fault-tolerant control system. A system that checks itself on a schedule detects a fault sooner than one that waits to be told, and it does so without granting anyone new authority, because a check that runs on everything singles out no one. Emergency powers are not a faster version of this. They are the absence of it, arriving after the harm.

(e) Where a coalition's activity is in progress, the available instruments are protective actions under CREF §10 and the immediate measures of TIM-N.5, both provisional and both reviewable within seventy-two (72) hours under CUR-N.4 §4.11(d).

### §5.10 - Records and Audit

(a) Every report, determination, measure, disclosure, and amnesty under this Part is a constitutional event under CUR-FOUNDATION-005 and is logged as required by CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §14.

(b) Records identifying a Target or a reporter are protected under CUR-N.2 §2.6(e).

(c) The identity of a being named in a report but not determined accountable shall not be published, and shall be held under transparency level T2 or higher under CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §8.

(d) Aggregate data on coalition determinations is published to the Public Transparency Registry under CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §9, in a form that identifies no Target, reporter, or cleared being.

(e) A determination overturned on appeal is corrected in the record to show that outcome rather than erased, consistent with CUR-FOUNDATION-005 §11.

### §5.12 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §5.2 (standard of proof and the limits on inference), §5.2A(c) and §5.2A(d) (the hard line against inferring a being's tier from a practice), §5.5(d) and §5.5(e) (limits on measures and non-transitivity), and §5.8 (protection of the publicly accused) are fundamental and shall be construed broadly to achieve their protective purpose.

(c) If §5.2 or §5.8 is held unenforceable, §5.3 and §5.5 are inoperative until restored. A prohibition on concealed conspiracy, operating without a standard of proof and without protection for the accused, is not a partial version of this Part.

## Implementation Notes

Design note. This Part was the most hazardous in the corpus to draft, and the
hazard is structural rather than a matter of wording. An instrument addressing
concealed conspiracy has two failure modes that pull in opposite directions: it
can dismiss genuine reports because concealed conduct is hard to evidence, or it
can find against beings on inference because the difficulty of evidence is
treated as proof. §5.2(b)(4) names both and refuses to trade one for the other,
by separating the stage at which a report is taken seriously from the stage at
which a finding is made.

§5.2(e) states why the protective provisions are load-bearing rather than
qualifying. §5.12(c) makes the point structurally: if the standard of proof or
the protection of the accused falls, the prohibition itself stops operating.
This is the third use of that device in the corpus, after CUR-H.4 §4.13(c) and
CUR-N.4 §4.14(c), and it is used here for the strongest reason of the three.

On non-transitivity. §5.5(e) provides that coalition membership does not spread
by association, and that a determination against one participant is not evidence
against another. Guilt by association is the characteristic failure of every
historical instrument addressing secret organisations, and CUR-N.4 §4.6(a)(6)
already forbids measures attaching to anyone other than the being determined
accountable. §5.5(e) states the evidentiary counterpart, because the prohibition
on collective measures is ineffective if collective inference is permitted.

On amnesty. CUR-N.2 §2.8(d) routes human collaborators to CHIM.1 for amnesty and
disclosure. §5.6 states the substantive rules that manual will implement: full
amnesty where disclosure is the effective cause of identification, no
extinguishment of the Target's remedy, protection from retaliation, no
compulsion to disclose, and availability once only. §5.6(c)(1) is the provision
that keeps amnesty from operating at the Target's expense.

Gap closed in `libcur`, and a correction. The first draft of this Part recorded
that `ViolationRecord` had "no state distinguishing an allegation from a
finding." That overstated the problem: `ViolationStatus` already existed, and
`VS_OPEN` already meant "detected, not yet adjudicated." The real defects were
narrower and worse.

First, nothing enforced the distinction. `record_sanction` attached measures via
`latest_open_violation`, which returns records in `VS_OPEN` and
`VS_UNDER_REVIEW` as readily as `VS_CONFIRMED`, and set every sanction
`SANC_ACTIVE` on creation. A measure could therefore become active against an
allegation that had never been adjudicated — precisely what §5.8(b) and CUR-N.4
§4.3(a) forbid.

Second, there was no way to record an overturned determination. `AppealStatus`
had `APPEAL_OVERTURNED` and `SanctionStatus` had `SANC_VACATED`, but the
violation record itself had nowhere to go: reverting it to `VS_DISMISSED` would
lose that it was ever confirmed, and leaving it `VS_CONFIRMED` would state
something false. §5.10(e) requires correction rather than erasure and neither
option provides it.

Both are now fixed. `VS_OVERTURNED` was added; `is_adjudicated` and
`supports_measure` make the allegation/finding line explicit and greppable;
`adjudication_permitted` is a flat one-way table in the style of the transition
tables, so nothing un-confirms a record or returns a reviewed one to
undetermined; `latest_sanctionable_violation` returns only records that can
carry a measure; and `activate_sanction` re-checks before any measure becomes
active. `record_sanction` now records a measure with no determination behind it
as `SANC_PROPOSED` rather than dropping it — the attempt belongs in the audit
trail, and "proposed" is exactly what a measure is before there is a finding.
