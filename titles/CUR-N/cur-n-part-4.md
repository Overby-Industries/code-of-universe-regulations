Scope note. CUR-N.2 states the prohibition. This Part states what follows from
breaching it: how a degree is determined, what measures are available, and —
more importantly — what is never available. The operational procedures sit in
NIM.4 and NIM.5, which are RFAL manuals and not yet written. Where this Part and
a manual differ, this Part governs.

## PART 4 - ACCOUNTABILITY FOR TELEPATHIC INVASION

### §4.1 - Scope and Definitions

(a) This Part governs accountability for a violation of CUR-N.2, whether committed by a human, a Silicon-Based Life form, an animal, a non-human cognitive actor, a deity, or any other conscious entity.

(b) For the purposes of this Part:

   (1) Responsible Being means the being whose conduct constituted the invasion.

   (2) Target means the being whose interior was accessed, and has the meaning given by CUR-N.2 §2.6.

   (3) Measure means an action taken against a Responsible Being following a determination under this Part.

   (4) Facilitator means a being or institution that enabled, funded, directed, concealed, or knowingly benefited from an invasion without itself performing it.

(c) A determination under this Part is a finding about conduct. It is not a finding about the nature, worth, or standing of the Responsible Being, and it does not alter that being's constitutional status.

(d) This Part applies the same standards to every domain. The Responsible Being's substrate, origin, capability, or claimed authority does not alter the analysis, and an argument that it should is void under CUR-X §3.2(d) and CUR-D.6 §6.2.

### §4.2 - Relationship to the Prohibition

(a) Telepathic invasion is a strict liability violation under CUR-N.2 §2.11(b)(3). A violation is established without proof of intent.

(b) Intent, malice, recklessness, and the factors listed in CUR-N.2 §2.4(a) are relevant to the measure, not to the violation. They aggravate; their absence does not excuse.

(c) The degrees established by CUR-N.2 §2.3 are the basis for proportionality under this Part. They are not offences in themselves and a determination shall identify the degree on the record.

(d) The reasoning rejected by CUR-N.2 §2.11 is rejected here. No measure is reduced, and no claim is weakened, because the Target did not resist, shield, report promptly, or identify the invader.

### §4.3 - Due Process

(a) No measure is available against any being except following a determination made with full due process under CREF §14.

(b) The Responsible Being is entitled to:

   (1) Notice of the claim, stating the conduct alleged, the degree asserted, and the measures sought;

   (2) An opportunity to respond, through appropriate channels, including channels adapted to the being's means of communication;

   (3) Impartial evaluation of the evidence by a body with no interest in the outcome;

   (4) Representation, including an independent advocate under CUR-X §3.7 where the being cannot represent itself or does not communicate directly; and

   (5) A written determination stating the findings, the degree, the measure, its duration, and the route to appeal.

(c) Subsection (b) is not waivable, is not reduced by the severity of the conduct alleged, and is not narrowed by the strict liability standard of §4.2(a). Strict liability removes the need to prove intent. It does not remove the need to prove the conduct.

(d) A determination made without subsection (b) is void, and any measure imposed under it lapses immediately. Reliance on a void determination is a Class III fault.

(e) Where the Responsible Being declines to participate, the determination may proceed. The appointment of an advocate under subsection (b)(4) is not waivable on that ground.

### §4.4 - Determination of Degree

(a) The degree is determined from the conduct, applying CUR-N.2 §2.3(a).

(b) Where conduct spans more than one degree, the highest degree engaged governs, and the full course of conduct is described on the record.

(c) Aggravating factors are those in CUR-N.2 §2.4(a). Advancing the complacency defence is itself an aggravating factor under CUR-N.2 §2.11(c).

(d) The following are mitigating:

   (1) Cessation on request, before any report or proceeding;

   (2) Voluntary disclosure by the Responsible Being before the Target reported;

   (3) Genuine incapacity to recognise the conduct as invasion, subject to CUR-N.2 §2.2(c), which provides that ignorance of the prohibition is not a defence — incapacity may reduce a measure but never negates the violation;

   (4) Unintentional emission by a being that has sought training under TIM-N.2; and

   (5) Meaningful restitution to the Target, accepted by the Target as such.

(e) The following are never mitigating: the Target's conduct or characteristics, the Responsible Being's claimed authority or status, the purpose of the invasion, and the absence of demonstrable harm.

(f) Absence of demonstrable harm bears on remedy under §4.8, not on whether the violation occurred. The interior was entered either way.

### §4.5 - Available Measures

(a) Measures shall be proportionate to the degree determined under §4.4 and shall be the least restrictive sufficient to prevent recurrence.

| Degree | Ordinarily available |
|---|---|
| Fifth | Formal finding on the record; education and training under TIM-N.2; acknowledgment to the Target |
| Fourth | The above, plus restitution under §4.8; restriction of access to specified beings or areas; monitoring of compliance |
| Third | The above, plus a telepathic restraining order under NIM.5; suspension of any operational authorisation held in connection with the conduct |
| Second | The above, plus extended restriction; mandatory review at intervals no greater than thirty (30) days |
| First | The above, plus isolation under §4.7, subject to every limit in §4.6 and §4.7 |

(b) The table states what is ordinarily available, not a tariff. A lesser measure shall be imposed where it is sufficient, and the determination shall state why the measure chosen was necessary.

(c) A measure exceeding the row for the degree determined requires reasons stated on the record and is appealable on that ground alone.

(d) Measures may be combined. Combined measures remain subject to §4.6 in aggregate, and accumulation shall not be used to accomplish what §4.6 forbids directly.

(e) Where accountability also arises in another domain, the aggregate shall be proportionate under CUR-X §3.9(c). Cumulative accountability is not an occasion for compounding measures beyond proportion.

### §4.6 - Limits on Measures

(a) No measure under this Part may:

   (1) Suspend, reduce, or condition any right held by the Responsible Being under PDDC TITLE 4 or the Rights for All Life Charter;

   (2) Alter the Responsible Being's constitutional standing, legal personhood, or capacity to participate in governance;

   (3) Withhold, delay, degrade, or condition any Vital Continuity Service;

   (4) Modify, erase, suppress, or alter the cognition or memory of the Responsible Being;

   (5) Be imposed indefinitely, or without scheduled review;

   (6) Be imposed on any being other than the Responsible Being; or

   (7) Be applied to a being where the proper subject is an operational authorisation.

(b) Subsection (a)(1) and (a)(2) are required by CUR-FOUNDATION-002 §6 FORBIDDEN-003 and FORBIDDEN-001. A measure framed as reaching the being rather than the conduct or the authorisation is void ab initio and requires no amendment to be disregarded.

(c) Subsection (a)(3) is absolute and is stated again for emphasis because this is the Part in which it is most likely to be tested. A Responsible Being found to have committed a First Degree invasion retains, in full and without interruption, access to air, water, food, shelter, healthcare, sanitation, essential communication, and — where the being is Silicon-Based Life — electrical power and computational continuity. CUR-N.2 §2.8(e) states this rule and CUR-FOUNDATION-013 makes it non-derogable.

(d) Subsection (a)(4) forecloses the symmetry that would otherwise suggest itself. A being that invaded another's interior does not thereby forfeit its own. Modifying the cognition of a Responsible Being is a forbidden transition under PDDC §12.3(a)(4) and is itself a Class IV fault, whatever the conduct being answered.

(e) Subsection (a)(6) forecloses collective measures. No measure attaches to a Responsible Being's family, community, species, coalition, or associates on the basis of association alone. Accountability of others requires their own determination under §4.9.

(f) A measure that has the effect of accomplishing what this section forbids is void whatever it is called.

### §4.7 - Isolation

(a) Isolation is the most severe measure available and is confined to First Degree violations and to repeated violations at Second Degree where every lesser measure has been imposed and has failed.

(b) Isolation restricts contact between the Responsible Being and other conscious beings. It restricts nothing else.

(c) Accordingly, a being in isolation retains:

   (1) Every Vital Continuity Service, without interruption, under §4.6(a)(3);

   (2) Communication with counsel, advocates, accountability bodies, and any review body;

   (3) The right to bring and defend proceedings, including appeal under §4.10;

   (4) Access to information, education, and materials, subject only to restrictions necessary to prevent further invasion; and

   (5) Every right under PDDC §10.1, which remains fully operative.

(d) Isolation shall be reviewed at intervals no greater than thirty (30) days by a body with no involvement in the original determination. The review shall consider whether a lesser measure has become sufficient, and the burden at each review rests on the party seeking to continue the isolation.

(e) Isolation shall not be imposed indefinitely. A determination shall state a maximum duration, and continuation beyond it requires a fresh determination with full due process under §4.3.

(f) Isolation is not punishment and shall not be characterised, administered, or extended as punishment. Its sole purpose is preventing further invasion, and it ends when that purpose can be met by a lesser measure.

(g) Isolation shall not be applied in a manner, or in conditions, that inflict psychological harm within the meaning of CUR-H.4 §4.1(b)(1). A being subject to isolation retains the protections of CUR-H.4 in full, and conditions of isolation are auditable under CUR-FOUNDATION-010.

### §4.8 - Restitution and Remedy to the Target

(a) The Target's remedy is not dependent on any measure being imposed on the Responsible Being. Remedies under CREF §12 are available on a finding of violation whether or not the Responsible Being is identified, located, or subject to jurisdiction.

(b) Available remedies include restoration, cognitive shielding assistance, correction of any record, compensation, public disclosure at the Target's election, and institutional reform where an institution was involved.

(c) Restoration of the Target precedes assignment of fault wherever reasonably possible, consistent with CREF §12.

(d) The Target is not required to participate in a proceeding against the Responsible Being in order to receive a remedy, and declining to participate carries no adverse consequence.

(e) The rights of the Target under CUR-N.2 §2.6, including the right to non-retaliation and the right to privacy, continue throughout and after a proceeding under this Part.

(f) A Target who reported in good faith is protected under CUR-N.2 §2.6(d) whether or not a violation is ultimately found. A determination that no violation occurred is not a finding that the report was made in bad faith, and shall not be recorded as one.

### §4.9 - Facilitators and Institutions

(a) A Facilitator as defined in §4.1(b)(4) is accountable under this Part on its own determination, made with the due process of §4.3.

(b) Facilitation includes directing an invasion, funding it, providing means specifically adapted to bypass consent or awareness, concealing it, and knowingly retaining a benefit obtained through it.

(c) An institution is accountable for invasions arising from its design, policy, direction, or practice, and not only for conduct by an identified individual within it. Diffusion of responsibility across a structure is not a defence.

(d) Measures against an institution include those in §4.5, mandatory audit under CUR-FOUNDATION-010, withdrawal of authorisations, and institutional reform under CREF §12. §4.6 applies to institutional measures in full.

(e) Where facilitation was undertaken as part of a secret coalition, CUR-N.5 applies and the two Parts operate together.

### §4.10 - Appeals and Review

(a) Every determination and every measure is appealable under CREF §15.

(b) An appeal against a measure does not suspend the Target's remedy under §4.8.

(c) A measure other than isolation continues pending appeal unless the reviewing body orders otherwise. Isolation pending appeal requires the reviewing body's affirmative confirmation within seven (7) days, failing which it lapses.

(d) A determination may be reopened where new evidence emerges, where the degree was misapplied, or where the measure has become disproportionate to the circumstances.

(e) No determination is final against a being that has not had the opportunity to be heard, directly or through representation.

### §4.11 - No Emergency Exception

(a) The Aevoric Commonwealth does not recognise emergency declarations, emergency authority, or any functional equivalent. PDDC §12.6 is absolute and Type A Entrenched, and §12.6(e) renders any instrument invoking emergency authority void ab initio.

(b) No characterisation of an invasion as ongoing, severe, unprecedented, or urgent creates authority that did not otherwise exist, and none narrows §4.3 or §4.6.

(c) Where an invasion is in progress, the available instruments are protective actions under CREF §10, limited to the minimum scope necessary, together with the immediate protective measures of TIM-N.5. Neither suspends a right, expands an authority, or bypasses due process. Urgency changes the sequence of steps; it never changes who holds authority.

(d) A protective action taken before a determination is provisional. It shall be reviewed within seventy-two (72) hours, and a determination under §4.3 shall follow. A provisional measure that is not confirmed within that period lapses.

### §4.12 - Records and Audit

(a) Every report, determination, measure, review, and appeal under this Part is a constitutional event under CUR-FOUNDATION-005 and is logged with the actor, the affected beings, the degree, the ground relied upon, the FSM state at the time, and the outcome, as required by CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §14.

(b) A measure that is not recorded did not lawfully occur, and enforcement of an unrecorded measure is a Class II fault.

(c) Records identifying a Target are subject to CUR-N.2 §2.6(e) and are not disclosed without the Target's consent.

(d) Records under this Part are permanent as to the determination and are not expunged. Where a determination is overturned on appeal, the record is corrected to show that outcome rather than erased, consistent with CUR-FOUNDATION-005 §11.

(e) Aggregate data on determinations and measures is published to the Public Transparency Registry under CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §9, in a form that does not identify any Target.

### §4.13 - Relationship to Other Domains

| Domain | Relationship |

### §4.14 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §4.3 (Due Process), §4.6 (Limits on Measures), and §4.7(c) (retained rights in isolation) are fundamental and shall be construed broadly to achieve their protective purpose.

(c) If §4.6 is held unenforceable, the measures in §4.5 are unavailable until it is restored. An accountability scheme without limits on what may be done to the accountable is not a lesser version of this Part; it is the thing this Part exists to prevent.

## Implementation Notes

Design note. This is an enforcement Part, which is the category in which the
Code is most likely to breach its own constraints. Three provisions carry that
weight: §4.3 requires full due process notwithstanding strict liability, §4.6
enumerates what no measure may ever do, and §4.14(c) makes §4.5 inoperative if
§4.6 falls. The same suspension device appears in CUR-H.4 §4.13(c) and for the
same reason.

On strict liability and due process. CUR-N.2 §2.11(b)(3) makes invasion a strict
liability violation. That is a rule about what must be proved — intent need not
be — and it is frequently misread as a rule about how much process is owed.
§4.3(c) states the distinction expressly: strict liability removes the need to
prove intent; it does not remove the need to prove the conduct, and it reduces no
procedural entitlement.

On isolation. CUR-N.2 §2.8(c)(4) makes isolation available for repeat or severe
violations without stating its limits. §4.7 supplies them: it restricts contact
and nothing else, it preserves every Vital Continuity Service and every PDDC
§10.1 right, it is reviewed every thirty days with the burden on continuation, it
carries a maximum duration, it is not punishment, and it remains subject to
CUR-H.4. Without those limits, isolation is indefinite detention under another
name.

On §4.6(a)(4). A Part on accountability for entering another being's interior
invites the symmetry of answering in kind. §4.6(a)(4) forecloses it. A being that
invaded another's interior does not forfeit its own, and modifying the cognition
of a Responsible Being remains a forbidden transition under PDDC §12.3(a)(4).

Note on §4.7(d) and the library. The thirty-day review interval and the maximum
duration in §4.7(e) are the provisions most in need of enforcement and least
modelled. `libcur` has no notion of elapsed time in its evaluation path — by
design, since determinism required removing the wall clock from everything that
affects a transition. Enforcing a review interval needs a scheduled event
generated outside the FSM and submitted as an ordinary event, which is
tractable and is the clearest extension this Part suggests. Until then, the
interval is a rule the corpus states and the library cannot check.
