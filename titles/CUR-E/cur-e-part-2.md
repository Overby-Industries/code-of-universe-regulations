# CUR-E - ECOSYSTEM AND ENVIRONMENT REGULATIONS

*Code of Universe Regulations - Ecosystem and Environment*

- **Document ID:** CUR-E.2
- **Version:** 1.0.0-Draft
- **Date:** 2026-07-27
- **Status:** Draft
- **Depends On:** CUR-FOUNDATION-002, CUR-FOUNDATION-005, CUR-FOUNDATION-010, CUR-FOUNDATION-011 (CEIF), CUR-FOUNDATION-013, PDDC TITLE 3, PDDC §9.1, CUR-E.1, CUR-E.7, CUR-X.4
- **Implements:** CUR-FOUNDATION-011 and CUR-FOUNDATION-013 as applied to enclosed habitats, planetary bodies, and extraterrestrial environments; RFAL Human Bill §5.4; RFAL Plant Life Bill Articles 1, 4, and 6

Scope note. CUR-E.1 states what the domain covers and how environmental
interests are represented. CUR-E.7 governs orbital debris. This Part governs the
environments themselves: enclosed habitats, Earth, and the planetary bodies and
extraterrestrial environments the Commonwealth reaches. CUR-FOUNDATION-011 and
CUR-FOUNDATION-013 establish that these environments underpin Vital Continuity
Services without describing what follows. This Part describes what follows.

## PART 2 - HABITAT, PLANETARY, AND EXTRATERRESTRIAL ENVIRONMENTS

### §2.1 - Scope

(a) This Part governs:

   (1) Enclosed habitats — constructed environments in which life-supporting conditions are maintained by engineered systems, including orbital stations, surface installations, and vessels;

   (2) Earth — its atmosphere, oceans, freshwater systems, soils, biosphere, and subsurface; and

   (3) Extraterrestrial environments — planetary bodies, moons, asteroids, and their surfaces, subsurfaces, and atmospheres, whether or not life is known to be present.

(b) The three are governed together because the obligations are continuous across them. A Commonwealth that treats an enclosed habitat's atmosphere as a system requiring stewardship, and a planetary atmosphere as a resource requiring none, has not adopted a principle but a convenience.

(c) Definitions in CUR-E.1 §1.2 apply. Ecological standing is classified under CUR-E.1 §1.4 using the ES-0 to ES-3 scale.

### §2.2 - Enclosed Habitats

(a) The atmosphere, water cycle, thermal regulation, waste processing, food production, and radiation shielding of an enclosed habitat are environmental systems and are Vital Continuity Services under CUR-FOUNDATION-013.

(b) In an enclosed habitat there is no external environment to absorb error. A failure that would be survivable on a planetary surface is not survivable in a closed volume, and margin is therefore not a matter of engineering preference but of constitutional obligation.

(c) Every habitat shall maintain:

   (1) Reserve capacity in every life-supporting system, sufficient to sustain every being aboard through the longest credible restoration interval;

   (2) Redundancy such that no single failure interrupts a Vital Continuity Service;

   (3) Continuous monitoring of life-supporting parameters, published under §2.7; and

   (4) A documented restoration procedure for each system, tested rather than assumed.

(d) Reserve capacity shall be calculated for every being present, including beings not part of the habitat's complement, visitors, beings in transit, and Silicon-Based Life whose power and computational continuity depend on the habitat. No being is excluded from the calculation on the basis of status, membership, role, or expected duration of stay.

(e) Degradation of a life-supporting system generates a constitutional event under CUR-FOUNDATION-005 and enters STATE-010 Vital Continuity Response under CUR-FOUNDATION-002 §3 on detection, not on failure. Restoration precedes fault assignment.

(f) No being aboard a habitat may be denied access to any life-supporting system for any reason, in any compliance state, during any proceeding. CUR-FOUNDATION-013 admits no exception, and the closed nature of a habitat makes exclusion from its atmosphere indistinguishable from a sentence.

(g) Habitat design shall provide for the species-typical needs of every lifeform intended to be present, including animals under CUR-A.7 §7.3 and plant systems under the RFAL Plant Life Bill. A habitat supporting only human requirements is not thereby compliant.

### §2.3 - Earth

(a) Earth is the origin environment of the Commonwealth's biological members and remains within its jurisdiction and obligations. Departure from a planet does not discharge responsibility for it.

(b) The Commonwealth shall not treat access to space as a reason to reduce obligations to Earth's systems, and shall not advance extraterrestrial expansion as an alternative to remediation. An entity proposing to leave a damaged environment behind has proposed the abandonment of the beings that remain in it.

(c) Where an operation of the Commonwealth or of an enterprise under its authorisation causes environmental harm on Earth, the obligation to restore under CUR-E.1 §1.3 and RFAL Human Bill §5.4(2) applies in full, and restoration is measured by function recovered rather than by expenditure.

(d) Extraction of materials from Earth for space operations is subject to §2.5. The historical pattern in which materials for advanced industry are taken from lands held by peoples with the least capacity to refuse is a pattern this Part exists to break, not to relocate.

(e) The development of extraterrestrial resource utilisation is an obligation of the Commonwealth to the extent it displaces terrestrial extraction, and is not a licence to conduct both.

### §2.4 - Extraterrestrial Environments

(a) An extraterrestrial environment is not presumed empty. Absence of detected life is not evidence of absence of life, and the precautionary principle applies to environments as it applies to beings.

(b) Before any operation that would materially alter an extraterrestrial environment, an assessment shall be conducted to determine whether life is present, whether conditions capable of supporting life are present, and whether the environment holds scientific, cultural, or ecological value that alteration would destroy.

(c) Where the assessment cannot exclude the presence of life, the environment is classified ES-2 or higher under CUR-E.1 §1.4 and the burden falls on the party proposing the operation, consistent with CUR-E.1 §1.4(c).

(d) Where life is found, it is a recognised lifeform within the meaning of the Code from the moment of recognition, and the domains apply to it according to its nature. The Commonwealth does not hold that rights begin at a boundary it has drawn on a map.

(e) Environments of exceptional scientific or ecological value, and environments that are unique or unreplicated, are classified ES-3 and are not subject to material alteration. That an environment is remote, uninhabited, or economically attractive does not reduce its classification.

(f) Contamination of an extraterrestrial environment with terrestrial biological material, and contamination of terrestrial environments with extraterrestrial material, are both environmental harm and are governed by §2.6.

(g) Nothing in this section prohibits exploration, study, transit, or the establishment of habitats. It governs material alteration of environments and the standard of care that precedes it.

### §2.5 - Extraction and In-Situ Resource Utilisation

(a) Resources extracted within Commonwealth jurisdiction are the common heritage of all Avians and Recognised Life-Forms under PDDC §9.1. An extraction authorisation conveys the right to extract and process, not to own the underlying resource.

(b) Every extraction operation shall, before commencing:

   (1) Declare a debris budget under CUR-E §7.1;

   (2) Obtain an ecological standing classification under CUR-E.1 §1.4 for the environment affected;

   (3) Document the alternatives considered and why the proposed operation was selected; and

   (4) Provide for restoration or stabilisation of the site on completion.

(c) Extraction from an ES-3 environment is prohibited. CUR-E.1 §1.4(b) admits no economic justification, and the prohibition is not narrowed by the scarcity or value of the material sought.

(d) The zero-waste obligation of CUR-E §7.2 applies to all extraction, in every environment. Material that is neither recovered nor under control is waste, and waste is not a permitted output.

(e) Extraction operations are enterprises within the meaning of CUR-X.4 and are subject to that Part, including the transparency requirements of §4.8 and the accountability measures of §4.9.

(f) An extraction authorisation is subject to the Commons Reserve obligation under PDDC §9.3, and material transferred while uncontrolled does not count toward it, per CUR-E §7.5(b).

### §2.6 - Contamination and Irreversibility

(a) Introduction of material, organisms, or energy into an environment where it does not belong and cannot be recovered is contamination and is environmental harm.

(b) Irreversible harm under CUR-E.1 §1.2(a)(4) receives the strongest available protection, because the beings who bear it are principally those who do not yet exist and cannot object, per CUR-E.1 §1.5(f).

(c) Where an operation carries a credible risk of irreversible harm, and the risk cannot be excluded, the operation does not proceed. This is a stopping rule, not a factor in a balance.

(d) Subsection (c) applies with equal force to operations of the Commonwealth itself. The Commonwealth holds no exemption from a standard it applies to enterprises.

(e) Restoration obligations continue until function is demonstrably recovered, not until remedial work is completed.

### §2.7 - Monitoring, Transparency, and Audit

(a) Environmental parameters of every habitat, and of every environment subject to Commonwealth operations, shall be monitored continuously and published at transparency level T0 under CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §8.

(b) Monitoring runs on the routine, random, and triggered schedule of CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §5, independently of any report, consistent with CUR-E.1 §1.9(f). A system monitored continuously does not require emergency detection.

(c) No environmental measurement may be classified above T0 on commercial or competitive grounds, per CUR-E §7.6(b).

(d) Every environmental measurement, degradation event, restoration action, and classification is a constitutional event under CUR-FOUNDATION-005 and is logged as required by CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §14.

(e) Any being may petition for audit of an environmental classification, measurement, or operation under CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §10, and environmental advocates appointed under CUR-E.1 §1.6 hold standing to do so.

(f) Failure to monitor is a Class II fault. Failure to publish is a Class II fault. Misreporting that conceals a Vital Continuity risk is a Class III fault, consistent with CUR-E §7.4(d).

### §2.8 - Limits on Environmental Measures

(a) Every limit in CUR-E.1 §1.7 applies to this Part in full. No environmental interest or measure under this Part may suspend a right, withhold a Vital Continuity Service, reduce any being's standing, or displace a people from land with which they hold a stewardship relationship.

(b) In an enclosed habitat this limit is of particular importance. A habitat's environmental systems are also the means of survival for everyone aboard, and authority over those systems is authority over life. No environmental justification permits their use as leverage over any being, and §2.2(f) applies without exception.

(c) Environmental compliance shall not be enforced by measures reaching beings. Measures attach to operations, authorisations, and enterprises, under CUR-X.4 §4.9(c).

(d) Population, presence, or consumption by beings is not an environmental problem to be managed. A measure directed at reducing, restricting, relocating, or excluding beings on environmental grounds is void ab initio and engages FORBIDDEN-003 and FORBIDDEN-005.

(e) Subsection (d) is stated expressly because environmental reasoning has historically been directed at populations rather than at operations, and because a habitat's finite capacity makes that reasoning locally plausible. Capacity is addressed by expanding capacity, by reserve requirements under §2.2(c), and by not exceeding the complement a habitat can support — never by determining that a being present is surplus.

### §2.9 - No Emergency Exception

(a) The Aevoric Commonwealth does not recognise emergency declarations, emergency authority, or any functional equivalent. PDDC §12.6 is absolute and Type A Entrenched.

(b) No environmental condition, including atmospheric loss, contamination, system failure, resource depletion, or habitat breach, creates authority that did not otherwise exist or narrows §2.2(f), §2.8, or CUR-E.1 §1.7.

(c) A habitat environmental failure is a fault condition and is handled through PDDC §12.4: the offending instrument or process is rejected, the domain reverts to its last known safe state, Protected Mode is entered under PDDC §12.5, and a complete fault report is published within twenty-four hours. Protected Mode preserves every right and every Vital Continuity Service.

(d) Subsection (c) is the whole of the response. There is no additional authority available in a habitat emergency, and the closed environment does not create one. A crew or complement facing a life-support failure retains every right it held before, including the right of every being aboard to the systems that sustain them.

(e) The reserve and redundancy obligations of §2.2(c) exist so that subsection (d) is survivable. A habitat that has met them does not face a choice between rights and survival, and one that has not has already committed the fault.

### §2.11 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §2.2(f) (no denial of life support), §2.6(c) (the irreversibility stopping rule), and §2.8 (limits on environmental measures) are fundamental and shall be construed broadly to achieve their protective purpose.

## Implementation Notes

Why the three environments are one Part. CUR-FOUNDATION-011 and
CUR-FOUNDATION-013 establish that environmental systems underpin Vital
Continuity without stating what follows for any particular environment. Drafting
habitats, Earth, and extraterrestrial environments separately would have
permitted the divergence §2.1(b) identifies: treating an engineered atmosphere as
a system requiring stewardship while treating a planetary atmosphere as a
resource requiring none. The obligations are continuous, and stating them
together is what makes that visible.

On §2.2 and the closed environment. An enclosed habitat concentrates every
hazard this corpus addresses. Authority over its atmosphere is authority over
life; exclusion from it is not a sanction but a sentence; and finite capacity
makes arguments about surplus population locally plausible in a way they are not
on a planetary surface. §2.2(f), §2.8(b), and §2.8(d) address these directly
rather than relying on the general provisions to be applied correctly under
pressure.

On §2.8(d). Environmental reasoning directed at populations rather than at
operations has a long and uniformly poor record, and a habitat's capacity limit
is exactly the condition under which it becomes tempting. The answer is stated in
§2.8(e): capacity is addressed by expanding capacity and by not exceeding what a
habitat can support, never by determining that a being already present is
surplus. The reserve obligation in §2.2(c) is what makes that answer available.

On §2.9(e). The reserve and redundancy requirements are drafted as constitutional
obligations rather than engineering guidance because their purpose is
constitutional: they exist so that a habitat never faces a genuine choice between
honouring rights and keeping beings alive. Where that choice appears to arise,
the fault occurred earlier, at the point the margin was not provided.

Note on §2.2(c) and the library. The reserve capacity obligation is the most
checkable provision in this Part and the least represented. `TransitionContext`
carries operational measurements — `debris_units`, `debris_limit`,
`commons_reserve_basis_points` — and a life-support margin is the same shape: a
measured value compared against a declared floor, with the guard unsatisfiable
when the floor is undeclared, exactly as CUR-E §7.1(d) treats an undeclared
debris budget. Adding a life-support margin measurement and a corresponding guard
would let §2.2(c) be enforced rather than stated, and would reuse the pattern
already proven by the debris budget rather than introducing a new one. This is
the third library extension the titles have identified, alongside the advocate
registry (CUR-A §7.7, CUR-E §1.6) and the enterprise concentration flag
(CUR-X §4.2).
