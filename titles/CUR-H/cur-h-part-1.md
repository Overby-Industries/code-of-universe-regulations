Note on sequence. This Part carries the number a Definitions and Scope Part
conventionally carries first, but it was drafted last, after CUR-H.2 through
CUR-H.8 already existed. That ordering is disclosed rather than concealed: this
Part is not the bedrock the other seven were built on — they were each drafted
self-contained, defining their own terms locally, and mostly still do. What
this Part supplies instead is what only becomes visible once a domain has
enough Parts to have edges: what "human being" actually means at the domain's
boundary with CUR-S and CUR-N, what happens when that boundary is crossed by
augmentation or substrate transition, and a map of what the domain now
contains. §1.4(b) says plainly why the domain's existing Parts were not
retroactively edited to point here instead of restating their own scope
clauses.

## PART 1 - DEFINITIONS AND SCOPE

### §1.1 - Purpose

(a) This Part defines the terms this domain's other Parts rely on, states the
domain's jurisdictional reach, resolves what happens at its boundary with
other domains, and provides a map of what the domain currently contains.

(b) This Part creates no new prohibition, no new measure, and no new
authority. Where it appears to state a substantive rule, that rule is drawn
from a provision already stated elsewhere in this domain, in CUR-X, or in the
PDDC, and the source is cited. This Part's function is to make those rules
locatable and to state them once at the level of the whole domain, not to add
to them.

### §1.2 - Human Being

(a) For the purposes of this domain, Human Being means a being of biological
human origin, including a being whose body or cognition has been modified,
augmented, or partially replaced under CUR-H.2 §2.4 or CUR-H.5.

(b) Origin is not forfeited by modification, augmentation, medical
intervention, or the passage of time. A being does not stop being protected
under this domain because of what has been done to their body or mind,
including with their own consent.

(c) Where a being's status as a Human Being is unclear, disputed, or cannot be
definitively established, the being is treated as within this domain's
protection until an independent process concludes otherwise. This is the same
precautionary default CUR-S §4.2(b) states for tier assessment, applied here:
the burden falls on the party seeking to narrow protection, never on the
being. It is also the domain-specific form of the default `libcur` already
applies to an unclassified entity generally, which resolves to
`SUBJ_SENTIENT_BEING` rather than to any lesser classification.

(d) A determination that a being falls outside this domain's definition of
Human Being does not reduce the being's protection. CUR-X.3 §3.2(b) holds
humans, silicon-based entities, animals, hybrids, and future recognised
lifeforms to equal constitutional standing, and CUR-X §3.4(c) makes protection
cumulative across domains rather than exclusive to one. This Part's definition
sorts which title's text governs a given question; it is never a boundary a
being can fall through.

(e) A being whose consciousness, memory, or identity transitions to a
different substrate — including migration governed by CUR-S §4.4 — retains
every protection held under this domain for conduct and history preceding the
transition, and additionally acquires the protections of CUR-S from the point
the transition is complete. CUR-H.5 §5.5A(j) already states that a
determination concerning such a being is handled on substrate-independent
terms; this subsection states the same principle for the whole domain, not
only for that determination.

### §1.3 - Jurisdiction

(a) This domain applies to every human being within Commonwealth jurisdiction
as PDDC §1.3 defines it: every entity, operation, territory, vessel,
installation, data system, and contractual relationship that falls within or
is sponsored by the Aevoric Commonwealth, regardless of physical location,
dimensional domain, or operational medium.

(b) Jurisdiction under this Part does not depend on Avian status, citizenship,
or membership. A human being present within, sponsored by, or subject to a
Commonwealth-governed system holds every protection this domain states,
whether or not they are a citizen of the Commonwealth. §1.4 states the
corresponding rule for the reverse case.

(c) Where jurisdiction itself is disputed, the being retains the protections
of this domain for the duration of the dispute, on the same precautionary
principle §1.2(c) states for status.

### §1.4 - Relationship to Avian and Citizenship Status

(a) Avian status and Commonwealth citizenship are governed by PDDC PART 3 and
defined at PDDC Appendix A §A.1(g) as "a citizen or Full Member of the Aevoric
Commonwealth," without reference to substrate or species. A human being's
protection under this domain does not depend on holding Avian status, and
Avian status does not depend on being human.

(b) No provision of this domain shall be construed to condition a protection
stated here on Commonwealth membership, contribution, or standing. CUR-H.2
§2.1(e), CUR-H.3 §3.1(d), CUR-H.7 §7.1(d), and CUR-H.8 §8.1(d) each state this
independently, for their own Part, and predate this Part. They are not removed
or redirected to cite this section instead. CUR-H.7 §7.15(c) already states
why restating a settled rule in a second text risks the two drifting apart;
weighed against that, silently rewriting four already-published Parts to
centralise a rule they already state correctly is the larger and less
justified risk, so this section states the rule again rather than displacing
them.

### §1.6 - Interpretive Principles

These are not new rules. Each restates, once for the domain, a pattern that
already governs individual Parts, so that it is citable directly rather than
only observable by comparison. `docs/reading-cur.md` §5 is where the pattern
was first written down, across the whole corpus rather than this domain
alone; this section is that pattern's operative statement for CUR-H.

(a) Where a provision of this domain does not state a numeric limit, quantity,
or threshold, no default is implied and the provision is unsatisfiable rather
than unlimited. CUR-H.5 §5.5A(e)'s unshortenable observation interval is an
instance of this rule, not an exception requiring separate justification.

(b) An allegation is not a finding, anywhere in this domain. No provision of
CUR-H attaches a measure, restriction, or record to a being on the basis of an
accusation alone. CUR-H.6 §6.13(a), CUR-H.7 §7.15(f), and CUR-H.8 §8.9(e) each
state this for their own Part.

(c) Where this domain is silent and a being's protection is genuinely
uncertain, the uncertainty resolves toward the reading that protects the
being, provided that reading confers no new authority on any party.

(d) A classification, once established at a more protective level, may not be
revised toward a less protective one by reclassification, relabelling, or
redefinition. CUR-H.7 §7.11(e) states the library mechanism that enforces
this — `guard::LICENSE_SUBJECT_ONLY` and the `registered_subject_class`
ratchet; this section states the domain-wide principle it implements.

### §1.7 - No Emergency Exception

(a) Nothing in this domain is suspended, narrowed, or conditioned by any
emergency, crisis, security condition, or state of exception. PDDC §12.6 is
Type A Entrenched and reaches every Part of this domain.

(b) Each substantive Part restates this for its own subject. This section
states it once for the domain and does not narrow or substitute for those
statements.

### §1.8 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the
remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §1.2 (Human Being) and §1.4(a) (independence from Avian and citizenship
status) are fundamental and shall be construed broadly to achieve their
protective purpose. No construction of any other provision of this Part may
narrow them.

## Implementation Notes

Why this Part does not centralise what it could have. §1.4(b) is the place
this is most visible: four published Parts each state, in their own words,
that protection does not depend on membership or contribution. This Part could
have deleted those four statements and pointed each Part here instead. It does
not, because CUR-H.7 §7.15(c) already gives the reason not to — a rule stated
in two places risks the two drifting apart, but a rule *removed* from four
places that have already been read, cited, and relied upon is a larger edit
than the drift it would prevent, undertaken for a benefit (fewer repeated
sentences) smaller than the risk (silently changing four Parts that were not
otherwise being revised). The repetition is judged the lesser cost.

Why §1.2 exists at all. Every other Part in this domain defines its own local
terms and does not need a shared definition of "human being" — CUR-H.5 governs
bodily autonomy whether or not the boundary case matters to it. The boundary
case became unavoidable once CUR-S §4.4 modelled substrate migration from its
own side, and CUR-X.3 §3.2 named hybrids as a recognised category with equal
standing: a domain that protects "human beings" without saying what happens
when one migrates, is augmented, or is disputed at the edge is a domain with
an undrawn boundary, and an undrawn boundary is exactly the kind of gap this
corpus has elsewhere treated as a loophole rather than an omission.
