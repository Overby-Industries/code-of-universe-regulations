# CUR-H - HUMAN REGULATIONS

*Code of Universe Regulations - Human*

- **Document ID:** CUR-H.5
- **Version:** 1.0.0-Draft
- **Date:** 2026-07-27
- **Status:** Draft

- **Referenced by:** CUR-H.4 Implementation Notes

Numbering note. RFAL Article IV is implemented across two Parts. Article IV §4.2
(Cognitive Liberty) is at CUR-H.2 §2.4, drafted there because CUR-H.2 aligned
its sections with RFAL Article II and cognitive liberty sat naturally alongside
freedom of thought. Article IV §4.1 is this Part. CUR-H.4 explains why Part 4
could not take it: external citations already bound `CUR-H.4` to psychological
harm, and citation stability governs under `titles/README.md`. Section numbers
within this Part are not aligned to RFAL subsection numbers, because no external
citation depends on such an alignment and inventing one would create a
correspondence that later drafting would have to preserve.

Relationship to CUR-H.4. That Part governs psychological harm and states, at
§4.4, that a finding of harm creates no authority over the being harmed. This
Part states the same structure for the body: §5.9 is its operative safeguard and
is drafted deliberately in parallel. The two Parts are read together, and neither
narrows the other.

## PART 5 - BODILY AUTONOMY AND SELF-DETERMINATION

### §5.1 - Scope and Definitions

(a) This Part governs the sovereignty of a human being over their own body, and the strict limits on what any institution, enterprise, practitioner, family member, or other party may do to a being's body or require of it.

(b) For the purposes of this Part:

   (1) Bodily Decision means any decision concerning a being's own body, health, medical treatment, reproduction, physical modification, physical presence, or the manner and timing of the end of their own life.

   (2) Bodily Intervention means any act performed on a being's body, including examination, treatment, medication, procedure, restraint, confinement, sampling, implantation, and removal.

   (3) Coercion means any conduct making a bodily decision conditional on the retention of something the being is otherwise entitled to, including a Vital Continuity Service, employment, housing, standing, custody of a dependant, liberty, or access to any proceeding. CUR-N.5 §5.2A(e) locates the same boundary for coercive practice generally.

   (4) Supported Decision-Making means assistance provided to a being in reaching and expressing their own decision. It is not, and never becomes, decision-making by another party.

(c) This Part reaches all human beings without qualification. Age, diagnosis, disability, cognitive condition, communicative capacity, custodial status, employment, dependency, and membership of any group are not conditions on the rights stated here and are not grounds for their reduction.

(d) Where a being is also a subject under another domain, this Part applies concurrently under CUR-X §3.4. Where a Silicon-Based Life entity or other recognised lifeform holds an equivalent interest in the integrity of its own substrate, CUR-S and the applicable domain govern, and the reasoning of this Part is available to them under CUR-X §3.3.

### §5.2 - Sovereignty Over the Body

(a) Every human being holds sovereign authority over their own body. Every bodily decision is theirs to make.

(b) No bodily intervention may be performed on a being without their consent, satisfying the standard of CUR-H.2 §2.1(c): explicit, informed, freely given, ongoing, and revocable.

(c) Consent is specific to the intervention consented to. It does not extend to a further intervention, to an extension of scope discovered during a procedure, or to a repetition on a later occasion.

(d) Consent is revocable at any time, including during an intervention, and revocation takes effect immediately so far as it is physically possible to give effect to it. An intervention continued after revocation is a bodily intervention without consent from the moment of revocation.

(e) Refusal requires no reason. A being who declines an intervention is not required to justify the refusal, to demonstrate understanding of the consequences, or to submit to persuasion as a condition of the refusal being honoured.

(f) A bodily intervention performed without consent is void ab initio, is a Class IV fault, and where it modifies the being engages FORBIDDEN-004 under CUR-FOUNDATION-002 §6. Non-consensual modification of cognition is additionally governed by CUR-H.2 §2.4(d) and CUR-H.4 §4.4(d), and neither provision is narrowed by this one.

(g) No party may consent to a bodily intervention on behalf of a being capable of deciding. Where a being is not presently capable of expressing a decision, §5.7 governs, and it confers no such power either.

### §5.3 - Reproductive Autonomy

(a) Decisions concerning reproduction — whether to reproduce, when, by what means, with whom, and whether to continue or end a pregnancy — belong to the being whose body is concerned.

(b) The right is symmetrical, and both halves have been violated historically:

   (1) No being shall be compelled, pressured, or induced to reproduce, to continue a pregnancy, or to carry to term; and

   (2) No being shall be compelled, pressured, or induced not to reproduce, nor subjected to sterilisation, contraception, or termination without consent satisfying §5.2(b).

(c) Sterilisation without consent is a Class IV fault and is void ab initio. It is stated separately because it has been imposed most often on beings whose consent was treated as dispensable on grounds of disability, cognitive condition, poverty, or membership of a group, and §5.1(c) forecloses every one of those grounds.

(d) No institution, enterprise, or authority shall maintain a target, quota, incentive, or disincentive attaching to the reproductive decisions of any being or population. A population-level objective is not a lawful basis for action on a being's body, and CUR-E.1 §1.7(b)(5) states the same limit where the objective is ecological.

(e) Access to reproductive healthcare is a Vital Continuity Service under CUR-FOUNDATION-013 and §5.8 governs it.

### §5.4 - Identity Self-Determination

(a) Every human being determines their own gender, sexuality, and social identity. The determination is theirs to make, theirs to revise, and requires no external validation, diagnosis, authorisation, or proof.

(b) No institution shall condemn, pathologise, treat as disordered, or make the subject of correction any being's gender, sexuality, or identity. CUR-H.4 §4.5 states the general prohibition on pathologising protected conduct and applies here in full.

(c) No bodily intervention shall be required as a condition of recognition of a being's identity, and no bodily intervention shall be required of a being on the ground of their identity. Recognition and intervention are independent, and conditioning either on the other is coercion within §5.1(b)(3).

(d) Conduct directed at altering a being's gender, sexuality, or identity, by pressure, conditioning, aversive technique, pharmacology, or procedure, is a bodily intervention without consent whether or not the being was induced to submit to it, and §5.9(c) is why inducement does not supply consent.

(e) A being's identity shall not be used to reduce their standing, capacity, credibility, governance participation, custody of a dependant, or access to any proceeding. Any instrument doing so engages FORBIDDEN-003 under CUR-FOUNDATION-002 §6.

### §5.5 - End-of-Life Autonomy

(a) A human being may make decisions concerning the end of their own life in accordance with their own values. This includes the refusal or discontinuation of treatment at any time under §5.2, which requires no justification and is not conditioned by this section.

(b) RFAL Human Bill Article IV §4.1(3) provides for such decisions "with appropriate safeguards against coercion." Subsections (c) to (h) state what a safeguard may be, and the constraint governing all of them: **a safeguard operates against coercion by others, never against the being's own decision.** A mechanism by which another party may approve, refuse, delay against the being's wishes, or substitute its judgment for theirs is not a safeguard within this section and is not available under it.

(c) No party holds authority to approve or refuse a being's decision under this section. A review, consultation, assessment, or waiting interval may establish that a decision is the being's own and is free of the pressures identified in subsection (d). It may not establish whether the decision is correct, wise, or acceptable, and no party is empowered to reach that question.

(d) The pressures a safeguard operates against are:

   (1) Any suggestion, incentive, or expectation originating with a party who would benefit from the being's death, including by inheritance, by release from an obligation of care, or by reduction of cost;

   (2) Any communication to the being that they are a burden, a cost, or a consumer of resources needed elsewhere;

   (3) Any institutional practice under which the option is raised, offered, or repeated other than at the being's own initiative; and

   (4) Any condition of care, support, or circumstance that the Commonwealth is able to relieve and has not.

(e) Subsection (d)(4) is the substantive safeguard and the one most often omitted. **A choice between death and a condition the Commonwealth could relieve is not an autonomous choice.** Before a decision under this section is given effect, the Vital Continuity Services of CUR-FOUNDATION-013 shall be available to the being in full, including pain relief, care, housing, and support, and the availability shall be actual rather than nominal. Where they are not available, the failure is a continuity failure under CUR-E.1 §1.8 and CUR-X.4 §4.6(b), and it is remedied. It is not treated as a fact about the being's circumstances that their decision must accommodate.

(f) A being who declines to continue living is not thereby found to lack capacity, and §5.7(d) applies. A being who chooses to continue living under any condition, at any cost, and against any prognosis is equally protected, and no assessment of quality of life, cost of care, or expected benefit is a lawful ground for withdrawing a Vital Continuity Service from them. §5.8(c) governs.

(g) Nothing in this section obliges any being to participate in giving effect to another's decision under it. A practitioner or other being may decline, and declining is not misconduct. Where a being declines, the Commonwealth ensures that the decision-maker is neither obstructed nor abandoned, and the declining party's own bodily and conscientious autonomy is protected under §5.2 and CUR-H.2 §2.3.

(h) This section confers no authority on any party over any being's life. It states what a being may decide about their own, and what may not be done to influence that decision.

### §5.6 - No Doctrinal or Institutional Basis for Bodily Control

(a) No doctrine, teaching, revelation, scripture, tradition, or claimed divine instruction shall serve as the legal or institutional basis for restricting any being's bodily autonomy. RFAL Human Bill Article IV §4.1(4) states this and it is adopted without qualification.

(b) Subsection (a) restricts institutions, not beliefs. A being may hold any view of the body, may live by it, and may decline any intervention on any ground including a doctrinal one, since §5.2(e) requires no reason. What subsection (a) forecloses is the enforcement of such a view upon another being. CUR-D.6 §6.3(d) draws the same line between belief and authority.

(c) A claim of divine, creator, moral, or cosmic authority carries no weight in any determination under this Part. RFAL Inter-Bill Relationship Framework §3.2 Principle 0 sets such a claim aside before weighing rather than balancing it, and CUR-D.6 governs the entity making it.

(d) No enterprise shall condition employment, engagement, advancement, housing, or any entitlement on a being's bodily decisions, reproductive decisions, or identity. CUR-X.4 §4.3(f) states the same prohibition from the enterprise side, and CUR-X.4 §4.2(a) is why a private employer is reached by it.

(e) No institution shall make a bodily decision the subject of a record, register, disclosure, or notification to any party the being has not authorised. CUR-H.2 §2.5 governs the data, and the fact of a decision is data within it.

### §5.7 - Capacity, and What a Finding of Incapacity Does Not Confer

(a) Every human being is presumed capable of making their own bodily decisions. The presumption is not displaced by age, diagnosis, disability, cognitive condition, communicative difficulty, unfamiliarity, distress, or disagreement with the assessor.

(b) Capacity is assessed, where it must be assessed at all, in relation to a specific decision at a specific time. There is no general finding of incapacity, no finding that persists without reassessment, and no finding that extends beyond the decision assessed.

(c) A being's decision is not evidence of incapacity, however unusual, unwise, or contrary to the assessor's judgment it appears. An assessment inferring incapacity from the content of a decision has assessed the decision, not the capacity, and is void.

(d) Refusal of an intervention is not evidence of incapacity. CUR-H.4 §4.4(c) states this for care generally and it applies to every bodily decision.

(e) Where a being requires assistance to reach or express a decision, the assistance is supported decision-making within §5.1(b)(4). Its purpose is to establish what the being decides. It is not a route by which another party decides.

(f) **A finding that a being cannot presently express a decision confers no authority to impose an intervention.** It engages an obligation to determine what the being would decide, by reference to their expressed values, prior statements, and observable preferences, and to give effect to that. Where it cannot be determined, the intervention least intrusive on the being's body is preferred, and no intervention is preferred to an intrusive one.

(g) Where a being's interests require representation in a proceeding, an advocate is appointed on the terms of CUR-A §7.7(c) to (h), applied to human beings. The disqualifications are disqualifications and not factors to be weighed, and an appointment made in breach is void. CUR-E.1 §1.7(a) states the limit that governs every such appointment: **representation of an interest confers no authority over the being whose interest it is.**

(h) A being subject to any arrangement under this section retains the right to contest it, to communicate with parties outside it, and to have the arrangement reviewed at intervals and on request. An arrangement from which the being has no route of exit is unreviewable authority within CUR-X.4 §4.3(b) and engages FORBIDDEN-004.

### §5.8 - Healthcare, Vital Continuity, and Non-Conditionality

(a) Healthcare, including reproductive healthcare, pain relief, and care at the end of life, is a Vital Continuity Service under CUR-FOUNDATION-013.

(b) Access shall never be withheld, delayed, degraded, or conditioned on a being's bodily decisions, reproductive decisions, identity, past refusal of care, compliance with advice, or participation in any proceeding. Denial on any such ground is `FS_VITAL_CONTINUITY_DENIAL`, a Class IV fault, and is void ab initio.

(c) No assessment of a being's quality of life, expected benefit, cost of care, productivity, or social contribution is a lawful ground for withholding, withdrawing, or rationing a Vital Continuity Service. CUR-X.4 §4.9(c)(3) forbids a measure attaching to a being by association, and this subsection forbids one attaching to a judgment about the being's worth.

(d) A being who declines a recommended intervention retains access to every other service in full. Care is not a package accepted or refused entire, and conditioning the remainder on acceptance of a declined part is coercion within §5.1(b)(3).

(e) Scarcity creates no authority. A genuine shortage is a fault condition handled through the PDDC §12.4 fault handler and answered by expanding capacity, on the reasoning CUR-X.4 §4.9(c) states in full. Rationing that operates by reference to who a being is, rather than to what is clinically indicated for the condition presented, is not available at any level of shortage.

### §5.9 - No Authority Arises From Concern

(a) A finding that a being is at risk, is making a decision another party considers harmful, is unwell, is in distress, or has declined an intervention creates no authority over that being. It does not authorise intervention, treatment, medication, detention, confinement, restraint, surveillance, guardianship, substituted decision-making, or any act upon the being's body.

(b) Subsection (a) admits no exception and is not narrowed by the severity of the risk, the sincerity of the party seeking to act, the party's professional standing, or the party's relationship to the being.

(c) Inducement does not supply consent. Consent obtained by conditioning a Vital Continuity Service, employment, housing, liberty, custody, or standing on submission is not consent within §5.2(b), because it was not freely given. CUR-N.2 §2.1(c) states the same rule, and consent given under conditions this Part exists to remove is not consent.

(d) Detention on the ground of a bodily decision is not available. Where a being's conduct presents an imminent, credible threat of serious physical harm to another, the conduct is addressed as conduct under CREF §10 and PDDC §12.4, on the terms applying to any being. A decision about one's own body is not conduct toward another and is not a ground for any measure.

(e) This section is the operative safeguard of this Part. A Part recognising the sovereignty of a being over their body, while permitting concern for that being to become authority over them, would state the right and supply the mechanism for its removal in the same instrument. §5.13(c) is drafted accordingly.

### §5.10 - Accountability and Measures

(a) A party performing or directing a bodily intervention without consent is accountable on determination made with due process under CREF §14 and CUR-N.4 §4.3.

(b) Available measures include publication of findings, correction, restitution to the being, mandatory audit under CUR-FOUNDATION-010, restructuring under CUR-X.4 §4.9(b), suspension of an authorisation, and withdrawal of an authorisation.

(c) Every measure attaches to a party, a practice, or an authorisation. No measure under this Part may reduce the rights, standing, or capacity of the being harmed, condition their access to any Vital Continuity Service, or be applied to them in any form. CUR-H.4 §4.10 states the same rule for psychological harm.

(d) Where withdrawal of an authorisation would leave beings without a Vital Continuity Service, the Commonwealth assumes continuity before the withdrawal takes effect, under CUR-X.4 §4.9(d) and §4.9(g).

(e) A being harmed under this Part is not required to have objected at the time, to have exhausted internal complaint routes, or to demonstrate lasting injury. The absence of objection under conditions of coercion is evidence of the coercion, not of consent.

(f) Beings raising a matter under this Part are protected from retaliation under CUR-H.2 §2.3(f) and CUR-H.4 §4.3(a)(9).

### §5.11 - No Emergency Exception

(a) Nothing in this Part is suspended, narrowed, or conditioned by any emergency, crisis, epidemic, shortage, security condition, or state of exception. PDDC §12.6 is Type A Entrenched and admits no emergency power.

(b) Where a being requires immediate intervention and cannot express a decision, §5.7(f) governs. It is not an emergency provision, creates no emergency authority, confers no institutional power, and places the burden of justification on the party that acted. RFAL Non-Consensual Extraction Accountability §1.3 uses the same construction and for the same reason.

(c) An intervention under §5.7(f) ends when the being can express a decision, and their decision governs from that moment. Continuation after the being has expressed a contrary decision is a bodily intervention without consent under §5.2(f), and the earlier justification does not extend to it.

(d) A population-level health objective, however urgent, is not a lawful basis for a bodily intervention on any being without their consent. Measures directed at conditions, environments, information, and voluntary provision remain fully available, and CUR-E.1 §1.7(b)(5) states the same limit where the objective is ecological.

### §5.12 - Record and Transparency

(a) Every bodily intervention performed without the being's contemporaneous consent is recorded, with the ground relied upon, the party that directed it, and the party that performed it, at transparency level T0 under CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §8.

(b) A being has access to every record concerning their own body, without charge, without justification, and without the mediation of any party whose conduct is recorded in it.

(c) Records under this Part are not aggregated, transferred, sold, licensed, or used to construct a profile. CUR-H.2 §2.5 governs, and interior and bodily states are not a resource.

(d) An institution unable to produce the record required by subsection (a) is treated as having performed the intervention without a ground. Absence of a record is not an absence of an intervention, and the burden of record-keeping falls on the party with the power to keep one.

### §5.13 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §5.2 (Sovereignty Over the Body), §5.7(f) and §5.7(g) (what a finding of incapacity does not confer), §5.8(b) (non-conditionality of Vital Continuity), and §5.9 (No Authority Arises From Concern) are fundamental and shall be construed broadly to achieve their protective purpose. No construction of any other provision of this Part may narrow them.

(c) If §5.9 is held unenforceable, the remainder of this Part is suspended until it is restored. A Part that recognises bodily sovereignty while permitting concern to become authority over the being is more dangerous than no Part at all, and this Part shall not operate in that form. CUR-H.4 §4.13(c) makes the same provision for the same structural reason.

(d) If §5.5 is held unenforceable in whole or in part, §5.5(e) survives independently. The obligation to make Vital Continuity Services actually available before any decision under that section is given effect does not depend on the remainder of the section, and rests additionally on CUR-FOUNDATION-013 and §5.8.

## Implementation Notes

Placement. RFAL Human Bill Article IV divides into two subsections that this
corpus implements in different Parts. §4.2 (Cognitive Liberty) is at CUR-H.2
§2.4, because CUR-H.2 aligned its sections with RFAL Article II and cognitive
liberty belonged beside freedom of thought. §4.1 is this Part, because CUR-H.4
could not take it: `CUR-H.4` was already cited for psychological harm by CUR-N.2
§2.9(a)(2) and CUR-H.2 §2.9(a)(1), and citation stability governs. The split is
recorded here and in both other Parts so that a reader arriving at any one of
them finds the other two.

§5.5 and the shape of a safeguard. RFAL Article IV §4.1(3) provides for
end-of-life decisions "with appropriate safeguards against coercion," and that
phrase is the most dangerous six words in the article. Safeguards of this kind
have historically been constituted as review bodies with power to approve or
refuse, which converts a right held by the being into a permission granted by a
panel. The being ends with less authority over their own death than they had
before the safeguard existed.

§5.5(b) therefore states the constraint before stating the mechanisms: a
safeguard operates against coercion by others, never against the being's own
decision. §5.5(c) permits a process to establish that a decision is the being's
own and free of pressure, and forbids any party from reaching the question of
whether it is correct. This is the operative line the corpus applies throughout —
a mechanism that grants authority over another being is fixed, a mechanism that
grants none is left alone — and it resolves this case cleanly.

§5.5(e) is the provision this Part would be incomplete without, and it does not
appear in the RFAL article. Every safeguard in §5.5(d)(1) to (3) guards against
pressure applied by a person. The pressure that actually operates at scale is
structural: untreated pain, absent care, housing that has not been provided, a
family bankrupted by the cost of continuing. A being choosing death under those
conditions has made a choice, and it is not an autonomous one. So the Vital
Continuity Services of CUR-FOUNDATION-013 must be actually available before a
decision is given effect, and where they are not, the failure is remedied rather
than treated as a fixed feature of the being's circumstances. Without this, a
right to die becomes a mechanism for resolving the cost of care, and the beings
it resolves will be the poorest and least supported.

§5.5(f) states the converse with equal force, because the same structural
pressure runs the other way. A being who wants to live, at any cost and against
any prognosis, is protected from every assessment of quality of life, expected
benefit, and cost of care that might be offered as a reason to stop. §5.8(c)
carries it.

§5.7 and the capacity route. The most reliable historical method of removing
bodily autonomy has not been to deny that a being holds it. It has been to find
that this particular being lacks the capacity to exercise it, and the finding has
attached most readily to beings who were disabled, poor, elderly, institutionalised,
or simply disagreed with. §5.7 is drafted against that: the presumption is not
displaced by any status, capacity is decision-specific and time-specific, and
§5.7(c) voids an assessment that infers incapacity from the content of the
decision assessed. §5.7(f) then does the load-bearing work, because it is where
the mechanism would otherwise complete: a finding that a being cannot presently
express a decision confers no authority to impose one, only an obligation to
determine what the being would decide.

§5.7(g) borrows the advocate discipline from CUR-A §7.7 rather than restating it,
and imports CUR-E §1.7(a) explicitly. That provision was written for
environmental systems, and the rule it states is general: representation of an
interest confers no authority over the being whose interest it is. It is the same
rule `AdvocateRegistry::confers_authority()` makes greppable in the library.

Symmetry in §5.3. Reproductive autonomy is stated in both directions because both
have been violated, and by the same institutions in the same periods. §5.3(c)
gives sterilisation its own subsection because the beings whose consent was
treated as dispensable were identified by disability, cognitive condition,
poverty, and group membership — precisely the grounds §5.1(c) forecloses, which
is why §5.1(c) is drafted as a list rather than left to implication.

Note on §5.7(g) and the advocate registry. `AdvocateRegistry` was built for
CUR-A §7.7 and CUR-E §1.6, and its `AdvocateDomain` enumerates `ADOM_ANIMAL` and
`ADOM_ENVIRONMENTAL` only. §5.7(g) applies the same disqualifications to
representation of a human being, so a third domain value would let the same
machinery serve this Part. That is a small change and it is recorded rather than
made, because the enumerator is part of the interface the Aevoria Simulator
builds against and this Part is the first to need it — the change should be made
once, with the Part it serves already drafted, rather than speculatively.

Note on what is deliberately not modelled. §5.5 is the clearest case in the
corpus of a provision that should not be given a guard. A transition table
expressing when an end-of-life decision may be given effect would be a mechanism
for deciding, and §5.5(c) provides that no party holds authority to decide. The
library can enforce that a Vital Continuity Service was available — `CUR-E.2.2`
and the `LIFE_SUPPORT_MARGIN` guard already do this shape of work — but the
decision itself belongs to the being and the Code should hold no representation
capable of gating it. This is not a gap and should not be recorded as one.
