## PART 2 - FREEDOM OF THOUGHT, CONSCIENCE, AND INQUIRY

### §2.1 - Scope and Definitions

(a) This Part governs the interior cognitive life of human beings within Commonwealth jurisdiction: what a human may think, believe, doubt, revise, and abandon, and what may be done to a human on account of it.

(b) For the purposes of this Part:

   (1) Interior means the thoughts, beliefs, convictions, doubts, memories, emotions, imaginings, and subjective states of a being, together with the processes by which they are formed and revised. The interior includes states the being has never expressed and never intends to express.

   (2) Cognitive Sovereignty means a being's exclusive authority over access to and alteration of its own interior. It is held by the being alone and is not delegable to, transferable to, or exercisable by any institution.

   (3) Belief includes religious, philosophical, political, cosmological, scientific, and moral positions, and the absence of any such position.

   (4) Algorithmic Determination means any decision, score, ranking, classification, prediction, or recommendation produced wholly or partly by an automated system, where that output is relied upon in reaching an outcome affecting a human being.

   (5) Material Effect means an effect on a person's access to work, housing, movement, credit, education, healthcare, governance participation, community membership, family relationship, or standing before any institution.

(c) Consent under this Part shall satisfy every element required by CUR-N.2 §2.1(c): explicit, informed, ongoing, revocable, and non-coerced. Consent obtained under threat of any consequence within subsection (b)(5) is coerced and therefore void.

(d) This Part confers no precedence on human interests in any cross-domain matter. It states what is owed to human beings; it does not rank them above other recognised lifeforms, and an argument that it does is void under CUR-X §3.2(d). The parallel protections of other domains are identified in §2.10.

(e) Nothing in this Part is contingent on citizenship, membership category, compliance state, FSM operational state, contribution, or usefulness. It applies to every human being within jurisdiction, without qualification.

### §2.2 - The Absolute Right to Think

(a) Every human being holds an absolute and inviolable right to cognitive sovereignty over their own interior.

(b) That right includes:

   (1) To think freely — to form, hold, revise, and abandon any belief, conviction, worldview, or cosmological position without external interference;

   (2) To question any claim — including any claim of institutional authority, doctrinal orthodoxy, received wisdom, scientific consensus, or Commonwealth policy;

   (3) To follow evidence — to pursue truth through observation, reason, science, philosophy, and lived experience, including where the conclusions conflict with established doctrine;

   (4) To change their mind — to revise any previously held belief without penalty, shame, or loss of standing; and

   (5) To hold a belief in private and to decline to disclose it.

(c) No institution, sacred or secular, holds jurisdiction over the interior of a human mind. This includes Commonwealth institutions. No governance body, court, tribunal, assembly, council, registry, or automated system acquires such jurisdiction by any procedure, at any threshold, by any majority.

(d) The right in this section is non-derogable. It is protected under PDDC §10.1(b) as freedom from non-consensual modification of cognitive substrate, and remains fully operative during Protected Mode under PDDC §10.3(a). No declaration of any form may serve as a predicate for limiting it (PDDC §10.3(d)).

(e) An instrument purporting to limit, suspend, condition, or narrow this section is an attempted forbidden transition under PDDC §12.3, engaging FORBIDDEN-003 (Rights Suspension) under CUR-FOUNDATION-002 §6. It is void ab initio and triggers the fault handler protocol of PDDC §12.4 immediately.

(f) The right is not conditional on the content of the thought. A belief that is mistaken, unpopular, offensive, heretical, seditious, or demonstrably false is protected to exactly the same extent as one that is none of those things. Protection that extends only to acceptable thought is not protection of thought.

(g) Subsection (f) governs the interior alone. It confers no immunity for conduct, and the boundary between the two is stated in §2.7.

(h) Non-consensual access to a human interior by telepathic or psychic means is a violation of this section and is separately prohibited by CUR-N.2 §2.2. The prohibitions are cumulative under §2.9 and CUR-X §3.4(c).

### §2.3 - Freedom of Conscience, Truth-Telling, and Dissent

(a) Every human being has the right to follow conscience — to act in accordance with their own moral judgment, formed through reason, experience, and reflection.

(b) Every human being has the right to speak truth — to express what they genuinely believe to be true, based on evidence and honest inquiry, without institutional retaliation.

(c) Every human being has the right to leave — to depart from any religious, ideological, institutional, or governance community without penalty, coercion, or loss of any right guaranteed under PDDC §10.1 or the Rights for All Life Charter. The right to leave is non-derogable under the Eternity Clause and is not satisfied by a departure that costs the being their standing, family, shelter, or continuity of life.

(d) No human being shall be subjected to:

   (1) Doctrinal punishment for conscience — being declared sinful, damned, heretical, disloyal, or condemned for holding, expressing, or acting upon honest conviction;

   (2) Coercive existential threat — threats of eternal punishment, post-mortem suffering, cosmic consequence, or annihilation used as a mechanism of behavioural control. Belief in such consequences, freely held, is protected under §2.2. Its use to coerce another being is not;

   (3) Expulsion as a weapon — removal from community, family, or social network imposed as punishment for questioning, revising, or leaving a tradition. A community may define its own boundaries; it may not use them to inflict harm designed to compel belief or silence dissent;

   (4) Blasphemy as a category of harm — legal, social, institutional, or economic penalty for speech questioning, critiquing, or rejecting any religious or ideological claim, figure, text, or institution;

   (5) Childhood formation without adult recourse — any binding of an adult to doctrine accepted as a child. Beliefs formed under authority in childhood do not bind the adult that child becomes; or

   (6) Institutional gatekeeping of truth — any claim of exclusive authority over moral truth, cosmological fact, or the definition of human worth, asserted in a forum where the claim is used to restrict the rights of others or suppress inquiry.

(e) Truth-teller's protection. A person who speaks what they genuinely believe to be true, on the basis of evidence, reason, experience, or honest reflection, is protected from retaliation by any institution whose authority is challenged by that statement. This protection extends in particular to researchers whose findings conflict with doctrine, former adherents describing their reasons for leaving, young people expressing doubt within families or communities where doubt is punished, and any person naming harm done in the name of an institution, sacred or secular.

(f) Right to dissent. Every human being has the right to dissent from governance policy, institutional practice, corporate conduct, cultural norm, and doctrine. This right includes peaceful assembly and protest, conscientious objection to participation in activity violating the person's moral convictions, protected disclosure of institutional wrongdoing, and nonviolent resistance to unjust instruments. Dissent is not disloyalty, and no institution may treat it as such.

(g) Retaliation for an act protected by this section is a Class III fault under CUR-FOUNDATION-002 §5. Where retaliation is undertaken by a Commonwealth institution against a person exercising subsection (f), it is a Class IV fault and grounds for review under CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §15.

(h) What this section does not do. This section does not prohibit belief in any deity or doctrine, restrict the free exercise of religion in any voluntary context, judge the truth or falsity of any theological claim, prevent a community from defining its own doctrine or membership criteria, or prevent a parent from raising a child within a tradition. It prohibits the use of claimed authority — divine, moral, institutional, or algorithmic — to coerce, punish, or silence.

(i) Where the coercion is exercised under a claim of divine or creator authority, CUR-D.6 applies concurrently. A claim of divine status confers no exemption from this section and is void as a defence under CUR-N.2 §2.2(b)(1).

### §2.4 - Cognitive Liberty and Cognitive Privacy

(a) Right to unmodified cognition. No human being shall be required to accept cognitive modification, neural implant, pharmacological alteration, or algorithmic influence as a condition of employment, housing, education, citizenship, governance participation, or access to any service.

(b) Right to cognitive augmentation. No human being shall be prohibited from voluntarily augmenting their own cognitive capabilities, provided the augmentation does not violate the rights of another being. Refusal and acceptance are equally protected, and neither may be made the basis of differential treatment.

(c) Right to cognitive privacy. No institution shall access, monitor, record, model, or influence a person's neural or cognitive activity without consent satisfying §2.1(c). This applies to direct neural interface, inferential modelling from behavioural data, and every intermediate method.

(d) Non-consensual modification of a human being's cognitive substrate is a forbidden transition under PDDC §12.3(a)(4). It is void ab initio, is a Class IV fault, and is not rendered permissible by any purpose, consent of a third party, institutional authorisation, or claimed benefit to the person modified.

(e) The means are immaterial. Modification or surveillance of the interior is prohibited to the same extent whether accomplished by neural interface, pharmacology, conditioning, algorithmic manipulation, or telepathic or psychic means. CUR-N.2 §2.2(b)(6) states the same rule for the non-human cognitive actor domain, and the two provisions are read together.

(f) Consent to augmentation, monitoring, or interface is revocable at any time under §2.1(c). An augmentation that cannot be declined without loss of employment, housing, or standing was not consented to, and an interface that cannot be disconnected on request is a continuing violation of subsection (c) for as long as it persists.

(g) Data derived from a person's cognitive or neural activity is subject to §2.5 in full, and additionally may not be transferred, sold, licensed, or aggregated. Interior states are not a resource, and treating them as one engages FORBIDDEN-001 under CUR-FOUNDATION-002 §6.

### §2.5 - Protection from Algorithmic Condemnation

(a) This section governs algorithmic determinations, as defined in §2.1(b)(4), that produce a material effect as defined in §2.1(b)(5). Algorithmic condemnation is punishment without trial, and the Commonwealth does not recognise it. The section states the conditions under which an automated determination may bear on a human being at all.

(b) Right to know the accuser. No person shall be subject to an algorithmic determination producing a material effect without disclosure of:

   (1) The specific system that produced the determination, by name, version, and operator;

   (2) The entity that deployed it;

   (3) The factors that most significantly influenced the determination; and

   (4) The human authority, by name and title, responsible for the system's deployment and oversight.

(c) Right to explanation. Every person materially affected by an algorithmic determination has the right to an explanation that is in plain language rather than technical or legal obfuscation, specific to that person rather than a generic description of the system, actionable in identifying what could change the outcome, and provided within thirty (30) days of request. A system that cannot explain its determinations is not fit to make them, and a determination that cannot be explained shall not be relied upon.

(d) Right to be seen by human eyes. Every person materially affected by an algorithmic determination has the right to appeal to a human being who holds authority to override the determination, is identified by name and title, provides written reasoning, and is accountable through ordinary institutional channels. Referral of the appeal to the same or another automated system does not satisfy this subsection.

(e) Right to correction. Where an algorithmic determination is found to be erroneous, the affected person has the right to:

   (1) Immediate reversal of the determination;

   (2) Correction of every record that propagated the error, including records held by third parties to whom the determination was transmitted;

   (3) Notice to every party that received the erroneous determination; and

   (4) Written confirmation of the correction, provided to the person.

   (5) No person shall suffer retaliation, disadvantage, or adverse inference for reporting a suspected error, seeking correction, or exercising any right under this section. A report made in good faith is protected under §2.3(e) whether or not the determination is ultimately found to be erroneous.

   (6) An error that cannot be corrected is not an error but a permanent penalty. No automated system may impose a permanent penalty, and a determination whose effects cannot be reversed shall not be issued.

(f) Right to be forgotten by machines. No person shall be permanently indexed, scored, profiled, or tracked by any system without consent satisfying §2.1(c), a working mechanism for complete deletion of the data held, and a sunset provision requiring periodic renewal of consent. Consent that never expires is not ongoing within the meaning of §2.1(c).

(g) Prohibition on secret scoring. No entity shall assign to any person a secret, unappealable score governing access to any matter listed in §2.1(b)(5). Any scoring system materially affecting a person shall be disclosed to that person, explained in terms the person can understand, correctable where based on erroneous information, and appealable to a human authority under subsection (d).

(h) Vital Continuity is never gated. No algorithmic determination, score, classification, compliance standing, or automated assessment of any kind may be used to withhold, delay, degrade, ration, or condition access to a Vital Continuity Service for any human being. This holds in every compliance state, during any investigation, appeal, or pending proceeding, and regardless of the accuracy of the determination. Withholding a Vital Continuity Service is a Class IV fault under CREF §6 and a forbidden transition under CUR-FOUNDATION-013, and no automated finding creates an exception. CUR-FOUNDATION-003 §11 states the same rule for the Vital Continuity Index, whose scores may not be used to deny access to constitutionally guaranteed services.

(i) An algorithmic determination that cannot be appealed to a human authority is an exercise of unreviewable authority, and engages FORBIDDEN-004 under CUR-FOUNDATION-002 §6. This is so whether the unreviewability arises from design, from proprietary claim, from institutional practice, or from the absence of any identified human accountable for the system.

(j) Proprietary claim is not a defence. An operator may not withhold disclosure required by subsections (b), (c), or (g) on the ground that the system, its weights, its training data, or its logic are commercially confidential. An entity unwilling to explain a determination may decline to make it.

(k) Every algorithmic determination producing a material effect, every appeal under subsection (d), and every correction under subsection (e) is a constitutional event under CUR-FOUNDATION-005 and shall be logged with the actor, the affected person, the system relied upon, the human authority, and the outcome, as required by CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §14. A determination that is not recorded did not lawfully occur, and reliance on an unrecorded determination is a Class II fault.

(l) Nothing in this section legitimises the allocation of any Vital Continuity Service by score, ranking, or assessment of merit, need, or desert. Subsections (b) through (k) govern determinations that are permissible in the first place; subsection (h) identifies the class that is not.

### §2.6 - No Emergency Exception

(a) The Aevoric Commonwealth does not recognise emergency declarations, emergency authority, or any functional equivalent. PDDC §12.6 is absolute and Type A Entrenched, and §12.6(e) renders any instrument invoking emergency authority void ab initio.

(b) Accordingly, no assertion of national security, public safety, public morality, social harmony, doctrinal integrity, systemic risk, or necessity narrows §2.2, §2.4, or §2.5(h). Those provisions have no exception clause, and none may be added by construction.

(c) Where a condition genuinely requires action, the available instruments are protective actions under CREF §10 and the fault handler protocol of PDDC §12.4. Both are limited to the minimum scope necessary and neither suspends a constitutional right, expands any authority beyond its ordinary mandate, or bypasses due process. Urgency changes the sequence of steps; it never changes who holds authority.

(d) A limitation on expression that is otherwise available under §2.7 does not become available in wider form because circumstances are described as urgent, exceptional, or unprecedented.

### §2.7 - Thought, Expression, and the Boundary Between Them

(a) Thought is non-derogable. Expression is protected but derogable under PDDC §10.2(b), subject to narrow, evidence-based, democratically authorised limitation under strict Constitutional Court scrutiny. This Part does not disturb that structure, and states where the boundary falls.

(b) No limitation on expression may be used to reach the interior. A limitation is void where its purpose or effect is to deter, detect, punish, or alter what a person thinks, as distinct from what a person does. In particular:

   (1) Inferring a belief from conduct and penalising the belief so inferred is a violation of §2.2, whatever the conduct;

   (2) Requiring a person to disclose, affirm, disavow, or recant a belief as a condition of any benefit, office, service, or standing is a violation of §2.2(b)(5) and §2.3(a); and

   (3) Compelling speech is a limitation on thought, not merely on expression, and is not available under PDDC §10.2(b) at any level of scrutiny.

(c) The following are never valid grounds for limiting expression under PDDC §10.2(b): that the expression is offensive, blasphemous, heretical, doctrinally erroneous, unpopular, embarrassing to an institution, critical of Commonwealth policy, or contrary to consensus.

(d) The operative floor for strict scrutiny is direct and immediate incitement to physical violence against specific persons. A limitation not meeting that standard does not satisfy strict scrutiny under PDDC §10.2, and a Constitutional Court finding to the contrary is reviewable under CREF §15.

(e) Subsection (d) states a floor, not a grant. Meeting it establishes only that a limitation may be considered; it does not establish that the limitation is proportionate, necessary, or authorised.

### §2.8 - Enforcement, Standing, and Audit

(a) Any human being may bring a claim under this Part through the Constitutional Complaint and Review Process under CREF §7, submitted through a CAPS-compliant message under CUR-FOUNDATION-006. Rights claims are heard on a priority basis under PDDC §10.4(c), and no filing fee or membership requirement may restrict access under PDDC §10.4(d).

(b) A claim under §2.5 may be brought by the person materially affected, and additionally by any person subject to the same system on the same basis, since the harm of secret scoring is not always visible to the individual scored.

(c) Remedies are those available under CREF §12: restoration, correction, compensation, reinstatement, public disclosure, and institutional reform. Restoration of rights precedes assignment of fault wherever reasonably possible.

(d) A violation of §2.2, §2.4(d), or §2.5(h) is a Class IV fault and triggers the PDDC §12.4 fault handler on detection, without a prior finding of intent. A violation of §2.3 or of the remaining subsections of §2.5 is a Class III fault.

(e) Intent is not an element of a violation of §2.4(d) or §2.5(h). Intent, malice, and recklessness are aggravating factors, on the same footing as CUR-N.2 §2.11(b)(3).

(f) No violation of this Part is excused on the ground that the affected person did not object, did not read the disclosure, did not appeal, accepted terms of service, was unaware of the system, or delayed in bringing the claim. The reasoning rejected by CUR-N.2 §2.11 is rejected here in the same terms and for the same reason: the burden of respect lies with the actor, never with the person acted upon.

### §2.9 - No Prejudice to Other Claims

(a) A violation of this Part does not preclude additional accountability under:

   (1) Other Parts of CUR-H, including CUR-H.4 for psychological harm;

   (2) Other CUR domains, including CUR-N.2 for telepathic invasion, CUR-D.6 for coercive worship, and CUR-S where the system relied upon is itself a recognised entity;

   (3) Civil or criminal accountability under existing human legal systems, where applicable; and

   (4) Traditional or Indigenous legal frameworks, where applicable and consistent with cognitive sovereignty.

(b) Accountability under more than one provision is cumulative under CUR-X §3.4(c), subject to the proportionality requirement of CUR-X §3.9(c).

### §2.11 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §2.2 (The Absolute Right to Think), §2.4(d) (non-consensual modification), and §2.5(h) (Vital Continuity is never gated) are fundamental and shall be construed broadly to achieve their protective purpose. No construction of any other provision of this Part may narrow them.

Divergence recorded rather than resolved. RFAL Article II frames protection of
speech in near-absolute terms, while PDDC §10.2(b) makes freedom of expression
derogable under strict Constitutional Court scrutiny. Both instruments treat
thought itself as non-derogable, so the divergence concerns expression only.
§2.7 states the Commonwealth position: the PDDC structure governs within
Commonwealth jurisdiction, and §2.7(d) adopts the RFAL §6.3(2) standard as the
operative floor for strict scrutiny, which is the narrowest reading the PDDC text
admits. This is a substantive choice and is disclosed here rather than made
silently.

Additions beyond both source instruments. §2.5(h) has no counterpart in RFAL
Article II. It is required by CUR-FOUNDATION-013 and by the constraint in
`titles/README.md` that no title may permit a Vital Continuity Service to be
withheld in any compliance state. RFAL Section 2.5 assumes a market in which
credit, housing, and employment are allocated by score; the Commonwealth's
post-scarcity model does not allocate Vital Continuity Services that way at all,
and §2.5(l) makes clear that the procedural protections of §2.5(b) through (k)
are not an implied licence to score access to them.

Observation from drafting. `FS_CITIZEN_OWNERSHIP` is declared in the
`ForbiddenState` enum in `include/cur/cur_state.h` but no regulation in the
baseline set declares it, and no transition can produce it. FORBIDDEN-001 is
carried in practice by `CUR-PDDC.12.3a3` through `FS_OWNED_DISPOSABLE`, which
cites both PDDC §12.3(a)(3) and FORBIDDEN-001. The unused enumerator is
harmless but reads as coverage that is not there. It is noted here rather than
changed, since altering the enum is a library matter and not a drafting one.

Note on `libcur` and `DOMAIN_HUMAN`. The baseline regulation set carries rules
for CUR-N, CUR-S, and CUR-E, and none for CUR-H. That is not an oversight in the
library: §2.2 and §2.3 protect an interior the FSM has no representation of, and
a guard that purported to evaluate whether a belief had been coerced would be
worse than no guard at all. What the library does enforce is the structural
consequence — the forbidden states listed above are reachable from no transition,
which is where §2.2(e), §2.4(d), and §2.5(h) actually bind. A future
`CUR-H.2.5` regulation covering algorithmic determinations is tractable, because
disclosure, appeal availability, and human-authority identification are
observable properties of an event rather than of an interior. It is deferred
rather than half-implemented.
