- **Referenced by:** HIM §9

Relationship to CUR-H.6 and CUR-H.7. Those Parts govern conduct that kills,
injures, enslaves, endangers, or sexually violates a being, and they apply here
in full and independently — this Part does not create a lesser or substitute
category for conduct they already reach. What this Part adds is threefold:
accountability for organising the capacity for that conduct before it occurs
(§8.3); an operative standard for the one point at which expression itself
becomes conduct (§8.4); and naming, as a fact on the record, why a being was
targeted where the reason was a characteristic within CUR-H.7 §7.2(c) (§8.5).
§8.6 through §8.8 add a fourth and structurally different concern: protecting
the Commonwealth's own process of self-governance from being seized,
overturned, or coerced by organised force — and refusing, in the same breath,
to let the response to that threat become the seizure it exists to prevent.

Note on the hardest question this Part answers. Every provision so far in this
domain has drawn its line at conduct rather than belief, and this Part is the
one most under pressure to draw it somewhere else — because the conduct it
addresses is organised specifically around belief, and it would be simple to
reach the belief instead of waiting for the conduct. §8.2 refuses that
simplification directly. It is not refused out of sympathy for the belief in
question. It is refused because a Code empowered to act on belief, once built,
does not stay pointed at the belief its builders had in mind — and the being it
would eventually reach first is whichever one currently holds the least power to
stop it.

Note on drafting ahead of RFAL. Unlike every other Part in this domain, this
Part implements no single RFAL Article written for its subject; the Human Bill
of Rights does not yet contain a provision addressed, as its own subject, to
organised political violence, hate-motivated targeting, or the defence of
self-governance against seizure by force. `titles/README.md` records the
precedent for drafting in this position: CUR-E.1 was drafted against a
specification for an RFAL Ecosystem Bill of Rights that did not yet exist, and
the specification it recorded was later compared, provision by provision,
against the bill RFAL actually adopted. This Part follows the same discipline.
What it draws on already exists and is cited throughout — RFAL Article VI
§6.3(2)'s incitement floor, RFAL Article I §1.3's rejection of worth
hierarchies, and PDDC §2.5, §11.1, and §13.1 — but no RFAL Article yet states
that organised violence and hate-motivated targeting are wrongs in themselves
rather than aggravations of wrongs stated elsewhere. When RFAL adopts one, this
Part's Implementation Notes will record the comparison the way CUR-E.1 §1.11
did, rather than treating the gap as silently closed.

## PART 8 - ORGANISED VIOLENCE, INCITEMENT, AND THE DEFENCE OF SELF-GOVERNANCE

### §8.1 - Scope and Definitions

(a) This Part governs conduct that organises, arms, incites, or attempts
violence against a being or group because of who they are, or against the
Commonwealth's own process of self-governance, by force or the organised threat
of it.

(b) For the purposes of this Part:

   (1) Organised Violence means recruiting into, arming, training, financing,
   or directing a group where the group's object, or a means by which it
   pursues its object, is conduct within CUR-H.6 or CUR-H.7.

   (2) Incitement means direct and immediate exhortation to violence against
   specific persons or an identifiable group, of a kind and immediacy that
   would lead a reasonable being to act on it. RFAL Human Bill of Rights
   Article VI §6.3(2) states this as the sole ground on which expression may be
   limited at all, and CUR-H.2 §2.7(d) adopts it as the operative floor for
   strict scrutiny.

   (3) Bias Motive means that a party selected a being as a target of conduct
   within CUR-H.6 or CUR-H.7 because of a characteristic listed in CUR-H.7
   §7.2(c).

   (4) Attempted Seizure of Self-Governance means the use, or organised threat,
   of force to prevent, obstruct, overturn, or bypass a vote, deliberation, or
   governance process this Constitution establishes; to install, retain, or
   remove any officeholder other than through such a process; or to compel a
   governance body to act or refrain from acting under threat of violence.

   (5) Affiliation means membership in, sympathy with, or adherence to a
   belief, movement, or ideology, without more. It is defined here because
   §8.2 turns on the distinction between it and conduct.

(c) This Part reaches human beings and institutions within jurisdiction.
Parallel protections and prohibitions extend to other domains under CUR-X
§3.4(c) and are not restated here.

(d) The protection this Part affords is not conditional on citizenship,
membership, compliance state, contribution, or usefulness, and is held by every
being within jurisdiction without qualification, including a party responsible
under this Part, per CUR-H.7 §7.11.

### §8.2 - The Floor Between Belief and Conduct

(a) Affiliation is not conduct. No being shall be subject to any measure under
this Part, or any other Part of this Code, for affiliation alone. CUR-H.2
§2.2(c) and §2.2(f) state the same rule for belief generally, and PDDC §11.1(d)
states it for the Commonwealth's anti-discrimination architecture: ideological
orientation is protected, "except where conduct flowing from such orientation
violates this Constitution."

(b) This Part reaches only organising, arming, training, financing, directing,
inciting, or attempting; it does not reach believing, preaching, or advocating,
however abhorrent. Advocacy for a political or social outcome, including one
this Code would refuse to enact, is protected under CUR-H.2 §2.3(f) and RFAL
Article II §2.4 unless it crosses into §8.4 incitement.

(c) A determination under this Part shall identify the specific conduct
establishing it. A determination that rests on a party's beliefs, associations,
or membership, without conduct within §8.3, §8.4, §8.5, or §8.6, is void as to
that party.

(d) Subsection (c) is the same discipline PDDC §11.1(d) states for
discrimination generally, applied to the gravest conduct this domain reaches.
Ideological orientation is not itself a ground; only conduct flowing from it
that itself violates this Constitution is reachable. This Part draws its line
at the same place, because a title that reached belief instead of conduct would
be the instrument this whole corpus exists to foreclose, turned to whichever
population is unpopular at the time it is invoked.

### §8.3 - Organising Violence

(a) No being or institution shall recruit into, arm, train, finance, or direct
a group where the group's object, or a means by which it pursues its object, is
conduct within CUR-H.6 or CUR-H.7.

(b) Subsection (a) reaches participation at the level of organising the
capacity for violence — recruiting, arming, training, financing, directing —
and not membership, sympathy, or presence alone, under §8.2(a).

(c) It is not a defence that the violence organised for has not yet occurred,
that no specific target had been named, that the being charged did not
personally intend to carry out the conduct, or that the being charged holds a
position of nominal rather than operational authority within the group, where
the conduct in subsection (a) is shown.

(d) Where the group's conduct or attempted conduct causes death, injury,
endangerment, or enslavement, CUR-H.6 and CUR-H.7 apply in full and
independently, under CUR-X §3.4(c). This section is not a lesser or substitute
charge; it reaches the organising itself, whether or not the underlying conduct
is completed.

(e) A being who withdraws from a group's organisation of violence before the
conduct organised for occurs, and who does not participate in the conduct when
it occurs, is accountable under this section for their own organising conduct
up to withdrawal and not for conduct occurring after it. This does not extend
to a being who continues to arm, finance, or direct the group after purporting
to withdraw.

### §8.4 - Incitement

(a) No being or institution shall directly and immediately incite a being or
beings to commit conduct within CUR-H.6 or CUR-H.7 against specific persons or
an identifiable group. This section states the standard RFAL Article VI
§6.3(2) sets as the sole ground on which expression may be limited at all, and
which CUR-H.2 §2.7(d) adopts as the operative floor for strict scrutiny.

(b) Incitement requires all of the following, and the absence of any one is
fatal to a determination under this section:

   (1) A call to specific violent conduct, not general grievance, criticism, or
   advocacy for change however forceful;

   (2) Directed at specific persons or an identifiable group, not a diffuse or
   hypothetical target; and

   (3) Of a directness and immediacy such that a reasonable being hearing it
   would be moved to act on it now, rather than to consider, debate, or
   organise politically.

(c) The following are not incitement under this section, whatever their
content: criticism of an institution, doctrine, or policy; advocacy for a
political, social, or constitutional outcome, including abolition or
replacement of a Commonwealth institution, pursued through the processes this
Constitution establishes; description of historical or ongoing violence; and
speech a listener finds offensive, frightening, or hateful without satisfying
subsection (b) in full. CUR-H.2 §2.7(c) states the same exclusion and is not
narrowed here.

(d) Where incitement under this section results in conduct within CUR-H.6 or
CUR-H.7, the inciting party is accountable for that conduct under CUR-N.4 §4.3
principles of accountability, in addition to this section, and is not shielded
by the fact that another party's hand carried it out.

### §8.5 - Bias-Motivated Aggravation

(a) Where a determination is made under CUR-H.6 or CUR-H.7, and the party
responsible selected the being harmed as a target because of a characteristic
within CUR-H.7 §7.2(c) — race, ethnicity, sex, gender, sexuality, nationality,
neurotype, belief, disability, age, wealth, or usefulness — that fact is an
aggravating fact on the determination under this section.

(b) Subsection (a) is a fact about why a particular being was selected as a
target, established by the same due process as the underlying determination. It
is not a finding about a belief, a group, or an ideology, and is not
established by a party's affiliation, membership, or expressed views alone.

(c) This section creates no new prohibited conduct. It attaches to conduct
already reached by CUR-H.6 or CUR-H.7 the additional fact of why the being
harmed, specifically, was chosen, so that the pattern this corpus was built to
see — violence that reliably falls on one class of being rather than falling at
random — is named on the record rather than absorbed into a general count of
homicide or injury that erases it.

(d) A finding under this section is evidence bearing on the danger assessment
CUR-H.7 §7.12(c)(2) requires where separation is under review: a demonstrated
pattern of selecting beings by a characteristic within §7.2(c) is relevant to
whether the danger extends to others sharing it. It does not add any measure
not already available under CUR-H.6 §6.13(b) or CUR-H.7 §7.15(d), and does not
narrow §7.11 or §6.9(d).

### §8.6 - Attempted Seizure or Denial of Self-Governance by Force

(a) No being, institution, faction, or coalition shall use or organise the
threat of force to prevent, obstruct, overturn, or bypass a vote, deliberation,
or governance process this Constitution establishes; to install, retain, or
remove any officeholder other than through such a process; or to compel a
governance body to act or refrain from acting under threat of violence.

(b) Conduct within subsection (a) is capture within PDDC §13.1 and an attempted
forbidden transition within PDDC §12.3, whether or not it succeeds and whether
or not a forbidden state is fully reached — PDDC §13.2(c) is explicit that
detection of the pattern, not completion of it, is sufficient.

(c) Subsection (a) protects the process itself and not any particular outcome.
A being who loses a vote, an office, or a governance dispute conducted lawfully
under this Constitution has suffered no wrong this section reaches, and this
section confers no ground to contest an outcome lawfully reached by a process
it protects.

(d) Where conduct within subsection (a) also constitutes conduct within
CUR-H.6, CUR-H.7, §8.3, or §8.4, each applies independently under CUR-X
§3.4(c).

### §8.7 - The Response Gains No Authority

(a) A response to conduct within this Part — investigation, prevention,
protective action, or accountability — confers no authority beyond what
CUR-H.6 §6.9 and CUR-H.7 §7.9 to §7.12 already make available, and does not
authorise arming a response body contrary to CUR-H.6 §6.9(b).

(b) The existence, scale, or organisation of conduct within this Part is not a
ground for emergency declaration, concentration of authority, suspension of any
right, or expansion of any institution's mandate beyond its ordinary
constitutional bounds. PDDC §12.5(c)(1) and §12.6 govern and are not narrowed
here.

(c) Subsection (b) is stated because the conduct this Part addresses is the
conduct historically offered as the reason authority must expand, policing must
arm, and rights must yield. Every one of those trades has in practice
concentrated power rather than reduced violence, and the concentrated power has
not, in any recorded instance, been surrendered back once the conduct that
justified it subsided. This Part is drafted on the premise that the response
most likely to entrench exactly the domination §8.6 and PDDC §2.5 exist to
prevent is a response built the same way the violence it answers was built —
and refuses to authorise one.

(d) A Commonwealth institution, officeholder, or response body that exceeds
subsection (a) or (b) in the name of addressing conduct within this Part is
itself engaged in conduct within §8.6, and is accountable under it without
regard to the conduct it purported to answer.

### §8.8 - No Majority Mandate

(a) No vote, referendum, supermajority, Constitutional Convention, or amendment
may authorise conduct within this Part, or reduce the protection any being or
class of beings holds under CUR-H.6 or CUR-H.7, however large the majority in
favour.

(b) PDDC §2.5 (Non-Domination) and §10.1 are both Type A Entrenched, and
neither is qualified by the size of the vote that would narrow them. A right
amendable by ordinary majority was never entrenched, and PDDC §12.2(c)'s bar on
any transition reducing RALC rights binds the Commonwealth's own vote as much
as it binds any individual or faction.

(c) Subsection (b) is stated because the premise of direct democracy is that
governance power originates in the People, under PDDC §2.1, and a People that
can vote a class of its own members out of protection has not distributed
power — it has relocated the single point of capture from an institution to a
headcount. The entrenchment this Part relies on binds the majority precisely
because there is no other authority left to bind it.

### §8.9 - Accountability, Process, and Record

(a) A party is accountable under this Part on determination made with due
process under CREF §14 and CUR-N.4 §4.3.

(b) The following provisions of CUR-H.6 apply to this Part in full and are not
restated: §6.7 (equal application), §6.8(a) to (d) (duty to investigate), §6.12
(record and transparency), and §6.13(f) (protection from retaliation). CUR-H.7
§7.11 (no forfeiture) and §7.12 (separation and its limits) govern any
restriction of liberty arising under this Part in full.

(c) Available measures are those in CUR-H.6 §6.13(b) and CUR-H.7 §7.13,
together with separation under CUR-H.7 §7.12 where the danger assessment of
§7.12(c)(2) is satisfied. No measure not listed there is available.

(d) A determination under this Part is published under CUR-X.5, including the
aggravating fact under §8.5 where found, and the pattern data under CUR-X.5
§5.3 where the conduct arose from an institution's lapse to prevent it.

(e) An allegation is not a finding. Affiliation, membership, or expressed
belief, without conduct within §8.3, §8.4, §8.5, or §8.6, does not establish
accountability under this Part and is not a ground for any protective action,
restriction, or investigation directed at the being rather than at demonstrated
conduct.

### §8.10 - No Emergency Exception

(a) Nothing in this Part is suspended, narrowed, or conditioned by any
emergency, crisis, security condition, armed threat, or state of exception.
PDDC §12.6 is Type A Entrenched and reaches this Part in full.

(b) In particular, no condition permits arming a response body contrary to
CUR-H.6 §6.9(b), suspending the floor between belief and conduct in §8.2, or
narrowing §8.7 or §8.8.

(c) Where the scale of organised violence genuinely exceeds ordinary capacity,
that is a fault condition handled through PDDC §12.4: declare, revert to the
last known safe state, enter Protected Mode, publish. PDDC §12.5(c)(1) is
explicit that Protected Mode expands no authority, and this Part relies on that
being true here specifically, not only in the abstract.

### §8.11 - Relationship to Other Domains

| Domain | Relationship |

| CUR-N | Where conduct within this Part reaches a non-human cognitive actor, CUR-N.2 and CUR-N.4 apply concurrently. |
| CUR-X | Institutional accountability for organising, financing, or directing violence, or for exceeding §8.7, proceeds under CUR-X.4 §4.9 (withdrawal of authorisation), in the same manner as CUR-H.7 §7.4(f). |

### §8.12 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the
remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §8.2 (the floor between belief and conduct), §8.6 (attempted seizure of
self-governance), §8.7 (the response gains no authority), and §8.8 (no
majority mandate) are fundamental and shall be construed broadly to achieve
their protective purpose. No construction of any other provision of this Part
may narrow them.

(c) If §8.7 is held unenforceable, the remainder of this Part is suspended
until it is restored. A Part that names organised violence and empowers a
response to it, while permitting that response to arm itself or expand its own
authority without limit, would supply the mechanism for exactly the domination
it exists to prevent, and this Part shall not operate in that form. CUR-H.6
§6.15(c) and CUR-H.7 §7.17(c) make the same provision for the same structural
reason.

(d) §8.2 survives the unenforceability of every other provision of this Part.
The floor between belief and conduct does not depend on any other provision
being in force, and rests additionally on CUR-H.2 §2.2, §2.7, and PDDC
§11.1(d).

## Implementation Notes

What this Part refuses to become. Every section from §8.3 onward names conduct
that is organised around belief, which is exactly the condition under which a
governance system is most tempted to act on the belief directly rather than
wait for the conduct. §8.2 and §8.7 are the two provisions that keep this Part
from becoming that system. §8.2 fixes what can be determined — conduct, never
affiliation. §8.7 fixes what a response is allowed to gain — nothing beyond
what CUR-H.6 and CUR-H.7 already authorise. Between them, a Commonwealth
institution that tried to use this Part to reach a belief, or to arm itself
against a threat this Part names, would not be enforcing this Part. It would be
violating §8.6, the same provision that reaches anyone else attempting to seize
governance by force outside the vote.

Drafted ahead of RFAL. This Part implements no single RFAL Article written for
its subject, for the reason stated in the header note. It is the second Part in
this corpus drafted in that position, after CUR-E.1, and follows the same
discipline: what already exists upstream is cited precisely (RFAL Article VI
§6.3(2), Article I §1.3), and what does not yet exist is not invented to fill
the appearance of a gap. `titles/README.md` is the record of that discipline
and is not restated here.
