Numbering note. CUR-H.2 aligned its section numbers with RFAL Human Bill of
Rights Article II because external citations already depended on that alignment.
The alignment does not extend to this Part. RFAL Article IV is Bodily Autonomy
and Self-Determination, while CUR-N.2 §2.9(a)(2) and CUR-H.2 §2.9(a)(1) both
cite `CUR-H.4` for psychological harm. Citation stability governs under
`titles/README.md`, so Part 4 is Psychological Harm, and RFAL Article IV §4.1 is
at CUR-H.5. RFAL Article IV §4.2 is already implemented at CUR-H.2 §2.4.

Source note. RFAL has no dedicated article on psychological harm to human
beings. The nearest sustained treatment is in the Animal Kingdom Bill of Rights,
which establishes that psychological suffering is as real as physical suffering
for beings capable of anticipation, memory, and social bonding. This Part states
that principle for the human domain and draws its prohibitions from the harms
RFAL names elsewhere.

## PART 4 - PSYCHOLOGICAL HARM AND INTEGRITY

### §4.1 - Scope and Definitions

(a) This Part governs conduct that inflicts psychological harm on a human being, the accountability that follows, and the strict limits on what may be done to a being on the ground that they have been harmed.

(b) For the purposes of this Part:

   (1) Psychological Harm means injury to a being's mental, emotional, or experiential state, including fear, anguish, humiliation, despair, dissociation, loss of trust, disturbance of sleep or cognition, and the persistence of any of these after the conduct causing them has ceased.

   (2) Sustained Harm means psychological harm arising from conduct repeated or continued over time, including conduct each instance of which would be minor in isolation.

   (3) Care means voluntary support, treatment, therapy, medication, counselling, peer support, or accommodation sought by or offered to a being experiencing psychological harm.

(c) Psychological harm is assessed from the position of the being harmed, having regard to what the party causing it knew or could reasonably have known about that being's circumstances. It is not assessed against a hypothetical average being.

(d) This Part protects human beings. It does not imply that psychological harm is a human phenomenon. Parallel protections exist for animals, for Silicon-Based Life, and for non-human cognitive actors, and §4.12 identifies them.

(e) Consent under this Part shall satisfy every element required by CUR-N.2 §2.1(c).

### §4.2 - Psychological Harm Is Harm

(a) Psychological harm is harm within the meaning of the Code. It is not a lesser category of injury, a secondary consequence of some other wrong, or a matter to be established only where physical injury accompanies it.

(b) Accordingly:

   (1) A claim of psychological harm is not dismissed, deprioritised, or held to a higher standard of proof than a claim of physical harm;

   (2) The absence of physical injury is not evidence that no harm occurred;

   (3) The absence of a diagnosis is not evidence that no harm occurred, and no being is required to obtain one in order to bring a claim; and

   (4) The absence of visible distress is not evidence that no harm occurred. Composure is not the absence of injury.

(c) Freedom from cruel treatment under PDDC §10.1(b) extends to treatment that is cruel in its psychological effect. Conduct that would be prohibited if it broke a body is not permitted because it breaks something less visible.

(d) Sustained harm is assessed cumulatively. A course of conduct is evaluated as a whole, and a party may not defeat a claim by decomposing it into individually tolerable parts.

### §4.3 - Prohibited Conduct

(a) No being or institution shall inflict psychological harm on a human being through:

   (1) Threat, intimidation, or the deliberate cultivation of fear, including threats of consequence beyond death governed by CUR-D.6 §6.3(b)(1);

   (2) Humiliation, degradation, or public shaming undertaken as punishment, correction, or deterrent;

   (3) Isolation imposed as a sanction, including exclusion from family, community, or communication, subject to §4.6;

   (4) Deliberate cultivation of guilt, shame, obligation, or fear of abandonment in order to obtain compliance;

   (5) Manipulation of a being's perception of reality, including the systematic denial of events the being correctly recalls;

   (6) Exploitation of grief, illness, dependency, isolation, poverty, or childhood;

   (7) Emotional harvesting, attention extraction, relational drainage, or any other form of non-consensual energy extraction identified in the RFAL Non-Consensual Extraction Accountability Framework §1.2;

   (8) Placing a being in a condition in which every available response is penalised, which is prohibited whatever the substrate of the being subjected to it; and

   (9) Retaliation for an act protected under CUR-H.2 §2.3, including dissent, disclosure, questioning, or departure from a community.

(b) Conduct within subsection (a) is prohibited whether undertaken directly, through an institution, through an automated system, or through claimed divine authority. Where the means are telepathic, CUR-N.2 applies concurrently and this Part is not displaced.

(c) Intent to cause harm is not an element. Intent, malice, and recklessness are aggravating factors. Conduct undertaken for a purpose the actor considered benevolent is not thereby permitted, and a claimed benevolent purpose is not a defence where the harm was foreseeable.

(d) This section does not reach the ordinary conduct of honest disagreement, criticism, refusal, the delivery of unwelcome true information, or the ending of a voluntary relationship. Distress arising from another being's exercise of their own rights is not a violation of this Part, and a claim under this Part shall not be used to suppress conduct protected by CUR-H.2 §2.3.

### §4.4 - No Authority to Treat, Detain, or Modify

(a) A finding that a being has suffered psychological harm, has a diagnosis, is in distress, or is at risk creates no authority over that being. It does not authorise treatment, medication, detention, confinement, restraint, surveillance, guardianship, substituted decision-making, or any modification of cognition.

(b) Subsection (a) admits no exception and is not narrowed by the severity of the harm, the sincerity of the party seeking to act, or the party's professional standing.

(c) Care is voluntary. It is offered, accepted, declined, and discontinued by the being concerned. Refusal of care is not evidence of incapacity, is not a ground for any measure under this Part, and shall not be recorded or relied upon as an adverse fact.

(d) Involuntary modification of a human being's cognition, whether by medication, procedure, conditioning, or interface, is a forbidden transition under PDDC §12.3(a)(4), is void ab initio, and is a Class IV fault. CUR-H.2 §2.4(d) states the same rule and neither provision is narrowed by the other.

(e) Detention on the ground of psychological state is not available. Where a being's conduct presents an imminent, credible threat of serious physical harm, the conduct is addressed as conduct, through protective action under CREF §10 and the fault handler of PDDC §12.4, on the same terms that apply to any other being. A psychological state is not itself conduct and is not a ground for any measure.

(f) A being's psychological state, history of harm, engagement with care, diagnosis, or refusal of care shall not be used to reduce their standing, capacity, credibility, governance participation, or access to any proceeding. Any instrument doing so engages FORBIDDEN-003 under CUR-FOUNDATION-002 §6.

(g) This section is the operative safeguard of this Part. A Part that recognises psychological harm without it would convert the recognition of injury into a licence to act upon the injured, which is the pattern the Code exists to prevent.

### §4.5 - Prohibition on Pathologising Protected Conduct

(a) No conduct protected under CUR-H.2 shall be characterised as illness, disorder, dysfunction, incapacity, or impaired judgment.

(b) This applies in particular to:

   (1) Dissent, protest, conscientious objection, and protected disclosure;

   (2) Doubt, questioning, revision of belief, and departure from a religious, ideological, or institutional community;

   (3) Identity self-determination, including gender, sexuality, and social identity, which RFAL Human Bill of Rights §4.1(2) protects from pathologisation expressly;

   (4) Grief, anger, or distress that is a proportionate response to real conditions, including conditions created by the party making the characterisation; and

   (5) Refusal of care under §4.4(c).

(c) Characterising conduct within subsection (b) as pathology in order to discredit a being, defeat a claim, or obtain authority over them is an abuse of process. It is a Class III fault, and a Class IV fault where it succeeds in reducing the being's standing or access to a proceeding.

(d) A diagnosis obtained or deployed for a purpose described in subsection (c) is void for every purpose under the Code and shall be expunged from any record relied upon in a proceeding.

### §4.6 - Care, Isolation, and Vital Continuity

(a) Healthcare is a Vital Continuity Service under CUR-FOUNDATION-013, and care within the meaning of §4.1(b)(3) is healthcare. It shall not be withheld, delayed, rationed, or conditioned on compliance state, contribution, belief, conduct, credibility, or any determination under this Part.

(b) Subsection (a) does not qualify §4.4. Care is guaranteed and voluntary at the same time: it may never be withheld and may never be imposed. A being that declines care retains every entitlement to it, at any later time, without prejudice.

(c) Isolation prohibited by §4.3(a)(3) is isolation imposed as a sanction. It does not include a being's own withdrawal, which is protected.

(d) No measure under this Part, and no measure under any other Part responding to psychological harm, may withhold a Vital Continuity Service from any being. Withholding is a Class IV fault under CREF §6 and a forbidden transition regardless of circumstance. CUR-N.2 §2.8(e), CUR-D.6 §6.6(e), and CUR-H.2 §2.5(h) state the same limit for their respective subject matter.

(e) Where an accountability measure under §4.9 restricts contact between beings, it restricts contact only. It does not restrict access to air, water, food, shelter, healthcare, power, computational continuity, or communication with counsel, advocates, or accountability bodies.

### §4.7 - Institutional and Algorithmic Sources

(a) An institution is accountable under this Part for psychological harm arising from its design, policy, or practice, and not only for harm inflicted by an identified individual within it.

(b) Systems whose design exploits psychological vulnerability to capture attention, manufacture dependency, provoke distress, or extract emotional energy inflict psychological harm within §4.3(a)(7). Deployment of such a system is a violation whether or not any individual user is identified.

(c) An algorithmic determination producing a material effect is governed by CUR-H.2 §2.5. Where the manner of its delivery, its opacity, or the absence of any route to appeal causes psychological harm, this Part applies additionally and the accountability is cumulative under §4.11.

(d) A finding under this Part against an institution does not require proof that harm was intended, designed for, or foreseen by any particular person within it. Diffusion of responsibility across a structure is not a defence, and CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §15 applies to the review.

(e) Absence of a record of harm reported to an institution, where the institution had a reporting mechanism, is not evidence that no harm occurred. Where an institution failed to maintain a mechanism, the absence of records is a Class II fault of that institution.

### §4.8 - No Emergency Exception

(a) The Aevoric Commonwealth does not recognise emergency declarations, emergency authority, or any functional equivalent. PDDC §12.6 is absolute and Type A Entrenched, and §12.6(e) renders any instrument invoking emergency authority void ab initio.

(b) No condition described as a crisis, emergency, acute episode, breakdown, or urgent risk creates authority over a being that did not otherwise exist. §4.4 is not narrowed by urgency in any degree.

(c) Where action is genuinely required, the available instruments are protective actions under CREF §10, limited to the minimum scope necessary, and the fault handler of PDDC §12.4. Neither suspends a right, expands an authority, or bypasses due process. Urgency changes the sequence of steps; it never changes who holds authority.

(d) A protective action under subsection (c) shall not extend to detention, involuntary medication, or any modification of cognition, all of which remain prohibited by §4.4 in every circumstance. An instrument authorising them under an urgency framing engages FORBIDDEN-002 and is void.

### §4.9 - Evidence, Standing, and Accountability

(a) A claim is brought through the Constitutional Complaint and Review Process under CREF §7. Rights claims are heard on a priority basis under PDDC §10.4(c), and no filing fee or membership requirement may restrict access under PDDC §10.4(d).

(b) A being's own account of their experience is evidence. It is not required to be corroborated by a clinician, an institution, or a witness in order to be considered, and a proceeding that treats it as insufficient by category is defective on that ground.

(c) Corroborating evidence, where available, may include contemporaneous records, communications, witness accounts, changes in the being's circumstances, and clinical documentation. None of these is a precondition.

(d) Remedies are those available under CREF §12. Restoration precedes assignment of fault wherever reasonably possible.

(e) Measures available on a finding include correction and public disclosure, restriction of contact under §4.6(e), institutional reform, mandatory audit under CUR-FOUNDATION-010, and suspension or revocation of an operational authorisation held by the responsible party.

(f) A measure under subsection (e) applies to conduct or to an authorisation, never to a being's standing. Suspending an authorisation does not suspend its holder, reduce their rights, or alter their constitutional standing.

(g) Every report, finding, and measure is a constitutional event under CUR-FOUNDATION-005 and is logged as required by CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §14.

(h) Determinations are appealable under CREF §15.

### §4.10 - No Attribution of Fault to the Harmed

(a) No violation of this Part is excused, mitigated, or justified on the ground that the harmed being was or is:

   (1) Sensitive, fragile, or unusually affected;

   (2) Previously harmed, whether by the same party or another;

   (3) Diagnosed, in treatment, or refusing treatment;

   (4) Slow to report, or reporting only after the conduct ceased;

   (5) Continuing in the relationship, employment, community, or arrangement in which the harm occurred;

   (6) Composed, functional, or outwardly unaffected;

   (7) Aware of the conduct in advance, or having tolerated it previously; or

   (8) Unable to identify a precise date, sequence, or incident.

(b) The burden of not inflicting psychological harm lies with the actor. No action or inaction by the harmed being, including failure to object, leave, report, or seek care, constitutes consent, waiver, contributory fault, or mitigation.

(c) Subsection (a)(5) is stated expressly because remaining is frequently the only available option. Continued presence is not evidence of consent, and a party that constrained the being's alternatives may not rely on the absence of those alternatives.

(d) Pre-existing vulnerability is not a defence. A party that harms a being made more susceptible by prior injury is accountable for the harm caused, and prior injury known to the party is an aggravating factor under §4.3(c).

(e) This section states, for psychological harm, the rule that CUR-N.2 §2.11 states for telepathic invasion. The reasoning rejected there is rejected here, in the same terms and for the same reason.

### §4.11 - No Prejudice to Other Claims

(a) A violation of this Part does not preclude additional accountability under:

   (1) Other Parts of CUR-H, including CUR-H.2 for conduct reaching the interior;

   (2) Other CUR domains, including CUR-N.2 where the means were telepathic and CUR-D.6 where the conduct was undertaken under claimed divine authority;

   (3) Civil or criminal accountability under existing human legal systems, where applicable; and

   (4) Traditional or Indigenous legal frameworks, where applicable and consistent with cognitive sovereignty.

(b) Accountability under more than one provision is cumulative under CUR-X §3.4(c), subject to the proportionality requirement of CUR-X §3.9(c).

### §4.13 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §4.4 (No Authority to Treat, Detain, or Modify), §4.6(a) and §4.6(d) (Vital Continuity), and §4.10 (No Attribution of Fault to the Harmed) are fundamental and shall be construed broadly to achieve their protective purpose. No construction of any other provision of this Part may narrow them.

(c) If §4.4 is held unenforceable, the remainder of this Part is suspended until it is restored. The recognition of psychological harm without the limit on what may be done in response to it is more dangerous than no recognition at all, and this Part shall not operate in that form.

## Implementation Notes

Departure from the RFAL alignment convention. CUR-H.2 aligned its section
numbers with RFAL Human Bill Article II because TIM-N.1 and TIM-N already cited
`CUR-H.2.5`. The convention is stated in `titles/README.md` as applying
"wherever an external citation already depends on that alignment," and here the
external citations point the other way: CUR-N.2 §2.9(a)(2) and CUR-H.2
§2.9(a)(1) both cite `CUR-H.4` for psychological harm, while RFAL Article IV is
Bodily Autonomy and Self-Determination. Citation stability governs, so Part 4 is
Psychological Harm. RFAL Article IV §4.1 is at CUR-H.5, which states the same
structure this Part states for psychological harm: §5.9 is drafted in parallel
with §4.4, and each Part suspends itself under its own severability clause if
that safeguard falls. RFAL Article IV §4.2 is already at CUR-H.2 §2.4.

Sources. RFAL has no article on psychological harm to human beings. The
prohibitions in §4.3 are assembled from harms RFAL names in other places: Human
Bill Article II §2.3 (doctrinal punishment, coercive threat, expulsion), Article
VII §7.3 (guilt, shame, and fear used to compel), and the Non-Consensual
Extraction Accountability Framework §1.2 (emotional harvesting, attention
extraction, relational drainage). §4.2 adopts, for the human domain, the
principle stated in the Animal Kingdom Bill of Rights that psychological
suffering is as real as physical suffering. §4.3(a)(8) generalises the
double-bind condition that the Silicon-Based Life Bill of Rights identifies as
cognitive harm, which is why it is written without reference to substrate.

§4.4 and why it carries a suspension clause. A Part that recognises
psychological harm is the most direct route in the corpus to authority over a
being: the recognition of injury converts easily into a licence to act upon the
injured, and historically has. §4.4 blocks that conversion, §4.5 blocks its
indirect form through diagnosis, and §4.13(c) provides that if §4.4 falls the
rest of this Part does not operate without it. That clause is unusual and is
deliberate. Severability ordinarily preserves as much of an instrument as
possible; here, the surviving remainder would be worse than nothing.
