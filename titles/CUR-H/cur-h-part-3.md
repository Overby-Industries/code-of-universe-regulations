# CUR-H - HUMAN REGULATIONS

*Code of Universe Regulations - Human*

- **Document ID:** CUR-H.3
- **Version:** 1.0.0-Draft
- **Date:** 2026-08-03
- **Status:** Draft
- **Depends On:** CUR-FOUNDATION-001, CUR-FOUNDATION-002, CUR-FOUNDATION-005, CUR-FOUNDATION-010, CUR-FOUNDATION-012 (CREF), CUR-FOUNDATION-013, PDDC TITLE 4, PDDC TITLE 5, PDDC §12.6, CUR-H.2, CUR-D.6, CUR-X.4
- **Implements:** RFAL Human Bill of Rights v1.7.0 Article III (Right to Knowledge and Understanding), in full
- **Referenced by:** PIM §8

Relationship to CUR-H.2 and CUR-D.6. CUR-H.2 protects the interior — what a
being may think, believe, question, and revise, free of any institution's
jurisdiction over it. CUR-D.6 governs the coercive mechanisms by which claimed
authority is turned against conscience. Neither states what an educational
authority must actually make available, or may not withhold, as a matter of
content. That is this Part's subject: not the freedom to think, which CUR-H.2
already makes absolute, but the floor of what a being is given to think *with*.
A right to an unpoliced interior is a formality for a being who was never shown
the evidence-based account of anything to weigh it against.

Note on PIM. PIM §8 names, among the questions the corpus does not yet answer,
"what a school or an education system owes a child, and what it may require."
This Part narrows that gap; it does not close it. It states what may not be
suppressed, withheld, or replaced with doctrine presented as settled fact. It
does not state a custody standard, a guardian's procedural authority, or what a
school owes a child beyond content. Those remain open, and PIM §8 is updated
to say so rather than to imply this Part answers more than it does.

## PART 3 - KNOWLEDGE, EDUCATION, AND PROTECTION FROM ENFORCED IGNORANCE

### §3.1 - Scope and Definitions

(a) This Part governs what a being is owed in accurate, evidence-based
information and education, and what an educational authority may not withhold,
suppress, or replace with doctrine presented as established fact.

(b) For the purposes of this Part:

   (1) Enforced Ignorance means the withholding, suppression, or replacement of
   accurate, evidence-based information with doctrine presented as settled
   fact, where done by an educational authority acting in that capacity.

   (2) Evidence-Based Information means information grounded in observation,
   reproducible method, and openness to revision in light of new evidence. It
   is distinguished from Belief as CUR-H.2 §2.1(b)(3) defines it, which this
   Part does not reach and does not judge.

   (3) Educational Authority means any party controlling a being's access to
   education in a custodial, institutional, or employment capacity: a school,
   a curriculum, a certifying body, an enterprise's training or onboarding
   process — CUR-X.4 §4.2 states that an enterprise directing a being's work
   holds authority over them on the same terms as any other governing body,
   and this Part reaches its instructional content on that basis — or a
   parent or guardian exercising the authority PIM §3 describes. It does not
   mean a being teaching, publishing, or expressing their own view outside
   such a capacity.

   (4) Suppressing Interest means a religious, political, corporate, or
   Commonwealth-institutional interest asserted as the ground for withholding,
   suppressing, or replacing evidence-based information. RFAL Article III
   §3.1 names censorship by "religious, political, or corporate interests"
   together, and this Part does not treat one as more reachable than another.

(c) This Part reaches human beings. Parallel considerations for other domains
are identified in §3.8.

(d) Nothing in this Part is contingent on age, citizenship, membership,
compliance state, contribution, or usefulness. CUR-H.2 §2.1(e) states the same
rule and is not narrowed here. In particular, this Part reaches a child in
exactly the terms PIM §1 states for CUR-H.5: nothing in how this Code is built
checks age before applying it.

### §3.2 - The Right to Accurate Information

(a) Every being holds the right to access the best available evidence-based
understanding of the natural world, the cosmos, and human history, without
suppression, distortion, or replacement on the ground of a Suppressing
Interest. RFAL Article III §3.1 states this as access to scientific literacy,
philosophical literacy — exposure to multiple traditions, none presented as
exclusively or self-evidently true — and historical accuracy.

(b) Subsection (a) protects access to the best available understanding, not a
fixed statement of it. Where evidence-based understanding is revised, the
right in this section follows the revision. No particular scientific or
historical claim is entrenched by this Part — including the specific claims
RFAL Article III §3.2 lists as illustration, such as the age of the universe
or the mechanism of stellar nucleosynthesis — and RFAL Article VIII's
evidence-responsiveness principle governs this section specifically rather
than being merely aspirational to it.

(c) No belief system, doctrine, or institution may be presented, by an
educational authority acting in that capacity, as the sole or self-evidently
true account of a matter on which evidence-based inquiry remains open or has
reached a different conclusion. This does not prevent a being from being
taught, or a parent teaching a child under PIM §3, a tradition's own account of
such a matter — it prevents that account from displacing, or being falsely
equated in authority with, the evidence-based account within an educational
authority's own instruction. CUR-D.6 §6.9(a) and PIM §3 protect the former;
this subsection is the boundary between it and enforced ignorance, stated
again and specifically for knowledge in §3.4.

(d) Historical accuracy includes honest accounting of harms done by
institutions and ideological systems, including Commonwealth institutions,
alongside their contributions. An educational authority may not omit or
minimise institutional harm on the ground that the institution is
Commonwealth-affiliated, sacred, or otherwise favoured. Where the omission
concerns a Commonwealth institution's own conduct, CUR-X.5 governs what must
already be on the public record, and this subsection forbids teaching against
it.

### §3.3 - Protection from Enforced Ignorance

(a) No being shall be denied education in science, history, or philosophy on
the ground of a Suppressing Interest, by any educational authority.

(b) No being shall be taught, by an educational authority acting in that
capacity, that a single text, tradition, or doctrine is the sole source of
truth about the natural world, to the exclusion of evidence-based
understanding.

(c) No being shall be punished, penalised, or subjected to adverse treatment by
an educational authority for seeking, encountering, or accepting knowledge
that contradicts the doctrine of their community, family, or institution.

(d) Subsections (a) to (c) reach an educational authority acting as one — a
school, a curriculum, a certifying body, an enterprise's own training, an
institution's own instruction. They do not reach a family's or community's own
teaching of its tradition under CUR-D.6 §6.9(a) and PIM §3, except at the
boundary §3.2(c) and §3.4 state.

### §3.4 - Where Teaching Becomes Enforced Ignorance

(a) The boundary is the one PIM §4 already draws for coercion generally,
applied here to knowledge specifically. Raising or educating a being within a
tradition is protected. Using custodial, institutional, or employment
authority to prevent that being from ever encountering, or being able to
evaluate, the evidence-based account is not teaching, and is enforced
ignorance within §3.3.

(b) An educational authority may not condition continued custody, membership,
employment, housing, or standing on a being's acceptance of a doctrinal
account over an evidence-based one. CUR-H.2 §2.3(d) and CUR-D.6 §6.3(a) state
the same prohibition for belief generally, and it applies here to knowledge
specifically.

(c) A child's ordinary religious or values education by a parent under PIM §3
is not enforced ignorance. What crosses into coercion is described in PIM §4
and is not restated here; this section identifies where the same line falls
for factual and scientific content specifically, and the two lines are the
same line, read for different subject matter.

### §3.5 - Truth-Telling and Inquiry Protected

(a) A being who teaches, publishes, or communicates evidence-based information
that conflicts with an institution's doctrine, policy, or preferred account is
protected from retaliation under CUR-H.2 §2.3(e). This section states the same
protection specifically for educators, researchers, and those describing
institutional harm under §3.2(d).

(b) Nothing in this Part authorises compelled instruction in any specific
doctrine, secular or religious, as truth. §3.2(c) states the corresponding
limit on what may be taught as settled, and this section does not create an
exception running the other way.

### §3.6 - Enforcement, Standing, and Audit

(a) Any being may bring a claim under this Part through the Constitutional
Complaint and Review Process under CREF §7.

(b) Remedies are those available under CREF §12: restoration, correction,
compensation, and institutional reform.

(c) A violation of §3.3 by a Commonwealth-affiliated educational authority is a
Class III fault. A pattern of violations across an institution is referred for
audit under CUR-FOUNDATION-010.

(d) No violation of this Part is excused on the ground that the being affected
did not object at the time, was a minor when the conduct occurred, or later
came to accept the doctrine taught. CUR-H.2 §2.8(f) states the same rule for
that Part and the reasoning is identical: the burden of respect lies with the
educational authority, never with the being taught.

### §3.7 - No Emergency Exception

(a) Nothing in this Part is suspended, narrowed, or conditioned by any
emergency, crisis, security condition, or state of exception. PDDC §12.6 is
Type A Entrenched.

(b) No condition permits suppression of evidence-based information, or
compelled doctrinal instruction, on grounds of public morality, social
harmony, doctrinal integrity, or national security. CUR-H.2 §2.6(b) states the
same rule for expression generally and applies here to education specifically.

### §3.8 - Relationship to Other Domains

| Domain | Relationship |
|---|---|
| CUR-D | Coercive use of doctrine against inquiry is governed concurrently by CUR-D.6. §3.4 identifies the boundary this Part shares with it, and neither displaces the other. |
| CUR-N | Where an automated system curates, ranks, or filters educational content, CUR-H.2 §2.5 governs the determination and this Part governs the content withheld or presented. |
| CUR-X | An enterprise's own training or certification content is reached as an educational authority under §3.1(b)(3), on the same footing CUR-X.4 §4.2 establishes generally. Institutional accountability for a pattern of violation proceeds under CUR-X.4 §4.9. |

### §3.9 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the
remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §3.2(a) (the right to accurate information) and §3.3 (protection from
enforced ignorance) are fundamental and shall be construed broadly to achieve
their protective purpose. No construction of any other provision of this Part
may narrow them.

(c) §3.2(c) and §3.4, which together state the boundary between protected
teaching and enforced ignorance, are read together and neither may be
construed to narrow the other. A reading of §3.4 that reaches ordinary
religious or values education, or a reading of §3.2(c) that permits
displacing evidence-based instruction, both fail this section.

## Implementation Notes

Alignment with RFAL. This Part implements RFAL Human Bill of Rights Article
III in full.

| RFAL Human Bill of Rights | This Part |
|---|---|
| Article III §3.1 Access to Information | §3.2(a), §3.2(d) |
| Article III §3.2 The Right to Cosmic Context | §3.2(a)-(b) |
| Article III §3.3 Protection from Enforced Ignorance | §3.3 |

Why §3.2(b) does not adopt RFAL's specific claims as legal text. RFAL Article
III §3.2 lists concrete facts — the age of the universe, the stellar origin of
the atoms in a human body — as the content this right protects access to. This
Part protects the right in general terms instead, because a regulatory code
that entrenches a specific empirical figure creates the appearance that
revising the figure requires amending the Code, which is exactly backwards:
the right is to the best current understanding, not to any one estimate of it.
RFAL Article VIII already states the corpus's general position on
evidence-responsiveness; §3.2(b) is the point at which that position had to be
made operative rather than left as background principle, because this is the
one Part whose entire subject is what a being is owed in factual content.

Why "Suppressing Interest" reaches corporate and political grounds and not
only doctrinal ones. RFAL Article III §3.1 names "religious, political, or
corporate interests" together as grounds of censorship it forbids, and earlier
drafting language in this domain has tended to narrow "doctrine" to mean
religious doctrine specifically. This Part does not narrow it. An enterprise
that omits known hazard data from its own safety training, or a political body
that pressures a curriculum to omit an inconvenient historical fact, is
reached on the same terms as a religious institution that teaches a
scientific claim is heretical. CUR-H.7 §7.8(d) already treats concealment of a
hazard as accountable independently of the hazard itself; §3.2(d) and §3.3
apply the same logic to educational content specifically.

Note on what this Part does not do. It was drafted in response to a gap PIM
§8 named honestly rather than papered over: what a school or an education
system owes a child. This Part answers the content half of that question — what
may not be withheld or replaced with doctrine — and no more. It does not state
a custody standard, does not state what authority a guardian holds where §3
does not reach, and does not resolve a conflict between a parent's own rights
and a child's protection beyond what CUR-D.6 §6.3(a) and PIM §4 already
resolve. PIM §8's remaining bullets stand, minus the one this Part narrows.
