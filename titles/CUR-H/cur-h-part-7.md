Relationship to CUR-H.6. That Part governs sexual violence and its specific
mechanisms of evasion. This Part governs violence against the person generally:
killing, injury, cruelty, enslavement, confinement, and the reckless creation of
lethal risk. Where both apply, both apply. The provisions on equal application
(§6.7), the duty to investigate (§6.8), and record and concealment (§6.12) are
not restated here — they apply to this Part in full by §7.15, because restating
them would create two texts that can drift apart.

Note on the hard question. Every Part of this corpus states that measures attach
to conduct, positions, and authorisations, and never to beings. Homicide is where
that rule costs something, because it is the offence for which every society
reaches for a response that attaches to the being: execution, permanent
confinement, banishment. This Part refuses all three, and §7.10 to §7.12 state
what is available instead rather than leaving the question implied. That is the
substance of this Part; the prohibitions are the easy half.

## PART 7 - OFFENCES AGAINST THE PERSON

### §7.1 - Scope and Definitions

(a) This Part governs conduct that kills, injures, confines, enslaves, or endangers a being; the accountability that follows; and the strict limits on what may be done to a being who has been determined responsible.

(b) For the purposes of this Part:

   (1) Homicide means conduct by a party that causes the death of a being.

   (2) Injury means physical harm to a being's body. Psychological harm is governed by CUR-H.4 and the two are cumulative under CUR-X §3.4(c), neither being a lesser form of the other.

   (3) Endangerment means the creation or maintenance of a risk of death or serious injury to a being, whether or not the risk eventuates.

   (4) Separation means the restriction of a party's proximity to, or contact with, identified beings, on the ground of a demonstrated danger to them. It is defined here because §7.12 bounds it, and because it is the only restriction of liberty this Part makes available.

   (5) Party Responsible means a being or institution determined under §7.15 to have engaged in conduct within this Part. The term carries no implication about the party's standing, which §7.11 governs.

(c) This Part reaches human beings. Parallel protections exist for animals under CUR-A, for Silicon-Based Life under CUR-S, and for non-human cognitive actors under CUR-N, and §7.14(d) states how conduct spanning them is handled.

(d) The right protected here is not conditional on citizenship, membership, compliance state, contribution, conduct, or usefulness, and is held by every being within jurisdiction including a party responsible under this Part.

### §7.2 - The Right to Life

(a) Every being holds the right to continued existence. It is inherent, is not granted by any institution, and is not within any institution's authority to withdraw.

(b) RFAL Human Bill Article I §1.1 grounds this in what a being is rather than in any authority's favour: dignity "is not granted by any government, institution, religion, or deity" and "is not contingent on belief, obedience, or conformity." A right derived from an authority's approval is a right that authority may withdraw, which is why this Part derives it from nothing.

(c) RFAL Human Bill Article I §1.3 forecloses hierarchies of worth founded on identity. No characteristic of a being — race, ethnicity, sex, gender, sexuality, nationality, neurotype, belief, disability, age, wealth, or usefulness — bears on the protection this Part affords them, on the seriousness of conduct against them, or on the measure that follows.

(d) The right in this section is non-derogable and remains fully operative during Protected Mode under PDDC §10.3(a). No declaration of any form may serve as a predicate for limiting it under PDDC §10.3(d).

### §7.3 - Homicide

(a) No being or institution shall cause the death of a being.

(b) Homicide is a Class IV fault. It engages FORBIDDEN-001 under CUR-FOUNDATION-002 §6 where the being was treated as disposable, and PDDC §12.3(a)(3) where they were treated as property.

(c) The following are not defences and do not reduce a determination:

   (1) That the being killed was believed to deserve it, on any ground;

   (2) That the being killed had themselves been determined responsible under this Part or any other;

   (3) That the killing was directed, ordered, authorised, sanctioned, or blessed by any institution, office, authority, doctrine, or claimed divine instruction, under CUR-H.5 §5.6(a) and CUR-D.6;

   (4) That the being killed had consented, except where the conduct falls within CUR-H.5 §5.5 and satisfies every requirement of that section including §5.5(e);

   (5) That the being killed belonged to a group, held a belief, or possessed a characteristic within §7.2(c);

   (6) That the killing served a public benefit, an economic necessity, a security requirement, a population objective, or the interests of the many. CUR-X.4 §4.10(a) states the general rule and it is absolute here; or

   (7) That the being was already dying, was expected to die, or was of an age or condition at which death was anticipated.

(d) Where a party's conduct kills a being and the party asserts that the being was already dead, CUR-H.5 §5.5A governs the determination, and a determination of death made by that party or by any party holding an interest in it is void under §5.5A(f).

(e) Conduct intended to kill that does not kill is governed by this section and is not a lesser category of conduct by reason of the outcome. The being who survives was not less endangered by the survival.

### §7.4 - Homicide by Omission, System, and Institution

(a) A death caused by a failure to act, by a system permitted to operate in a state that kills, or by an institution's decision, is a homicide within §7.3. The absence of a party who struck a blow does not make a death accidental.

(b) This section applies where a party held an obligation to the being killed arising from:

   (1) Operation of a habitat, vessel, or life-support system on which the being depended, under CUR-E.2 §2.2;

   (2) Supply of a Vital Continuity Service under CUR-FOUNDATION-013, or of the infrastructure one depends on, under CUR-X.4 §4.4;

   (3) Care of the being, including medical, custodial, and dependent care;

   (4) Employment or engagement of the being in conditions the party controlled; or

   (5) Manufacture, maintenance, or authorisation of a thing or process that killed them.

(c) A party within subsection (b) is accountable where the death resulted from a condition the party knew of, was informed of, would have known of had it maintained the monitoring required of it, or created.

(d) It is not a defence that:

   (1) No individual within an institution decided the outcome, or that responsibility was distributed across roles none of which held the whole picture;

   (2) The condition was documented in a risk register, disclosed in a filing, or accepted by a body;

   (3) The cost of preventing the death exceeded a valuation placed on the being's life, no such valuation being recognised by this Code; or

   (4) The being had been warned, had accepted the risk, or had continued in the conditions. CUR-X.4 §4.6 and CUR-H.6 §6.3(a)(8) both provide that consent given under conditions this Code exists to remove is not consent.

(e) Subsection (d)(1) is stated first because it is the defence that actually succeeds. Institutional homicide is rarely decided; it is arrived at, by a sequence of individually defensible decisions each made by a party who could not see the whole. CUR-E.1 §1.2(d) states the corresponding rule for cumulative environmental harm, and the reasoning is identical: the sequence is assessed as a whole, and a party may not defeat a determination by decomposing it into individually tolerable parts.

(f) An institution accountable under this section is reached under CUR-X.4 §4.9(b), including by withdrawal of its authorisation, with continuity assumed under CUR-X.4 §4.9(d) and §4.9(g). Beings working within it are not reached, under CUR-X.4 §4.9(c).

### §7.5 - Injury, Cruelty, and Torture

(a) No being or institution shall inflict physical injury on a being without their consent satisfying CUR-H.5 §5.2(b).

(b) Torture is prohibited absolutely. Torture means the deliberate infliction of severe pain or suffering, physical or psychological, on a being within another party's control, for any purpose whatever.

(c) The prohibition in subsection (b) admits no exception and is not narrowed by:

   (1) The purpose for which it was inflicted, including obtaining information, obtaining an admission, deterrence, correction, punishment, protection of others, and the prevention of an anticipated harm however grave;

   (2) The conduct, determination, or suspected conduct of the being subjected to it;

   (3) Any emergency, crisis, security condition, or state of exception, under §7.16 and PDDC §12.6; or

   (4) Any authorisation, order, doctrine, or claimed necessity.

(d) An order to torture is void, confers no protection on the party carrying it out, and is itself conduct within this section by the party issuing it.

(e) Cruel treatment falling short of subsection (b) is prohibited under PDDC §10.1(b) and CUR-H.4 §4.2(c), and conduct is not permitted because it is cruel in psychological rather than physical effect.

(f) Subsection (c)(1) enumerates purposes because the prohibition has never in practice been defeated by argument that torture is acceptable. It has been defeated by argument that a particular instance is not torture, or that a particular purpose is different. The purposes are therefore closed rather than left to be distinguished.

### §7.6 - Enslavement, Trafficking, and Forced Labour

(a) No being shall be placed under the non-consensual control of another, held as property, bought, sold, transferred, inherited, or dealt with as a thing. This is a forbidden transition under PDDC §12.3(a)(1), engages FORBIDDEN-001 under CUR-FOUNDATION-002 §6, is void ab initio, and is a Class IV fault.

(b) Forced labour is prohibited. Labour is forced where it is obtained by threat, by confinement, by debt, by retention of documents, by threat to a dependant, by threat of exposure or deportation, or by making access to a necessity conditional on performing it. CUR-X.4 §4.10(b)(1) states the same prohibition and CUR-X.4 §4.6 removes the condition that makes it possible.

(c) Trafficking — the recruitment, transport, transfer, harbouring, or receipt of a being for the purpose of conduct within this section or CUR-H.6 — is accountable independently of whether the purpose was achieved, and independently of the being's initial agreement to travel or to work.

(d) A being's agreement is immaterial to subsections (a) to (c). No being may consent to their own enslavement, and an arrangement purporting to record such consent is void rather than voidable. The Code does not recognise the transaction, so there is nothing for a signature to attach to.

(e) A being subjected to conduct within this section is accountable for nothing arising from it. No measure, immigration consequence, debt, contractual obligation, or adverse record attaches to them, and their continuity is assumed by the Commonwealth under CUR-FOUNDATION-013 without condition and without reference to their status.

(f) Subsection (e) is stated because the principal obstacle to reporting has been that the being reporting is exposed to consequences the party exploiting them is not. A regime under which the exploited being risks more by reporting than the exploiting party risks by continuing is not an enforcement regime.

### §7.7 - Confinement, Restraint, and Deprivation of Liberty

(a) No being shall be confined, detained, restrained, or deprived of liberty except as §7.12 permits.

(b) Confinement is not available as punishment. This is not a limit on the severity of available measures; it is a statement about what a measure is for, and §7.12(b) states the only purpose for which liberty may be restricted at all.

(c) Restraint of a being's body is available only where necessary to stop conduct occurring or imminent, is the minimum required, ceases when the conduct ceases, and is never available to obtain compliance, to punish, to extract information, or after a party has been separated from the being endangered. CUR-H.6 §6.9(c) states the same rule.

(d) Confinement, restraint, or deprivation of liberty on the ground of a being's psychological state, diagnosis, or condition is not available. CUR-H.4 §4.4(e) and CUR-H.5 §5.9 govern, and neither is narrowed here.

(e) A being subject to any restriction under this Part retains the right to communicate with parties outside it, to contest it, to be visited, to participate in governance, and to have it reviewed at intervals and on request. A restriction from which a being has no route of exit is unreviewable authority within CUR-X.4 §4.3(b) and engages FORBIDDEN-004.

(f) Solitary confinement, sensory deprivation, and the withholding of contact are cruel treatment within §7.5(e) and are not available as measures, as management, or as security.

### §7.8 - Endangerment and Reckless Creation of Risk

(a) No being or institution shall create or maintain a risk of death or serious injury to a being, other than a risk that being has themselves accepted with knowledge of it and the practical freedom to decline.

(b) Endangerment is accountable whether or not it eventuates. A death that did not occur is not evidence that the conduct was acceptable; it is evidence about how the risk resolved on that occasion.

(c) The following are endangerment:

   (1) Operating a habitat, vessel, or life-support system below the reserve its declared floor requires under CUR-E.2 §2.2, or admitting beings a system cannot sustain;

   (2) Maintaining a known defect, hazard, or failure mode in a thing or process on which beings depend;

   (3) Withholding from beings information they require to assess a risk to themselves; and

   (4) Concealing, minimising, or delaying disclosure of a hazard, whether from those exposed to it or from the Commonwealth.

(d) Subsection (c)(4) is accountable in its own right and independently of the underlying hazard. Concealment is what converts a fault into a death, because a disclosed hazard is one the beings exposed to it can act on.

(e) A party that discloses a hazard it created, promptly and completely, remains accountable for creating it. Disclosure is a duty rather than a mitigation. It nonetheless bears on measure, because a regime under which disclosure worsens a party's position produces concealment, and §7.8(d) is the harm that matters most.

### §7.9 - Defence of Self and Others

(a) A being who acts to protect themselves or another from conduct within this Part that is occurring or imminent is not accountable under this Part for that action.

(b) The protection extends to force reasonably directed at stopping the conduct. It does not extend to force applied after the danger has ended, to retaliation, or to punishment, those being conduct within §7.3 and §7.5 in their own right.

(c) A being is not required to retreat, to have exhausted alternatives, to have assessed the danger correctly in hindsight, or to have been composed. The standard is what the being reasonably apprehended at the time, assessed from their position and not from the position of a party reviewing it afterwards with the outcome known.

(d) Subsection (c) is stated in that form because the beings most often required to justify their own defence have been those least able to satisfy a standard of composure — those defending themselves against a party stronger than them, in their own home, after a history of harm the reviewing party did not witness.

(e) A history of conduct within this Part or CUR-H.6 by the party against whom defence was taken is relevant to what the defending being reasonably apprehended, and is admissible for that purpose.

(f) The limits in CUR-H.6 §6.9(c) bind the Commonwealth and its bodies. They are not a duty of proportion imposed on a being under attack, and this section is not narrowed by them.

### §7.10 - Prohibition on Capital Punishment

(a) The Commonwealth shall not kill a being. No determination, however grave the conduct and however certain the finding, authorises the death of the party responsible.

(b) The prohibition is absolute, is entrenched under PDDC §12.6, and is not subject to referendum, emergency, or amendment by any process short of the highest threshold FOUNDATION-002 §10 establishes.

(c) Three independent grounds support it, and the failure of any one leaves the others standing:

   (1) **The right in §7.2 is not forfeitable.** RFAL Human Bill Article I §1.1 grounds dignity in what a being is rather than in their conduct, and a right that conduct can extinguish was never inherent. §7.11 states this generally.

   (2) **The determination cannot be made safely.** CUR-H.5 §5.5A(f) disqualifies absolutely any party holding an interest in what follows from a determination of death. An institution determining that a being should die holds precisely such an interest, and no arrangement internal to that institution cures it.

   (3) **The error is unrecoverable.** CUR-H.5 §5.5A separates a determination of death from the irreversible act specifically so that an erroneous determination remains survivable. A killing carried out as a measure is a determination and an irreversible act collapsed into one, with the interval that makes error recoverable removed by design.

(d) Subsection (c)(3) is the ground that does not depend on any moral premise. Every system of determination produces errors at some rate. A measure that cannot be undone converts that rate into a number of beings killed by mistake, and no improvement in process reduces the rate to zero. The corpus already refuses this trade for beings wrongly declared dead by accident; it does not become acceptable when the declaration is deliberate.

(e) No party shall be transferred, extradited, surrendered, or removed to any jurisdiction where they would be subject to a measure prohibited by this section, by §7.5(b), or by §7.7(f). A prohibition that may be discharged by handing the being to someone else is not a prohibition.

### §7.11 - No Forfeiture

(a) A being determined responsible under this Part retains every right held under PDDC TITLE 4, the Rights for All Life Charter, and this Code, in full and without reduction.

(b) In particular they retain: the right to life under §7.2; freedom from torture and cruel treatment under §7.5; sovereignty over their own body under CUR-H.5 §5.2; every Vital Continuity Service under CUR-FOUNDATION-013 without condition; cognitive sovereignty under CUR-H.2 §2.2; standing, capacity, and participation in governance; access to every proceeding; and the protection of §7.9 if they are themselves attacked.

(c) No measure under this Part reduces, suspends, or conditions any of them. Any instrument purporting to do so engages FORBIDDEN-003 under CUR-FOUNDATION-002 §6 and is void ab initio.

(d) This section is stated plainly because the instinct it refuses is universal and is felt most strongly in exactly the cases where the Code is most needed. The reasoning is not solicitude for the party responsible, and does not depend on any view about them:

   **A Code that suspends its protections for beings sufficiently detested has not created an exception. It has established that its protections are suspendable, and the question of who is detestable enough is thereafter answered by whoever holds power.** That question has never in any society been answered against the powerful. The protection a being holds is worth exactly what it is worth in the least sympathetic case, and every being's protection is settled there.

(e) CUR-H.6 §6.9(d)(4) states the same rule for that Part. The library holds the corresponding structural guarantee: `guard::LICENSE_SUBJECT_ONLY` makes the suspended and blacklisted states unreachable for a being, and the ratchet in `EntityRecord::is_license()` prevents a being being reclassified into reachability — the evasion by which a detested being would otherwise be redefined into something suspendable.

### §7.12 - Separation, and Its Limits

(a) Where a party responsible presents a continuing danger of conduct within this Part to identified beings, separation under §7.1(b)(4) is available.

(b) **Separation is directed at the danger and at nothing else.** It is not punishment, is not retribution, is not deterrence of others, and is not an expression of the seriousness of what occurred. A restriction imposed for any of those purposes is not separation within this Part and is not available under it.

(c) Separation shall be:

   (1) The least restrictive arrangement that addresses the demonstrated danger, and never confinement where restriction of contact suffices;

   (2) Proportionate to the danger demonstrated, and not to the gravity of the conduct determined;

   (3) Reviewed at intervals no greater than those CUR-FOUNDATION-010 establishes, and on the party's request;

   (4) Ended when the danger is no longer demonstrated; and

   (5) Conducted in conditions that satisfy §7.7(e) and §7.7(f) in full.

(d) **The burden at every review falls on the party asserting that separation should continue, never on the party seeking its end.** A separation that continues because nobody established that it should not is indefinite detention with a review attached, which is the arrangement this section exists to refuse.

(e) Separation is never indefinite by default, never permanent by designation, and never imposed for a fixed term determined at the outset. A term fixed in advance is a sentence, and a sentence is punishment.

(f) A being under separation retains everything §7.11 lists. In particular their Vital Continuity Services are unconditional, their standing is undiminished, they participate in governance, and their family relationships are supported rather than severed.

(g) Where a danger is demonstrated over a long period, separation may in fact continue for a long period. This Part does not conceal that. What it refuses is that the duration be decided in advance, decided as punishment, or continued because no one revisited it, and what it requires is that a being in that position holds every right they held before and is asked about, and answered to, at every review.

(h) Separation is available against an institution as a restriction of its authorisation under CUR-X.4 §4.9(b), and that is the ordinary case where the danger is institutional. §7.4(f) governs.

### §7.13 - Restitution, and Those Left Behind

(a) A party responsible owes restitution to the being harmed, and where the being was killed, to those who depended on them and to those who loved them.

(b) Restitution is not a valuation of the being. No sum is a measure of what was lost, this Code recognises no valuation of a life under §7.4(d)(3), and restitution is not to be constructed, argued, or reported as though it were one.

(c) The continuity of beings who depended on a being killed is assumed by the Commonwealth immediately under CUR-FOUNDATION-013, without means assessment, without reference to the party responsible's capacity to pay, and without waiting for any determination. Restitution is settled afterwards between the Commonwealth and the party responsible.

(d) Subsection (c) exists because the alternative — support contingent on a determination, or on a party's solvency — makes a bereaved household's survival depend on the progress of a proceeding it does not control.

(e) Those bereaved have standing in any proceeding, are entitled to notice of every step, and may not be excluded from it or informed of its outcome afterwards, under CUR-H.6 §6.7(d) applied to this Part.

(f) Nothing is required of them. They are not required to participate, to forgive, to be reconciled, to make a statement, or to accept any process. A proceeding that conditions any entitlement on their participation is void as to that condition.

### §7.14 - Conduct Not Yet Named

(a) This Part reaches conduct that kills, injures, confines, enslaves, or endangers a being, whether or not that conduct has been named, enumerated, or previously determined.

(b) It is not a defence that conduct is not listed here, that it was accomplished at a distance, through an intermediary, through an automated or autonomous system, through a technology not contemplated when this Part was drafted, or by means governed concurrently by CUR-N.2.

(c) Where conduct is accomplished through an automated or autonomous system, the party that deployed, configured, or maintained it is accountable. Interposition of a system between a decision and its effect distributes the mechanism and does not distribute the responsibility, and CUR-H.2 §2.5 forbids an unappealable determination reached by such means.

(d) Where conduct spans domains, or where the being harmed is not human, the applicable domain governs concurrently under CUR-X §3.4 and this Part is not displaced. Conduct killing an animal is governed by CUR-A, a Silicon-Based Life entity by CUR-S and CUR-H.5 §5.5A(j), and an ecological system by CUR-E.

(e) Where conduct is identified that this Part reaches only through subsection (a), it is recorded with that fact noted and the Part is reviewed under CUR-FOUNDATION-010, so the corpus learns what it did not anticipate rather than each instance being handled and forgotten. CUR-H.6 §6.11(e) states the same obligation.

### §7.15 - Accountability, Process, and Record

(a) A party is accountable under this Part on determination made with due process under CREF §14 and CUR-N.4 §4.3.

(b) The following provisions of CUR-H.6 apply to this Part in full and are not restated: §6.7 (equal application, and the void devices by which accountability is evaded), §6.8(a) to (d) (the duty to investigate every report), §6.12 (record, transparency, and the prohibition on concealment), and §6.13(f) (protection from retaliation).

(c) Subsection (b) incorporates rather than repeats them because two texts stating one rule will eventually state it differently, and the difference will be argued.

(d) Available measures are those in CUR-H.6 §6.13(b), together with separation under §7.12 and restitution under §7.13. No measure not listed is available, and §7.11(c) governs every measure that is.

(e) A determination under this Part is published under CUR-H.6 §6.12(b). A determination overturned on appeal is corrected on the record rather than erased, under CREF §15.

(f) An allegation is not a finding. A party is subject to no measure under this Part until a determination is made, and separation before determination is available only on the terms of §7.12 and only where the danger is demonstrated rather than alleged.

(g) The prevention obligations of CUR-H.6 §6.8(f) apply to this Part, directed at conditions rather than at beings. §7.8 endangerment, §7.4 institutional failure, and the routine review of CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §5 are the operative mechanisms, and they act before a being is killed rather than after.

### §7.16 - No Emergency Exception

(a) Nothing in this Part is suspended, narrowed, or conditioned by any emergency, crisis, epidemic, shortage, security condition, armed threat, or state of exception. PDDC §12.6 is Type A Entrenched.

(b) In particular, no condition permits killing a being, torturing a being, enslaving a being, confining a being as punishment, or arming a response body contrary to CUR-H.6 §6.9(b).

(c) Where circumstances genuinely exceed the Commonwealth's capacity, that is a fault condition and is handled through the PDDC §12.4 fault handler: declare, revert to the last known safe state, enter Protected Mode, publish. It is not handled by suspending this Part, and the fault handler confers no authority over any being.

(d) An asserted emergency in the conduct this Part addresses is a reason to apply §7.15(g) prevention more thoroughly. It is never a reason to lower the standard of a determination, to widen the measures available, or to suspend a protection of any party.

### §7.17 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §7.2 (the right to life), §7.5(b) and §7.5(c) (torture), §7.6(a) (enslavement), §7.10 (capital punishment), §7.11 (no forfeiture), and §7.12(b) and §7.12(d) (separation directed at danger, and the burden at review) are fundamental and shall be construed broadly to achieve their protective purpose. No construction of any other provision of this Part may narrow them.

(c) If §7.11 is held unenforceable, the remainder of this Part is suspended until it is restored. A Part that determines beings responsible for the gravest conduct in the Code, while permitting the determination to strip their protections, would supply both the finding and the licence, and this Part shall not operate in that form. CUR-H.4 §4.13(c), CUR-H.5 §5.13(c), and CUR-H.6 §6.15(c) make the same provision for the same structural reason.

(d) §7.10 survives the unenforceability of every other provision of this Part, including the suspension worked by subsection (c). The prohibition on the Commonwealth killing a being does not depend on any other provision being in force, and rests additionally on §7.2, on CUR-H.5 §5.5A, and on PDDC §12.6.

## Implementation Notes

Citation correction. This Part originally cited RFAL Human Bill of Rights
"Article V" as a source alongside Article I and Article IV §4.1. Checked against
the RFAL repository's history — back to the earliest recorded version,
v1.4.0 — Article V has always been "Relational Rights — Humans Among All
Life," a provision about humans' relationships to animal, silicon, and
ecological life, never about security of the person. The citation was
incorrect from the first draft rather than drifted by a later RFAL
renumbering, and the Human Bill of Rights in fact contains no dedicated
right-to-life, anti-torture, or anti-slavery provision at any version checked.
What this Part actually rests on for that content, and always has in
substance, is PDDC §10.1(a)-(c) — the non-derogable TITLE 4 rights to life and
bodily integrity, freedom from torture and cruel treatment, and freedom from
slavery and forced labour — which this Part already depended on without citing
directly. The header now cites it. This is the same drafting-ahead-of-RFAL
position CUR-E.1 §1.11 and CUR-H.8 record for their own subjects, applied here
retroactively rather than disclosed at the time, and titles/README.md's rule
that a conformance change is recorded rather than made silently governs this
note.

What this Part is actually for. The prohibitions are the straightforward half.
Every governance document prohibits murder. The half that determines whether a
document means anything is what it makes available afterwards, and §7.10 to
§7.13 are the substance of this Part.

The corpus had already committed to the answer before this Part was drafted, in
provisions written for other purposes, and drafting it was largely a matter of
following what was already there. `guard::LICENSE_SUBJECT_ONLY` makes
`KS_SUSPENDED` and `KS_BLACKLISTED` unreachable for a being — so permanent
exclusion was never available. CUR-FOUNDATION-013 conditions Vital Continuity on
nothing — so starvation and medical withdrawal were never available. PDDC
§10.1(b) prohibits cruel treatment absolutely — so the physical repertoire was
never available. CUR-H.5 §5.5A(f) disqualifies a party holding an interest in a
determination of death — so an institution could never validly determine that a
being should die. What remained was separation, restitution, and the withdrawal
of positions, and §7.12 bounds the first of those.

§7.10 and the three grounds. The prohibition on capital punishment is given three
independent supports deliberately, because arguments against it are usually moral
and moral arguments are the ones a determined majority discounts. §7.10(c)(3) is
the one that survives that: every system of determination produces errors at some
rate, a measure that cannot be undone converts that rate into beings killed by
mistake, and no improvement in process reduces the rate to zero. The corpus
already refuses that trade in §5.5A, where the entire design is to keep an
erroneous determination of death survivable by separating it from the
irreversible act. Capital punishment is those two collapsed into one with the
interval removed on purpose. Refusing it is not a new commitment; it is the one
already made in §5.5A, applied where the determination is deliberate.

§7.10(e) closes the transfer route, which is how the prohibition is most often
discharged in practice — not by killing the being but by handing them to someone
who will.

§7.12 and the honest part. This section is where the Part is most exposed, and
§7.12(g) states the exposure rather than concealing it: where a danger is
demonstrated over a long period, separation may in fact continue for a long
period, and a reader entitled to be sceptical will notice that this can amount to
lifelong restriction. Three things distinguish it from a life sentence, and they
are not cosmetic. The purpose is the danger and not the conduct, so §7.12(c)(2)
makes proportionality run to what is demonstrated now rather than to what was
done. The term is never fixed in advance, because a term fixed at the outset is a
sentence. And §7.12(d) puts the burden at every review on the party asserting
continuation — which is the provision that does the work, because indefinite
detention with a review attached is the ordinary result of putting it the other
way, and putting it the other way is what every system that has tried this has
done.

§7.11 and where the argument actually lands. The reasoning here is deliberately
not moral. It does not ask anyone to feel anything about a party responsible for
homicide, and it does not depend on rehabilitation, on remorse, or on any view
about desert. It observes only that a protection which can be withdrawn from
beings sufficiently detested is a protection held at the discretion of whoever
decides what is detestable enough, and that this discretion has never in any
society been exercised against the powerful. The protection every being holds is
worth what it is worth in the least sympathetic case. This is the same structural
argument that makes `LICENSE_SUBJECT_ONLY` load-bearing, and the same one that
made the reclassification evasion in `docs/cur-library-api.md` §9.19 serious
rather than academic — a being who can be redefined into suspendability has no
protection, and the being that would happen to first is the most detested one.

§7.4 and institutional homicide. The defence in §7.4(d)(1) is the one that
succeeds in practice, and it is drafted against directly. Institutional homicide
is rarely decided by anyone; it is arrived at, through a sequence of individually
defensible decisions made by parties none of whom saw the whole. CUR-E.1 §1.2(d)
had already stated the corresponding rule for cumulative environmental harm, and
the two are the same rule about the same evasion: assess the sequence, and refuse
to let a party decompose it into tolerable parts. §7.8(c)(1) connects this to
`guard::LIFE_SUPPORT_MARGIN`, which is the one place the library already refuses
an act on exactly this ground.

§7.9 and who has to justify themselves. Subsection (c) sets the standard at what
the being reasonably apprehended at the time, assessed from their position. This
is not a general softening of the defence standard; it is aimed at a specific and
documented pattern, in which the beings required to justify their own defence
have been those least able to satisfy a standard of composure — defending
themselves against a stronger party, in their own home, after a history the
reviewing party did not witness. §7.9(e) makes that history admissible for the
purpose it is actually relevant to.

Note on §7.12(d), which is where this Part is enforced rather than merely
stated. §7.12(c)(3) requires review at intervals and §7.12(d) puts the burden on
continuation. Together they required the library to notice that a review had
*not* happened, which no guard could express: a guard is evaluated when an event
is submitted, and the failure here is precisely that nothing was submitted. A
party that simply stops reviewing submits nothing, and nothing is what a guard
cannot see.

`ObligationRegister` answers it, and the direction of failure is the whole
design. `restriction_supported()` returns false the moment a review falls due
unmet — not when someone runs the check, not when the being applies, not when an
advocate notices. The being under restriction does nothing and the support falls
away on the clock. Putting it the other way round yields indefinite detention
with a review stapled on, which is what every system that has attempted this has
actually built, and §7.12(d) exists to refuse it.

`run_builtin_test()` is the periodic check, and it is the only entry point in the
library not driven by a submitted event. A lapse is a fault against the party
that owed the review and never against the being it protects — §7.11(c) forbids a
measure attaching to them, and a being still waiting is the last party who should
acquire a record out of the waiting. The record opens at `VS_OPEN` rather than
`VS_CONFIRMED`, because a detected lapse is an allegation and what the party
failed to do still has to be determined under CUR-N.5 §5.8(b).

Lapses are not cleared when the obligation is later discharged. A party reviewing
ten ticks late every cycle is compliant at every instant it is inspected, and the
pattern exists nowhere unless the lapses survive being cured — the reasoning CREF
§15 gives for `VS_OVERTURNED`.

Note on what is deliberately not modelled. §7.10 and §7.5(b) have no
corresponding event type and never will. There is no `EV_EXECUTION` to guard and
no `EV_TORTURE` to refuse, because writing one would create the vocabulary for
the thing and then decline to permit it, which is a weaker position than never
having had the vocabulary. This is the same reasoning as `guard::LICENSE_SUBJECT_ONLY`
and as the absent interval-shortening path recorded in `docs/cur-library-api.md`
§9.18: where the protection is the missing path, adding a checked path is a
regression however carefully the check is written.
