Numbering note. CUR-H Part numbers are not aligned to RFAL article numbers. That
alignment was broken deliberately at CUR-H.4 and the reasons are recorded there.
This Part draws on several RFAL articles and belongs to none of them alone.

Relationship to CUR-H.5. That Part states sovereignty over the body in general
terms. This Part governs the specific conduct by which that sovereignty is most
often overridden, and the specific mechanisms by which the overriding has
historically been excused, concealed, or exempted. Nothing here narrows CUR-H.5,
and where both apply, both apply.

Scope note on generality. This Part is written in terms of beings rather than of
any group, because the protections belong to everyone and a right stated for a
class is a right that can be argued away for anyone outside it. The Part is
nonetheless drafted with the pattern of the actual harm in view: the overwhelming
majority of this conduct is directed by men at women and children, the mechanisms
of exemption in §6.7 were built to protect wealthy men, and §6.5 exists because
the marriage of children to adults is defended somewhere in every generation.
Naming that is not a departure from generality. A provision drafted without
knowing what it is for tends to be drafted badly.

## PART 6 - PROTECTION FROM SEXUAL VIOLENCE, COERCION, AND EXPLOITATION

### §6.1 - Scope and Definitions

(a) This Part governs sexual violence, sexual coercion, sexual harassment, and the sexual exploitation of beings who cannot consent; the accountability that follows; and the mechanisms by which accountability has historically been evaded.

(b) For the purposes of this Part:

   (1) Sexual Contact means any contact of a sexual character with a being's body, and any act performed on, with, or in the presence of a being for the sexual purpose of another.

   (2) Sexual Violence means sexual contact without consent satisfying §6.2(b). It is a bodily intervention without consent within CUR-H.5 §5.2(f) and is governed by both provisions.

   (3) Persistence means the repetition, renewal, or continuation of a sexual approach after a refusal, and includes approaches renewed through third parties, through institutional position, through altered circumstances, or after an interval.

   (4) Child means a being below the age of majority established by the Commonwealth, and the term does not vary by jurisdiction, community, custom, rite, or the being's physical development.

   (5) Position of Authority means any relationship in which one being holds power over another's employment, housing, education, care, standing, liberty, immigration status, access to a Vital Continuity Service, or custody of a dependant.

(c) This Part reaches every being without qualification, and reaches conduct wherever it occurs within Commonwealth jurisdiction, including within a household, a marriage, a religious community, an enterprise, a care setting, a custodial setting, and any space described as private.

(d) The privacy of a space is not a limit on this Part. A space in which a being may be harmed without recourse is not private; it is unreviewable, and unreviewable authority over a being engages FORBIDDEN-004 under CUR-FOUNDATION-002 §6 wherever it is exercised.

### §6.2 - Sexual Autonomy and the Consent Standard

(a) Every being holds sovereign authority over their own body under CUR-H.5 §5.2, and no exception to that sovereignty exists for sexual contact.

(b) Consent to sexual contact means consent satisfying the standard of CUR-H.2 §2.1(c) and CUR-H.5 §5.2(b): explicit, informed, freely given, ongoing, and revocable. Every element is required and the absence of any one of them means there was no consent.

(c) Consent is specific to the contact consented to, to the occasion, and to the party. It does not extend to a different act, to a later occasion, or to any other party.

(d) Consent is revocable at any moment, including after contact has begun, and revocation takes effect immediately. Contact continued after revocation is sexual violence from the moment of revocation, and the earlier consent does not extend to it.

(e) Consent is a state of a being, not a state of a record. It is not established by a signature, a contract, a ceremony, a status, a prior course of conduct, or another party's belief about it.

(f) The burden of establishing consent falls on the party asserting it. A being is not required to prove they refused.

### §6.3 - What Is Not Consent

(a) None of the following is consent, and none of them supplies, substitutes for, or evidences consent:

   (1) The absence of refusal, silence, stillness, or the failure to resist. A being who does not fight is not a being who agreed, and freezing is a documented response to threat rather than an expression of will;

   (2) Consent given on a previous occasion, however recent;

   (3) Marriage, partnership, cohabitation, betrothal, or any relationship. Marriage is not a standing consent and confers no entitlement to a being's body. Sexual violence within a marriage is sexual violence, and the marriage is not a defence, a mitigation, or a matter to be resolved privately;

   (4) The consent of a parent, guardian, family member, community, elder, employer, or authority. No party may consent on behalf of another to sexual contact, and no party holds such authority to give — §6.5(c) states this for children, and it holds for every being;

   (5) Consent given while a being is unconscious, asleep, or incapacitated by intoxication, medication, injury, or condition to the point of being unable to form or express a decision;

   (6) Consent obtained by deception as to the nature of the act, the identity of the party, or a fact material to the being's decision;

   (7) Consent obtained by threat, whether of force, exposure, expulsion, deportation, loss of employment, loss of housing, loss of standing, loss of custody, or consequence beyond death within CUR-D.6 §6.3(b)(1);

   (8) Consent obtained by a party in a position of authority within §6.1(b)(5), where the being's compliance is conditioned on anything that party controls. CUR-H.5 §5.9(c) and CUR-N.2 §2.1(c) state the same rule: consent given under conditions this Code exists to remove is not consent; or

   (9) Consent inferred from a being's clothing, occupation, past conduct, sexual history, intoxication, presence in a place, acceptance of hospitality, or any other circumstance offered as though it were an expression of will.

(b) A being's sexual history is not relevant to whether they consented on a later occasion, is not admissible for that purpose, and shall not be recorded, sought, or referred to in any proceeding under this Part except where the being themselves raises it.

(c) Subsection (b) is stated as a rule of the proceeding rather than as guidance. The practice it forecloses has functioned as a deterrent to reporting more effectively than any provision of law has functioned as an encouragement.

### §6.4 - Refusal, and the Prohibition on Persistence

(a) A refusal is complete when it is given. It requires no reason, no repetition, no consistency with the being's earlier conduct, and no particular form of words.

(b) **A refusal governs until the being who gave it revisits it.** No other party may treat it as provisional, as an opening position, as a test of sincerity, or as a state to be worn down.

(c) Persistence within §6.1(b)(3) is prohibited. It is a harm in itself, independent of whether the party persisting ever obtains what they sought, and it is psychological harm within CUR-H.4 §4.3(a).

(d) The following aggravate a determination under subsection (c) rather than excusing it: that the persistence was polite; that it was framed as courtship, devotion, or persistence in the face of adversity; that the party believed the refusal insincere; that the party believed the being would change their mind; and that no threat was made.

(e) Persistence graded as a coercive practice under CUR-N.5 §5.2A reaches CP-2 at the point that section identifies: an approach directed at an identified being combined with a consequence that being cannot decline without loss. The consequence need not be threatened in words. A being who must change their route, their workplace, their social circle, or their home to end an approach has already borne it.

(f) Escalating persistence following refusal is a recognised precursor to sexual violence and to homicide. It is accordingly a ground for protective action under §6.9 at the point it is identified, and not only at the point it becomes violent. §6.8 states the corresponding duty to act on a report rather than to await an escalation.

(g) Nothing in this section reaches an honest single approach, the ordinary formation of relationships, or a being's own decision to revisit a refusal they gave. The conduct this section names is the refusal to accept an answer, not the asking of a question.

### §6.5 - Children

(a) A child cannot consent to sexual contact with an adult. This is not a presumption, a factor, or a matter for assessment in the individual case. There is no evidence that could establish such consent, because the capacity the consent standard requires does not exist to be exercised.

(b) Sexual contact between an adult and a child is sexual violence. It is a Class IV fault, is void ab initio, and admits no defence of belief as to age where reasonable steps to establish age were not taken, no defence of the child's apparent maturity, willingness, initiative, or physical development, and no defence founded on the practice of any community.

(c) **No parent, guardian, family member, community, court, authority, or rite may supply a child's consent, because none of them holds it to give.** A child's body is not within any other party's authority to dispose of. CUR-H.5 §5.2(g) states the general rule; this subsection removes any doubt about its application here.

(d) Marriage of a child is prohibited. No marriage, betrothal, union, or equivalent arrangement involving a being below the age of majority is valid, recognised, registered, or given any effect within Commonwealth jurisdiction, and there is no exception for:

   (1) The consent of a parent, guardian, or family;

   (2) Religious rite, doctrine, custom, tradition, or community practice;

   (3) The pregnancy of the child, which is evidence of an offence under subsection (b) rather than a reason to formalise one;

   (4) An order, dispensation, or authorisation of any body; or

   (5) A marriage contracted in another jurisdiction, which is void here whatever its status there.

(e) Subsection (d) is stated with its exceptions enumerated and closed because each of them has been used. A marriage certificate is a label, and a label does not transform the act it is placed on. CUR-X.4 §4.2(a) states the governing principle — a thing is what it is in fact, regardless of how it is constituted or described — and CUR-N.5 §5.2A grades practices rather than the names given to them. **The marriage of a child to an adult is the sexual exploitation of a child with paperwork attached, and this Part treats it as what it is.**

(f) A party who arranges, facilitates, solemnises, registers, transports for, or profits from an arrangement within subsection (d) is accountable under §6.13 independently of the party who contracted it, and independently of whether contact occurred.

(g) No child is accountable under this Part for conduct arising from their own exploitation, and no measure of any kind attaches to a child on that ground. A child removed from an arrangement within subsection (d) retains every entitlement in full, and the Commonwealth assumes their continuity under CUR-FOUNDATION-013 without condition, without means assessment, and without reference to the family that arranged it.

(h) The age of majority is a single Commonwealth-wide standard. It does not vary by community, by the being's sex or gender, by the marital status of any party, or by any accommodation extended to a tradition. A rule permitting one class of children to be married at an age at which another class may not is a species privilege within FORBIDDEN-005 applied within a species, and is void.

### §6.6 - Doctrine Supplies No Authority Over a Body

(a) No doctrine, teaching, scripture, revelation, tradition, or claimed divine instruction supplies any authority over another being's body, and none is a defence to conduct under this Part. CUR-H.5 §5.6(a) states this generally and it is absolute here.

(b) A doctrine holding that a class of beings is inferior, unclean, culpable, owed as property, or obliged to provide sexual or reproductive service to another supplies no entitlement, no mitigation, and no defence. A being holding such a belief may hold it; CUR-H.2 §2.2 protects the interior absolutely, and this Part reaches no belief. What it reaches is the act, and the act is not licensed by the belief behind it.

(c) The distinction in subsection (b) is the line CUR-D.6 §6.3(d) draws between belief and authority, and it is the whole of this section. Teaching a doctrine is protected. Enforcing it upon a being who has not accepted it is not, and enforcing it upon a being who has accepted it is not either, because a being's beliefs do not transfer their body into another's authority.

(d) No community, congregation, family, or association may operate an internal process for handling conduct within this Part in place of the Commonwealth's. A community may hold whatever internal process it wishes in addition. It may not require a being to use it, condition their standing on using it, discourage or penalise a report, or treat the matter as concluded by its own outcome.

(e) Subsection (d) closes the mechanism by which this conduct has most reliably escaped accountability: not denial, but internal resolution, in which the party harmed is counselled toward forgiveness, the party responsible is relocated, and the record is kept by the institution that produced the harm.

(f) A being who reports conduct under this Part is protected from expulsion, shunning, loss of community, loss of family relationship, and loss of standing under CUR-H.2 §2.3(c) and §2.3(d)(3). Expulsion imposed as a response to a report is retaliation within §6.13(f) and is a violation in its own right.

(g) Nothing in this section is a finding about any religion, and no measure under this Part attaches to a being by reason of their faith, their community, or their attendance. CUR-D.6 §6.8(d) draws the distinction this section relies on: conduct and authorisation are reachable, beings and their beliefs are not.

### §6.7 - Equal Application

(a) The wealth, position, office, reputation, institutional value, professional standing, public usefulness, family, connections, or contribution of a party is irrelevant to whether a determination is made under this Part, to what measure follows, and to whether either is published.

(b) The following are void ab initio:

   (1) Any agreement not to report, not to investigate, not to prosecute, or not to determine;

   (2) Any agreement conditioning a payment, settlement, or benefit on a being's silence, non-participation, withdrawal, or non-cooperation;

   (3) Any non-disclosure obligation purporting to bind a being from describing conduct done to them, whatever it is called and whenever it was entered into;

   (4) Any sealing, restriction, or suppression of a determination or its record on the ground of a party's status, reputation, business interest, or the consequences of publication to them; and

   (5) Any disposal of a matter that resolves it without a determination, where the being harmed did not seek that outcome.

(c) Subsection (b)(3) operates retrospectively. An obligation of silence already entered into is unenforceable from the commencement of this Part, and a being bound by one is free of it without applying to anyone.

(d) A being harmed under this Part has standing in any proceeding concerning it, is entitled to notice of every step, and may not be excluded from it, settled around, or informed of its outcome afterwards. A proceeding conducted without the being's knowledge is not a proceeding under this Part.

(e) No party holds immunity, exemption, or preferential process under this Part by reason of office, employment, diplomatic status, religious position, or membership of any body. A party's position is relevant to a determination only where it is the position of authority that made the conduct possible, in which case §6.3(a)(8) applies and it aggravates.

(f) Where a party responsible for conduct under this Part holds a position of authority within §6.1(b)(5), the position is withdrawn as a measure under §6.13(b). The withdrawal is not a punishment of the being and is not treated as one; it removes the authority the conduct was committed through, and CUR-X.4 §4.9(c) governs so that no other entitlement of theirs is reduced by it.

(g) This section exists because the pattern it addresses is not occasional. Systems for determining this conduct have reliably produced one outcome for parties with resources and another for parties without, and have done so through the specific devices enumerated in subsection (b) rather than through open exemption. Closing them individually is the only form of closure that has ever worked.

### §6.8 - Duty to Investigate, and Prevention

(a) Every report of conduct within this Part is investigated. There is no threshold of seriousness, credibility, corroboration, or prospect of determination below which a report is not investigated, and no discretion to decline.

(b) A being making a report is not required to be certain, consistent, composed, immediate, or willing to proceed. Delay in reporting is not evidence that conduct did not occur, and is a documented consequence of the conduct rather than a fact about its truth.

(c) A being who reports may decline to participate further at any point without the investigation ceasing, and without any consequence to them. The Commonwealth's obligation to investigate is owed to the Commonwealth as well as to the being, because a party who has done this once is a hazard to beings who have not yet met them.

(d) Investigation is conducted so as not to inflict the harm it examines. A being is not required to repeat their account more times than the investigation requires, to be examined by a party they object to, or to encounter the party responsible.

(e) Routine, random, and triggered review under CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §5 applies to every setting in which beings are subject to a position of authority within §6.1(b)(5), independently of any complaint, on the reasoning of CUR-N.5 §5.2B(f). A regime that operates only on complaint detects only what beings in the weakest position are able to report, which is a sample selected by exactly the wrong variable.

(f) Prevention is a governance obligation, not an aspiration, and is discharged by measures directed at conditions rather than at beings:

   (1) Universal, accurate education on the consent standard in §6.2(b), the meaning of refusal under §6.4, and what this Part prohibits, provided to everyone and not conditioned on any community's approval;

   (2) Removal of the dependencies that make refusal costly, including access to housing, income-independent access to necessities under CUR-X.4 §4.6, and the right to leave any community under CUR-H.2 §2.3(c);

   (3) Design of shared spaces, housing, transport, and habitat systems so that beings are not required to be alone with parties holding authority over them;

   (4) Acting on §6.4(f) precursor patterns at the point they are identified; and

   (5) Voluntary support, freely available and freely declinable, to any being who seeks help with their own conduct before there is anything to determine.

(g) Subsection (f)(5) is stated because it is the measure most often omitted and the only one that operates before a being is harmed. It confers no authority over anyone under §6.10, and a being who seeks it acquires no record, no status, and no adverse consequence by doing so.

### §6.9 - Response, Protective Action, and the Limits on Force

(a) Response to conduct under this Part is directed at stopping the conduct and protecting the being. It is not directed at punishing the party responsible, and the distinction determines what is available.

(b) **The Commonwealth maintains no militarised response capability.** No body responding to conduct under this Part, or to any ordinary matter of public safety, shall be equipped with firearms, military weapons, military vehicles, or military equipment, and no such body shall be trained, organised, or deployed as a military formation.

(c) Force used in protective action shall be the minimum required to stop conduct that is occurring or imminent, shall cease when the conduct stops, and is available only for that purpose. Force is not available to punish, to obtain compliance with an instruction, to extract information, to secure an admission, or after a party has ceased and been separated from the being.

(d) The following are prohibited absolutely and are not available at any point, to any body, on any ground:

   (1) Corporal punishment, whether imposed by a body, a court, a community, or a being acting in place of one;

   (2) Any treatment cruel in its physical or psychological effect, under PDDC §10.1(b) and CUR-H.4 §4.2(c);

   (3) Any measure directed at a party's body rather than at their conduct, their authority, or their authorisation; and

   (4) Any measure that treats a party responsible for conduct under this Part as having forfeited the protections of this Code.

(e) Subsection (d)(4) is the difficult one and it is stated deliberately. A being who has committed sexual violence remains a being. They may be stopped, determined against, deprived of every position through which the conduct was possible, required to make restitution, and separated from those they endanger. They may not be beaten, degraded, tortured, or disposed of, and the reason is not solicitude for them. **A Code that suspends its protections for beings sufficiently detested has not made an exception; it has established that its protections are suspendable, and every other being now holds them at the discretion of whoever decides what is detestable enough.** CUR-H.4 §4.4 and CUR-H.5 §5.9 state the same structure, and PDDC §12.6 makes it entrenched.

(f) Separation of a party from beings they endanger is available, is directed at the danger rather than at the party, is reviewable at intervals and on request, and is bounded by CUR-N.4 §4.7. It is not confinement as punishment, does not reduce the party's standing under PDDC TITLE 4, and does not condition any Vital Continuity Service under CUR-FOUNDATION-013.

(g) A being defending themselves or another from conduct occurring or imminent is not accountable under subsection (c). The limits in this section bind the Commonwealth and its bodies; they are not a duty of composure imposed on a being under attack.

(h) Prevention of the harm is the objective of this Part and force is an admission that prevention failed. §6.8(f) is accordingly the operative section and this one is the residue.

### §6.10 - Psychological Condition Is Neither Defence Nor Ground

(a) A psychological condition, diagnosis, disorder, compulsion, upbringing, indoctrination, or history of harm suffered by a party is not a defence to conduct under this Part and does not reduce a determination.

(b) Nor is any of them a ground for a measure against that party beyond those §6.13 makes available. A finding that a party is unwell, disordered, at risk, or shaped by a doctrine creates no authority to treat, detain, confine, medicate, or modify them. CUR-H.4 §4.4 admits no exception and none is created here.

(c) Subsections (a) and (b) are two halves of one rule and neither works alone. Where a condition excuses conduct, beings are harmed and nothing follows. Where a condition licenses detention, the category expands until it holds whoever is inconvenient, and it has never in any society expanded toward the powerful.

(d) Conduct is addressed as conduct. Care is offered, is voluntary under CUR-H.4 §4.4(c), is available under §6.8(f)(5) before there is anything to determine, and is neither a condition of nor a substitute for accountability.

(e) Where conduct under this Part is shaped by a doctrine, the doctrine is addressed under §6.6 and CUR-D.6 as a practice of the institution teaching it. It is addressed there because that is where it is tractable. Treating it as an illness of the individual locates the problem in the being least able to change it and leaves the institution untouched.

### §6.11 - Conduct Not Yet Named

(a) This Part reaches conduct by which a being's sovereignty over their own body is overridden, whether or not that conduct has been named, enumerated, or previously determined.

(b) It is not a defence that conduct is not listed in this Part, that it was accomplished by a means this Part does not describe, that it was accomplished at a distance, through an intermediary, through an automated system, through a technology not contemplated when this Part was drafted, or through telepathic or psychic means governed concurrently by CUR-N.2.

(c) The test is the one §6.2(b) states: was there consent satisfying the standard, given by the being whose body it was. Where there was not, this Part applies.

(d) Enumeration in this Part is illustrative of the harm and never exhaustive of it. A list of prohibited acts is a list of the acts someone thought of, and conduct designed to fall outside a list is the ordinary result of publishing one.

(e) Where conduct is identified that this Part reaches only through subsection (a), it is recorded under §6.12 with that fact noted, and the Part is reviewed under CUR-FOUNDATION-010. The purpose of the record is that the corpus learns what it did not anticipate, rather than each instance being handled and forgotten.

### §6.12 - Record, Transparency, and the Prohibition on Concealment

(a) Every report, investigation, determination, and measure under this Part is recorded, and the record is not destroyed, sealed, or restricted by reason of any party's status.

(b) Determinations are published at transparency level T0 under CUR-FOUNDATION-010 §8. The identity of a being harmed is published only with their consent, freely given and revocable, and its absence never suppresses the determination itself.

(c) A being harmed has access to every record concerning them, without charge, without justification, and without the mediation of any party whose conduct is recorded in it.

(d) Patterns are visible by construction. Records are held so that repeated conduct by one party, repeated conduct within one setting, and repeated failure of one process are apparent without a complaint drawing attention to them, on the reasoning of CUR-N.5 §5.2B.

(e) A determination overturned on appeal is corrected on the record rather than erased, under CREF §15. A determination that was wrong is a fact about the process and the process cannot be improved from a record that removes its errors.

(f) An institution unable to produce a record required by this section is treated as not having conducted the process the record would evidence. The burden of record-keeping falls on the party with the power to keep one.

### §6.13 - Accountability and Measures

(a) A party is accountable under this Part on determination made with due process under CREF §14 and CUR-N.4 §4.3. §6.7(a) governs the application of that process.

(b) Available measures include publication of findings; withdrawal of any position of authority within §6.1(b)(5); prohibition on holding such a position; restitution to the being harmed; mandatory audit of an institution under CUR-FOUNDATION-010; restructuring of an enterprise under CUR-X.4 §4.9(b); withdrawal of an authorisation; and separation under §6.9(f).

(c) Every measure attaches to a party's conduct, position, or authorisation. No measure under this Part may reduce a party's standing under PDDC TITLE 4, condition any Vital Continuity Service, attach to a being by association with a party, or attach to a being harmed in any form.

(d) Restitution is owed to the being harmed and is not discharged by payment to any institution, fund, or family. It is not conditioned on the being's cooperation, forgiveness, silence, or continued relationship with anyone.

(e) An institution that concealed, mishandled, discouraged, or internally resolved a matter within this Part is accountable in its own right, independently of the party who committed the conduct, under CUR-X.4 §4.3(b) where the arrangement was unreviewable.

(f) Retaliation against a being for reporting, participating in, or declining to participate in a proceeding under this Part is a violation in its own right, and is protected against under CUR-H.2 §2.3(f) and CUR-H.4 §4.3(a)(9). Expulsion from a community under §6.6(f) is retaliation within this subsection.

(g) A being harmed is not required to have objected at the time, to have resisted, to have reported promptly, to have exhausted an internal process, or to demonstrate lasting injury.

### §6.14 - No Emergency Exception

(a) Nothing in this Part is suspended, narrowed, or conditioned by any emergency, crisis, security condition, shortage, or state of exception. PDDC §12.6 is Type A Entrenched.

(b) No condition justifies suspending the consent standard in §6.2(b), the prohibitions in §6.5, the equal application required by §6.7, or the limits on force in §6.9.

(c) An asserted emergency in the conduct this Part addresses is a reason to apply §6.8(f) prevention more thoroughly, and is never a reason to arm a response body, to lower the standard of a determination, or to suspend a protection of any party.

(d) Subsection (c) is stated because urgency about this conduct is the most common argument offered for militarised response and for summary process, and both have historically been directed at populations rather than at the parties responsible. CUR-E.1 §1.7(b)(5) states the same limit where the objective is ecological, and CUR-N.5 §5.2A grades practices rather than populations.

### §6.15 - Severability

(a) If any provision of this Part is held unenforceable or invalid, the remaining provisions remain in full force.

(b) §6.2 (the consent standard), §6.3 (what is not consent), §6.5 (children), §6.7(b) (the void devices), §6.9(d) (the absolute prohibitions on force), and §6.10 (condition neither defence nor ground) are fundamental and shall be construed broadly to achieve their protective purpose. No construction of any other provision of this Part may narrow them.

(c) If §6.9(d) is held unenforceable, the remainder of this Part is suspended until it is restored. A Part that names conduct deserving of accountability while permitting the response to be unbounded is a Part that has supplied both the target and the licence, and this Part shall not operate in that form. CUR-H.4 §4.13(c) and CUR-H.5 §5.13(c) make the same provision for the same structural reason.

(d) §6.5 survives the unenforceability of every other provision of this Part, including the suspension worked by subsection (c). The protection of children from sexual exploitation and from marriage does not depend on any other provision being in force.

## Implementation Notes

Why this Part is drafted generally and researched specifically. Every protection
here belongs to every being, and that is not diplomatic phrasing — a right stated
for a class is a right that can be argued away for anyone the class is later held
not to include, and the classes most often excluded have been exactly the ones
most often harmed. But the mechanisms in §6.7(b) were not invented in the
abstract. Non-prosecution agreements, sealed determinations, non-disclosure
obligations covering conduct rather than commercial information, and settlements
conditioned on non-participation are the specific devices by which wealthy
parties have escaped accountability for this conduct, repeatedly and recently.
They are enumerated and voided individually because closing them individually is
the only closure that has held.

§6.5 and the label problem. The marriage of a child to an adult is defended in
every generation, and it is defended by the same move each time: the act is not
disputed, the description is changed, and a certificate is produced. The corpus
already has the answer to that move and did not need a new one. CUR-X.4 §4.2(a)
holds that an enterprise is a governance structure in fact "regardless of how it
is constituted or described"; CUR-N.5 §5.2A grades practices rather than the
names given to them; and the library learned the same lesson about itself when a
being reclassified as an operational licence became suspendable — see
`docs/cur-library-api.md` §9.19. §6.5(e) applies the principle here and says the
plain thing: it is the sexual exploitation of a child with paperwork attached.

§6.5(c) is the load-bearing subsection, and it is drafted as a statement about
authority rather than about consent. The argument for child marriage is almost
never that the child agreed; it is that the parent agreed on the child's behalf.
So the provision does not weigh that consent, it denies the authority: no party
holds a child's consent to give. This is the operative line the corpus applies
throughout — a mechanism granting authority over another being is fixed, a
mechanism granting none is left alone.

§6.6 and the choice not to characterise believers. This Part reaches doctrine as
enforcement and nowhere reaches belief, and that is a deliberate drafting
decision rather than a softening. Three reasons. It is what CUR-H.2 §2.2 already
requires, the interior being absolutely protected. It is what CUR-D.6 §6.3(d)
already establishes as the line between belief and authority. And it is the
version that works: a provision reaching beings by their faith would be resisted
by everyone who holds it including those who would never act on it, while a
provision reaching the specific practice — the internal process, the counselled
forgiveness, the relocated offender, the community-kept record — reaches the
mechanism that actually produces the harm and is defensible to the members of
that community who are themselves harmed by it. §6.6(d) and §6.6(e) are the
operative provisions of that section, and neither mentions a doctrine's content.

§6.9(d)(4) and why it is stated. The instinct that a party who has done this
forfeits protection is understandable and this Part refuses it, not out of
sympathy but out of structure. A Code that suspends its protections for beings
sufficiently detested has not created an exception; it has established that the
protections are suspendable, and the question of who is detestable enough is then
answered by whoever holds power. That question has never in any society been
answered against the powerful. This is the same reasoning that makes
`guard::LICENSE_SUBJECT_ONLY` load-bearing and PDDC §12.6 entrenched, and §6.15(c)
suspends the Part if §6.9(d) falls.

The corporal punishment question. An unarmed response capability is a substantive
commitment and this Part makes it — §6.9(b) is unqualified. What §6.9(d)(1) does
not do is permit unofficial physical punishment in place of official force. That
is not a lesser evil than a militarised police force; it is the same authority
with the accountability removed, and the beings it has historically fallen on are
not the ones with resources. The strength this Part offers instead is in §6.7:
the determination actually happens, it attaches to the party regardless of who
they are, it cannot be purchased, sealed, or settled away, and the position
through which the conduct was possible is withdrawn. A system in which that
reliably occurs is a greater deterrent than one in which it occasionally does not
and someone is beaten instead.

§6.10 and the trap in the mental-health framing. Attributing this conduct to
psychological injury caused by doctrine is often accurate and it carries a
hazard in both directions, which is why §6.10 closes both. If a condition
excuses, beings are harmed and nothing follows. If a condition licenses
detention, the category expands, and it expands toward the powerless every time —
this is the mechanism CUR-H.4 §4.4 was written against and it is not weakened
here. §6.10(e) places the doctrine where it is actually tractable: as a practice
of the institution teaching it, under §6.6 and CUR-D.6, rather than as an illness
of the individual, which locates the problem in the being least able to change it
and leaves the institution untouched.

Note on the two recorded gaps. Both are real and both are smaller than they look.
`EntityRecord` carries no age, so §6.5 cannot be checked by the library at all —
an event involving a child is indistinguishable from any other. Adding a
minority flag rather than an age would be enough for the guard §6.5(b) implies,
carries less personal data, and cannot be used to construct the age-graded
distinctions §6.5(h) forbids. §6.8(a) needs an `EV_REPORT_RECEIVED` and a check
that every such event is followed by an investigation event, which is the first
provision in the corpus requiring the library to notice something that did *not*
happen. Neither is built here: both touch the published interface, and three
interface changes have landed in as many days.

Note on what §6.9(b) cannot be. An unarmed Commonwealth is a decision about what
is procured, trained, and deployed, and there is no code path whose absence
expresses it — unlike §5.5A(e), where the absence of a shortening path *is* the
protection, because there the thing being prevented was a transition the machine
evaluates. Here the thing being prevented is a purchase order. §6.9(b) is
enforceable through CUR-FOUNDATION-010 audit of what bodies hold, and it is
recorded as not modelled rather than as a gap, because a library that reported
compliance here would be reporting on evidence it does not have.
